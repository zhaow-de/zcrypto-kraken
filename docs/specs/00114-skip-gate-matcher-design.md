# Skip gates match an enumerated form, and the reachability half of the opt-in rule is held

## Problem

`CLAUDE.md` carries the venue opt-in rule as two entries. The first — one flag opens every test that reaches a venue — is held by `tests/test_live_venue_opt_in.py` through `infra/scripts/count-list.sh skip-gate-contract`. The second — no skip is decided by whether a venue answers — is held by nothing, and the entry says so: four review rounds on [[T0190]] tried to assert it and the owner ruled on 2026-09-11 that it is its own piece of work.

The guard that holds the first half is a REDUCER: it walks each guard expression asking what it reads, and at every node it cannot classify it chooses between refusing and permitting. Its own docstring lists what that shape lets through, each measured by planting it: a second opt-in name reaching a gate through five bindings the scope merge does not see, `unittest`'s decorator and method forms yielding no gate, an environment read hidden inside a skip helper, and six mutation probes reverting its most recent recognition additions that all SURVIVED. A reducer's closed world is a property of every branch, and every widening added branches.

A MATCHER has no branch that can leak: a guard either matches one of an enumerated set of forms, each of which names what it reads, or it is refused. The cost that stopped the matcher on T0190's branch was measured then as 18 of 61 guard expressions with no form readable off their shape. Re-measured for this spec on 2026-09-17: 22 of 66, and they are three kinds, not eighteen problems — ten calls to one helper, seven `shutil.which` results read a line later, and five bare locals over a local scan. Each takes one declaration. Ten more are refused for what their receiver is BOUND to rather than for its shape (D2), and they are three kinds too: four import the root, five take it from the local config, and one tests a parametrize value for membership.

## Decisions

**D1. The tree assertion becomes "every guard of every skip gate matches one form", and a guard that matches none is refused with the remedy.** The two assertions the reducer holds — the one name, and the refusal of what it cannot read — are both consequences of this one: the opt-in form admits only the one key, and an unmatched guard is the refusal. What the assertion adds is the half nothing held: with every guard matched to a form whose reading is named, the set of things a skip can be decided by is closed, and the venue is not in it.

**D2. The forms are the shapes the tree carries, and the only thing followed is a receiver's root.** The census below groups the 66 guards into fourteen blanked shapes. The manifest ones — a path-presence method (`exists`, `is_file`, `is_dir`) on a literal-rooted pathlib expression, `os.geteuid() == 0`, `shutil.which(<literal>) is None`, an environment read of a literal or plain-module-constant key, compared to a literal, spelled `<key> in os.environ`, or tested against a plain-module-constant collection, and membership of a call-free expression in a plain-module-constant collection — name their reading in the form itself. Three of them name what is read — `os.geteuid`, `shutil.which`, and the environment mapping — so the form fixes the reading and only the key can vary, which the clause below pins. The reading is fixed only where the whole CALL is: a call that reaches a manifest form is pinned to its callee's exact positional count, with no keyword and no starred or double-starred argument, because an argument nothing judges is an expression a call may sit in — `shutil.which(<literal>, path=<expr>)` reads whatever `path` names, and `os.geteuid(*<expr>)` returns normally rather than raising. An environment read with a DEFAULT is refused rather than matched for the same reason, and the registry call of D3 is the one call whose arguments stay free, its argument being a claim rather than a reading. The other two name neither: `exists` names a method and `in` names an operator, and any expression at all may sit under either, so `not fetch_ohlc(PAIR).exists()` and `kraken.system_status() in _DOWN` are venue reads wearing a form's clothes. Both operands are therefore constrained rather than free: a path receiver, and a membership test's left operand that is not itself an environment read, must each be LITERAL-ROOTED — every chain written in it, and every call written inside it, rooted at a string literal, at `__file__`, at a name this module imported from `pathlib`, or at a name bound exactly ONCE — at the module's top level, or inside the gate's own function — to another such expression. This is the discipline `_plain_literal` already gives the opt-in key, applied to a receiver: a receiver written at the gate is judged where it stands, and a receiver that is a name is judged at its binding, so `marker = _venue_reads()` one line above the gate is refused there rather than reading as the local it is written as, and `probe.status` on a `@property` that reads remotely is refused at `probe`. A parameter, an imported name, a name no assignment in the module binds, a name bound from any other call — a helper, a `resolve` of a computed value, an environment read — and a name bound more than once are each refused, and the refusal names the binding it saw. The remedy is never to hoist, because the line above is read too: it is to write the path as a literal-rooted expression at the module top or at the gate, or to declare what the guard reads through `tests/skip_gates.py`. What the rule roots is the chain, not what sits beside it — the right side of a `/` join, an argument of a `pathlib` method — and D5 names that residual rather than claiming it closed. Over the 66 the tree carries, this keeps 23 of its 32 path gates, refuses the other nine, and refuses its one membership gate; those ten migrate in D8's order beside the 22. The `uid` form is matched on `<os>.geteuid`, under any name `os` is bound to; the `binary` form is matched on the literal `shutil.which`, and an alias spelling of `shutil` is refused with a remedy that prints the accepted one. The opt-in key is resolved in exactly one way, a string literal or a plain top-level `NAME = "<literal>"` assignment; an annotated constant, a binding inside a module-level `if` or `try`, an attribute, a subscript, a name that arrived by star-import and a call are all refused, which is how the five bindings the reducer let through become refusals rather than recognitions. What is followed is a binding, never a reading: the root of a receiver is resolved to the expression bound to it and that expression is judged by the same grammar, and no binding is ever followed to find a manifest form in it. The alternative — following a local binding one step to the manifest form it holds — is the reducer's design and its leak, and is not taken.

**D3. A guard whose reading is not on its face declares it by calling a function of `tests/skip_gates.py`, and a call into that module is the sixth form.** Three functions cover 27 of the 32 — the 22 whose shape names no form, and five of the ten whose receiver the local config roots: `develop_resolves()` (the `git rev-parse` the count-list tests gate on, moved from `tests/test_count_list.py`), `no_binary(name)` (`shutil.which(name) is None`, so the seven `bash is None` gates say what they read at the gate), and `nothing_found(rows)` (`not rows`, the declaration that a local collection came from a scan of local data and is empty). The matcher resolves the callee syntactically: a name imported from `tests.skip_gates`, or an attribute on a name bound to the module itself — `import tests.skip_gates as declared`, and `from tests import skip_gates`, which is this suite's own idiom for a `tests/` helper and the spelling four of its files already use. A name any assignment in the module rebinds, at any scope, is not bound to the module and is refused — the once-bound discipline the opt-in key gets, applied to a module, and over the same whole-tree scope; it holds over both spellings, the imported name as well as the module receiver, and what it sees is an assignment to that name and not a `def`, a `class` or a second `import ... as` of it. A plain `import tests.skip_gates` binds the PACKAGE rather than the module, so `tests.skip_gates.no_binary(...)` is refused rather than blessing every `tests.<name>()` call under that import; a call to anything else is refused. The declaration cannot verify its argument's provenance — `nothing_found(rows)` is a claim the call site makes — and that is the point: an unmatched guard forces someone to write the claim where a reviewer greps for it, which is what the topic said a matcher would have done for the tape-bars gate on day one.

The alternative, a marker comment on the gate line read through the tokenizer, is not taken: a comment is not executed, is invisible to the AST the matcher already walks, and is the kind of prose this repo deletes when it stops paying; a call is code, and the module it calls into can be held closed by one test.

**D4. The registry is closed-world by test, not by convention.** One test parses `tests/skip_gates.py` and asserts that its imports are within an allowlist (`shutil`, `subprocess`, `pathlib`, `typing`), that every call in it reaches a name one of those imports binds or a builtin it is allowlisted for — of which there are none, the three functions calling only what their imports bind — that the one process launch it may make, under whatever name an import binds the launcher to, is `git` with every word of its argv a literal drawn from the local `rev-parse` `develop_resolves()` runs, the checkout it runs in handed to `cwd` and never written as an argv word, and that every function it defines anywhere in the module — not only at its top level, private ones included — is a single `return`, the public ones being exactly the three D3 names. The launch clause and the walk are what close the direction that matters: `subprocess` is on the allowlist for `develop_resolves()`, and without them a `subprocess.run(["curl", ...])` under a module-level `if` satisfies every other clause — under `import subprocess as sp` or `from subprocess import run` too, which is why the launcher is resolved through the imports rather than by its literal root. Pinning argv's head alone would not close it either: `git` is itself a network client, and `git ls-remote origin` — or a `-c protocol.ext.allow=always` transport — decides a skip on whether a remote answers, so the words are allowlisted rather than the program, and the shape of every argv element is pinned beside them: a word that is not a literal — a splat, a concatenation, an f-string, a name, a call — is a word an allowlist of literals cannot see, so it is refused rather than passed over, which the argv can require of every one of its elements because the path it runs in reaches the launch as `cwd` and not as a word to be read by text. The builtin allowlist closes the same direction one level down: `__import__("socket")` is a call and not an import statement, so the import allowlist above never sees it, and blocking the four names that import would not have held — `globals()["__builtins__"]["__import__"]` reaches the same module without loading any of them as a name. A registry that could open a socket would be the reducer's leak moved one file over; this test is what makes D3 a declaration rather than a loophole.

**D5. The reachability half is held by construction, and the count entry now holds both halves.** Under D1–D4 a skip is decided only by a form's reading or a registry call, and neither reaches a network: the forms are syntactic and each constrains what it reads, and the registry can neither import a module that opens a socket, nor reach one through a builtin it is not allowlisted for, nor launch a `git` whose argv leaves the local repository — every word of that argv is a literal on the allowlist, and the directory it runs in reaches it as `cwd`, a `pathlib` expression naming a filesystem path of the kind the path form already reads. One reading is left over, and it is named rather than claimed closed: D2 roots a receiver's chain and every call written in it, and does not judge what sits BESIDE that root, so a venue answer bound a line above and joined into a literal-rooted path — `s = _venue_name()`, then `not (ROOT / s).exists()` — decides the skip through a filesystem read the form allows. It is the narrow remainder of the hole D2's binding rule closes: the venue has to answer with something that names a path, where before it had only to answer at all. It is parked at this change's closeout as a topic, so it is evaluated rather than remembered. `infra/scripts/count-list.sh skip-gate-contract` runs the guard's suite and does not change. `CLAUDE.md`'s second entry, whose parenthetical says nothing asserts it, becomes true to the count once this lands; that edit is a guidance change on its own branch, on the owner's word, and this spec's closeout hands it over as a topic rather than making it — a PR body is never re-read after merge, so it is not a place an owed edit can live.

**D6. Discovery stays a walker and is widened by two forms, and its reach is asserted by the fixture alone.** The gates the matcher judges are the ones `_gates()` finds: `pytest.skip` calls under any enclosing condition, `skipif` marks, `raise unittest.SkipTest`. `@unittest.skipIf(...)`/`skipUnless(...)` and `self.skipTest(...)` are added, closing the gap the docstring names, and the decorator's callee is recognised the way this file already recognises pytest's: under any name `unittest` is bound to, and as `skipIf`/`skipUnless` imported from it under any name, alias included — the bound-name dict loses the imported name, so that half is resolved off the import nodes. A one-spelling arm would close one of that construct's four spellings and leave the other three a discovery hole no assertion in the file can see. The tree has no such gate today (two test modules import `unittest.mock`, neither skips through it), so the widening costs no migration. Discovery cannot be asserted from inside the guard — any set it computes to check the walker is computed by the walker — so `test_the_fixture_carries_every_position_a_skip_can_sit` stays as the one guard over it, and the docstring keeps saying so.

**D7. The reducer's resolution code is deleted, and its 27 planted defects become the matcher's fixture with a disposition each.** Two guards over one set diverge, and a reducer kept beside a matcher would be the permitting branch reappearing. Every fixture case the reducer carries is kept as source and re-labelled: the shapes a form accepts assert the form and its reading — the helper-hidden read among them, because the gate is the skip helper's own `pytest.skip` and the matcher reads the key at it, so the disposition that case asserts is the name and not a refusal; and every planted defect — the five bindings, the reachability helper and the inline probe, a reading one helper further from the gate, the computed key, the second flag in each spelling — asserts refusal. The six recognition mutations that SURVIVED against the reducer cannot survive here: a recognition the matcher loses turns a matched fixture case into a refused one, which the case asserts against.

**D8. The 32 gates migrate in the same change as the matcher, and no commit of the branch is red.** The order is: registry; the 32 gates rewritten under the reducer still in place, which reads a call into a module of ours and accepts the three registry names and every path the rewrites leave; the matcher over the fixture and the tree assertion in one commit, green over the migrated tree, with the assertion run once against the pre-migration tree to record that it names the 22; the reducer's resolution deleted. `tests/test_count_list.py`'s ten gates call `develop_resolves()` from the registry, and so does the unconditional assertion in that file which names the same precondition; the seven `shutil.which` gates read `no_binary("bash")` or `no_binary("sed")` at the gate, keeping the local for the run that follows and keeping their reason string and coverage pragma. Four of the five collection gates read `nothing_found(...)` over the local they already bind, and no decision of theirs moves. The fifth cannot be declared: `tests/test_tape_bars_rest_control.py`'s `day` is filtered by `stamps`, which is Kraken's REST answer, so `nothing_found(day)` would be a false claim over the one gate this spec cites as what a matcher would have refused on day one. Its local half is split out — `nothing_found(...)` over the archived days that are heal-complete, computed without `stamps` and bounded by the CALENDAR to the days a full REST window must reach, so the scan costs the window rather than the archive and an empty result is a statement about the archive alone. Its venue half becomes a `pytest.fail` beside the two that file already carries: inside that bound every heal-complete day is wholly inside a full-length window whatever hour the run starts at, so a window covering none of them is an answer short of the expected one and not an archive that has fallen behind.

The ten D2's receiver rule adds are three kinds again. Four read `DATA_ROOT` imported from `cli/portfolio/`, which is `<repo>/data/ohlc-full` and nothing else, so each file binds it at its own top level as the pathlib-of-literals it already is — the spelling `tests/test_crossfreq_system.py` and `tests/test_portfolio_builder.py` use for the same root today, and the two files read it at their gates and nowhere else. Five read a root the local config names — `_substrate_root(...)` in the two derivatives files, `load_config().nfs_mount_dir` in `tests/test_tape_bars_rest_control.py` — which is a local read the matcher cannot see and no literal may replace without pinning a mount the config owns, so each declares the presence it already computes through `nothing_found(...)` over the root it found. The tenth is the one membership gate, `tests/test_config_selectors_are_parsed.py`'s `path.name in _GRANDFATHERED`, whose operand is a parametrize value: a skip decided by a committed literal list is an exclusion rather than a gate, so the list filters the parametrize and the gate goes. The counts: 32 gates rewritten, 27 of them declarations; 65 tree gates, two skip gates leaving and one arriving.

**D9. Three probes, each on the guard's own reach.** A form widened to accept any call (mutation on the matcher; the fixture's reachability cases must fail); the registry importing `socket` (mutation on the registry; D4's test must fail); a real gate rewritten to an unmatched shape (mutation on one migrated test file; the tree assertion must fail and name the site). Each is run through `infra/scripts/mutate-probe.sh` and recorded on the commit that carries the guard it proves.

## Out of scope

**The `CLAUDE.md` entry's parenthetical** moves from "nothing asserts it" to the count when this lands; it is a guidance change, it rides its own branch on the owner's word, and the closeout hands it over.

**Gate discovery beyond the fixture.** A static analyser that proves the walker finds every skip in the tree is the same kind of work as this spec and is not it; D6 states the limit where the reducer stated it. [[T0190]]'s `## Suggested next steps` is where that residual is recorded today, in a bullet that defers to `tests/test_live_venue_opt_in.py`'s module docstring for what the surviving assertions miss — and this change archives the topic and deletes the docstring's sections that bullet points at, so the residual loses both carriers at once. It is parked as a topic minted at this change's closeout, ripe on the next branch that adds a skip in a position the fixture does not already carry.

**A venue answer joined into a literal-rooted path.** D2 roots a receiver's chain and refuses every call written in it, and does not judge the right side of a `/` join or an argument of a `pathlib` method, so `s = _venue_name()` then `not (ROOT / s).exists()` matches `path`. Rooting every operand instead would refuse three more of the tree's own path gates, measured: `tests/test_manifest_conformance.py` and `tests/test_registry_observed.py` join a parametrize value into the path, and `tests/test_universe_provenance.py` joins a regex group read off a committed document. So the rule roots the chain, and this is parked as a topic minted at this change's closeout, ripe on the next branch that writes a `/` join over a name the gate's own function binds.

**The registry's arguments.** `nothing_found(rows)` declares a provenance it cannot check; making it check — a scan helper that takes the path and does the glob itself — is a better registry and a larger migration, and can follow once the matcher holds. It is parked as a topic minted at this change's closeout, ripe on the next branch that adds a `nothing_found` gate, so the residual is evaluated rather than remembered.
