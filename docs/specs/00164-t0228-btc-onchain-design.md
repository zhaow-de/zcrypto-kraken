## Intent

C3 (`00.master-plan.md` §5, line 168) asks one question: "the only remaining question is marginal value inside the A1 gate", and it "would justify the first paid data dollar … only if the free version shows a signal worth sharpening" (the measured basis, 1).
Every vendor's multi-year on-chain history is restated in place with no vintages ([[T0016]]'s findings, *C3's data sources*), so this step computes BTC's valuation series ourselves from public chain data with Bitcoin Core and Bitview/BRK at a pinned commit, writes each UTC day once as its vintage, measures the build against Coin Metrics Community, and reads whether BTC's on-chain regime adds anything inside the A1 gate.
BTC only. LTC and DOGE follow only on a BTC signal worth sharpening, and nothing is bought for C3 unless it does (D2; T0228's gate, `T0228…:30`).
It is research. No value it writes feeds the live engine in this change.

## The question (no hypothesis, no bar)

After a decision close at which an on-chain feature, read one day late, lies in a tail of its own history, and inside one state of the A1 sleeve — long or flat — does an asset's forward return differ from its forward return after the other closes of that state, by enough to name a gate action (a veto or de-risk in the long state, a re-entry in the flat state) that clears the round trip? A cell that clears the potential criterion (D12) names the trial a later spec would register under a new family key `C3`; none clearing it is the evidence the owner's C3 family call reads and, by T0228, the reason nothing is bought.

## D6 — the day cut, the vintage's depth and the reading's lag (the owner's)

**The owner's ruling, 2026-11-22 (by 00:20:16Z), on D6–D13 together, verbatim: "All as recommended (Recommended)".** The option marked Recommended below is taken.

The cut decides which blocks a day's value holds, the depth decides when a day may be written, and the lag decides when the reading may use it; all three are fixed before any value is written.
1. **BRK's own cut, a depth of 12 blocks, a one-day lag** — **Recommended** `(the owner's ruling of 2026-11-22)`.
   - **The cut**: a block belongs to the UTC day of its monotonic timestamp, and a day's value is read at its last block (basis 2). Monotonic timestamps never decrease, so day D is closed in the chain's own terms once a block whose monotonic day is after D exists: no later block can join D (**inferred** from the definition). Day D's last block is the block before the first such block.
   - **The depth**: day D's vintage is written once D is closed and its last block `h(D)` has at least **12** confirmations, the node's tip at or above `h(D) + 12`. At the basis's inferred 559 s a block that is about 1 h 52 min after D closes (**inferred**). The reorg facts: BRK rolls its state back to the last valid chain state and recomputes (basis 2), so a reorg shallower than the depth before the write is absorbed; no historical reorg depth was read. A reorg deeper than the depth after the write is caught, never absorbed: each writer run re-reads the block hash at the recorded `h(D)` of the newest 30 written days and, on any mismatch, writes nothing, logs ERROR naming the day, the height and both hashes, and stays stopped until the owner rules — a written vintage is never rewritten, and a recompute is a new chain (D8, D9). **Author's call — 12**: the reading lags a full day anyway, so depth costs it nothing; 12 at the inferred pace leaves about 22 hours before the decision that first reads the day.
   - **The lag**: the reading uses day D's values from D+1's decision on. The A1 decision for the return from close k is taken at bar k's close, the 00:00Z that ends bar k's day; day `ts[k]` ends at that very instant and is not yet closed, while day `ts[k] − 1 day` has been closed and confirmed for most of a day. So the feature read at index k is day `ts[k] − 1 day`'s, and no value is read before it was knowable (D12).
   - Cost: a full day of staleness against the regime layer's daily grid; the history has no arrival times, so the cut cannot be checked against when blocks reached a node, only against the chain's own timestamps.
2. **A wall-clock cut** — the last block our node received before 00:00Z. Matches the engine's clock, but the history has no arrival times, so the history and the live days would be cut by two rules across a seam, and a re-index cannot reproduce a live day.
3. **A cut before the close** — the last block at a margin before 00:00Z (say 23:00Z by monotonic time), so a day's value reaches the same day's decision. BRK's date index is a whole UTC day (basis 2), so it needs a height-indexed read and our own aggregation, which the route's "No custom code" (D3) rules out; and a block's timestamp near the margin is the miner's to set.
