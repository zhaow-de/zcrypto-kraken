# The engine's cache, the engine-side half: the proxy sidecar, the wiring, the startup pass under a restored Cache, the harness, the one-gap rollout and the conditional lift

## Problem

A restart with a Kraken margin position open is forbidden today. On the pinned `nautilus-trader` (`2.0.0rc6.dev20260921`) Kraken's margin position report carries no entry price, and a start with such a position open goes one of two ways by the pair's fill history: it fails with `Unresolved positions during startup reconciliation … missing avg_px_open for position recovery` and the container restarts into the same failure, or it boots with the position's entry price at 0, where the realized-PnL gauge reads wrong; so the owner's operating rule of 2026-09-23 has every engine converge or restart close positions first, on the anchor paragraph and seven links in `infra/runbooks/engine-procedures.md`, nine links in `infra/runbooks/engine.md`, the standing rule and four drills (A2, D, F2, G) in `infra/runbooks/drills-order-path.md`, §1.7, §6 and §8 of `infra/runbooks/order-semantics-verification.md` and two lines of `docs/reference/fleet.md`'s Reboots, the anchor paragraph naming upstream #5065 as the rule's only lift. No restart on this pin has ever held a position: the adapter-verification page's step 6 is `Not run`, drill D's restore waited for B to flatten and read `Received 0 order(s), 23 fill(s), 0 position(s)`, A2 is `blocked` under the rule, and the flat restarts of the drill day, G and A1, read `Received 1 order(s), 21 fill(s), 0 position(s)` with the adopted order cancelled by the pass and its terminal minted 31 s on. What a restart must recover is what Rung 1's verdict, the `[iter-172]` entry of `docs/research/14.phase6-decisions.md`, and the drills of 2026-09-26 read: the position's quantity and entry price, the resting order's identity and fills, and the intents the restart orphans, which spec 00119 D13 to D15 and D17 to D20 settle for a cache-less restart and this spec keeps working under a restored one.

Spec 00118 built the store, three Valkey 9.1.2 nodes under Sentinel on the `zcache0` mesh, and its plan's Resolution left one list for this half: D1's live proof, D4's proxy, D8's password path, D10's wiring, D11's startup pass, D12's proxy parts, D13's proxy role and tag, D14's proxy rollout, and D15's harness and steps 5 and 6. The probes of 2026-09-28, offline on the pinned wheel against a local Valkey 8.1.1 (the fleet runs 9.1.2), measured what the library's restore does with 00118 D10's wiring exactly: it fails on every Kraken instrument, because the store saves a currency as its bare code and the loader resolves that code against a registry the adapter has not yet filled (`Failed to deserialize currency ZEUR … Unknown currency: ZEUR`, then every instrument, order and position priced in it dropped, and the start aborting on the position exactly as today); with the codes registered before the node builds, the restore works: the order keeps its own client order id and strategy id and reconciliation matches it by venue order id without re-keying, the position restores with its id, `avg_px_open 150.0` and no `Unresolved positions`, a fill made while down is booked from `TradesHistory`, and every later event reaches the own strategy's topic flagged `reconciliation=True`. Three defects of the same restore shape the startup pass: reconciliation regresses a partially filled restored order's status to ACCEPTED; an order the venue no longer lists open is never closed, since mass status reads open orders only and `ClosedOrders` is never requested; and a WebSocket trade frame on a restored open order is booked twice, an inferred fill for the cumulative gap and then the fill itself, leaving the order at 0.8 where the venue said 0.6. Beside those: `.build()` touches no cache and `run()` creates the backing before any venue client connects, raising `RuntimeError: failed to create cache database backing` in 0.22 s on a refused port and 35 s on a silent peer under six retries, the library's defaults still blocked past 540 s, the refused shape's backoff growing about as 2^N; the library's cache-write failures log at ERROR and WARN under `nautilus_infrastructure::redis::cache`, match the capture Alloy's regex and reach Loki as `container="engine-nautilus"`, the link recovers lazily on the next write, which is dropped, and nothing written during an outage is replayed; the proxy's Sentinel check needs its RESP length markers escaped as `\$` inside double quotes and no `QUIT` step, and the address-only check keeps routing a dead primary until Sentinel promotes a replica; and `.RestartCount` is a field of one container's record that `compose down` removes, so it reads 0 after every `systemctl restart` and proves nothing.

This spec is the engine half, serial 00120, under the owner's rulings of 2026-09-28: it carries 00118's decisions by reference and amends them in 00118's own `## Spec amendments` where the probes or the rulings depart from their text; a cold start is a valid scenario, the cache an accelerator and a recovery of what the venue cannot give, never the authority on what is open, so 00118 D11's pre-start guard is withdrawn; every issue found is decided here or named in the measured basis as read at a named moment, and no new topic is registered; and the two design choices, the proxy under the engine role and the startup pass attaching the restored own orders at `on_start` with the venue as the authority, were taken on the owner's word of 2026-09-28, the six design sections approved as presented. The memo's standing facts land here: one engine image carrying spec 00119 and this half, converged in one gap together with the ops drop-in of PR #625, then Rung 2, then the nautilus bump; a first start on this half, 2026-09-25, was abandoned and kept nothing, so no branch, commit or decision of it is carried here; and the seating is Fable for the spec, the plan and the plan-review's fixer, Opus for everything else, the plan-review's lenses, the task loop's implementers and reviewers, and the trio.

## Decisions

**D1. The proxy is a `cache-proxy` service beside `engine` in the engine's compose project, rendered by the engine role under the `engine` tag, the official `haproxy` image digest-pinned, its config a root-owned 0600 file bind-mounted read-only, three Sentinel-checked backends over the mesh addresses, the Prometheus endpoint on `127.0.0.1:9104`.** `compose.yaml.j2` gains the service: `image: haproxy@{{ cache_proxy_image_digest }}` (a `fleet-pins.md` row `cache-proxy` on `zcrypto`, first pin; `converge.sh`'s `EVKEYS` gains the key; the engine role's three digest guards apply to it as to the engine image — non-empty, pre-staged on the host, the replaced running digest recorded in `fleet-pins.md` — and the canary parity does not, being scoped to `engine_image_digest`: there is no capture bake of an upstream proxy image), `container_name: zcrypto-cache-proxy`, `restart: unless-stopped`, a 32m memory cap that holds only with `maxconn 256` in the config's `global` — without a bound HAProxy sizes itself off the container's file-descriptor hard limit, 524288 on the fleet's containerd, and its worker, about 69 MB resident, is killed under the cap at load; at 256 it runs and answers `/metrics` under it, measured on 3.4.5 — `logging: json-file` as the engine's, the config bind-mounted `/opt/zcrypto-engine/haproxy.cfg:/usr/local/etc/haproxy/haproxy.cfg:ro`, `ports: 127.0.0.1:9104:9104`. The role renders the file root-owned 0600 with `no_log` and `diff: false`, since the Sentinel `requirepass` lives in it and nowhere in the compose file, whose secret-free property stays; the service runs its entrypoint as root so the 0600 file is readable at start and the config's `global` section drops to the image's `haproxy` user after the read, the same file ownership `engine.env` has. The file is rendered to `haproxy.cfg.next`, validated with the pinned image's own `haproxy -c` (`docker run --rm` with the file mounted, `no_log`), and moved into place only then, so an invalid render never sits where the unit's next start would read it; the four tasks run ahead of the engine's env, toml and compose renders, so a refusal stops before any engine file is rewritten and the file exists before the compose file names it, and the render and the validation run in check mode too, so `converge.sh`'s preview is the validation's first run — measured: `-c` refuses an unescaped `$` inside a double-quoted argument as an environment variable, and passes an unset variable, which is why the password is in the rendered file and not the container's environment, where a missing value fails only at runtime as a backend with no UP server. Three backends `node1` to `node3`, each three `server` lines at the node's mesh address on 6379 with `check addr <one Sentinel's mesh address> port 26379`, `inter 1s`, `fall 2`, `rise 2`, `init-state fully-down`, `on-marked-down shutdown-sessions`, and one `tcp-check` sequence: `AUTH <requirepass>` expecting `+OK`, `PING` expecting `+PONG`, `SENTINEL master zcache` expecting the anchored `\$2\r\nip\r\n\$10\r\n10.98.0.1N\r\n`, and no `QUIT` step, which Sentinel refuses as an unknown command; `timeout check 2s`, since a mismatched reply is not detected as wrong but waited for until that timeout, so a node leaves routing within about six seconds of its Sentinels ceasing to name it; the frontend on 6379 routes `use_backend nodeN if { nbsrv(nodeN) ge 2 }`, two of three Sentinels agreeing; `timeout client` and `timeout server` are disabled, since the library's session idles for hours between writes and reconnects only on the next write, which it drops, so an idle cut would cost a write per idle period. `init-state` is a keyword of HAProxy 3.1 and later by its changelog, so the pin is the current LTS line, read at plan time, and `-c` on the rendered template against the pinned image refuses one without it. The engine's `depends_on: cache-proxy` takes `condition: service_started` only, the engine's retry budget (D5) being the start-order guard, and the engine's Alloy-side scrape of `:9104` is D14's. D4's check keeps routing a dead primary until Sentinel promotes a replica, `down-after-milliseconds` 5000 plus the failover; the gap is accepted, and a flags-aware expect, `flags` exactly `master`, which went DOWN within one check of the primary's kill in the probe, is named and set aside as brittle: it drops on any transient Sentinel-to-primary link loss, carries benign flags, and 9.1.2's flag order is unread. Three homes were read and set aside: a separate `cache_proxy` role with its own handler recreating one service in a project the unit runs attached (untested here, and the unit's `down` stops the proxy with every engine stop anyway); its own compose project and unit joined by an external network (a declared network in both files, a start-order dependency the engine's budget cannot cover, a second unit to recover after a reboot); and a host-networked proxy on loopback, which the engine's bridge network cannot reach. The cost is that every proxy change or re-pin is an engine restart, gap-bound and flat under the operating rule until D17's lift, and that a failover's `shutdown-sessions` costs the engine the first write after it, which the lazy reconnect drops (the measured shape on a killed server), so the store is behind by that write and `cache-manual-failover` in `infra/runbooks/cache.md` says so — and not a failover's alone: each backend's three server entries are one Valkey address checked through one Sentinel each, the default roundrobin balance spreads the engine's sessions over them, and `on-marked-down shutdown-sessions` closes the sessions an entry carries whenever that one Sentinel fails two checks, a replica node's reboot, a Sentinel restart or a mesh blip to one node among the causes, so a cut costs a write more often than failovers would, and the no-session and write-failed pages name the per-entry cut.

**D2. The two cache secrets the engine play needs move to `group_vars/all/vault.yml`; the rotation gains an engine-host leg; the `cache-link` tag on `zcrypto` joins the engine window guard.** `cache_engine_password` and `cache_sentinel_requirepass` move from `group_vars/cache_host/vault.yml` to `group_vars/all/vault.yml`, where `cache_wg_zcrypto_private_key` and the bridgehead's keys already sit, so the cache role reads them unchanged and the engine play reads them at all; the other three stay in the cache group's vault. Two options were set aside: a `hostvars` lookup into a cache node's group vars from the engine play, which decrypts the cache group's file on every engine run and is a shape this tree has nowhere; and a duplicate in `group_vars/engine_host/vault.yml`, two copies to keep equal with a drift assert and a rotation naming both. The cost is two secrets readable by every play, a class `group_vars/all/vault.yml` already holds with the Grafana token and the Slack webhook. `engine.env.j2` gains `ZCRYPTO_CACHE_PASSWORD={{ cache_engine_password }}` under the file's existing `no_log` render, and `haproxy.cfg` carries `cache_sentinel_requirepass` (D1); the role's five-character floor on a password (00118 D8) holds for both, and a value shorter than five characters of `[A-Za-z0-9]` fails the engine role too, since the check's inline `AUTH` has no quoting. `cache-password-rotation` gains one leg: after the three nodes' one stop, in the same gap, a `--tags engine` converge of `zcrypto` re-renders `engine.env` and `haproxy.cfg` and its handler restarts the engine and the proxy; between the nodes' stop and that converge the engine holds no session the new passwords admit, its writes are dropped and the store is behind, so the rotation is scheduled while flat and the engine converge is the restart the outage line of D14 names. The engine play's window-guard pre-tasks in `site.yml` take `tags: [engine, cache-link]`, so a `cache-link` converge on `zcrypto` — whose handler restarts `wg-quick@zcache0` on any change and drops the engine's cache traffic for the seconds the interface is down, cutting the session as a failover does — waits for the inter-cycle gap, and the `cache_link` role takes `when: "'engine' not in ansible_skip_tags"`, since `--skip-tags engine` — the Alloy bump's primary shape — skips the guard's tasks with the engine's and would otherwise converge the link at any hour; the comment-only timer drift pending on all four mesh members lands at the next `--tags cache-link` converge inside a gap, `--tags engine` running the guard and not the `cache_link` role, and not at this rollout's, whose tags are D16's.

**D3. `EngineConfig` gains one nested frozen dataclass `cache` from `[zcrypto.engine.cache]` with its own strict arm; the password is `ZCRYPTO_CACHE_PASSWORD` from the environment, read at node build and refused there when absent.** `CacheSettings(enabled: bool = False, host: str = "cache-proxy", port: int = 6379, username: str = "engine")`, and `_build_engine` hands the `cache` key to `_build_cache`, which refuses a non-table, an unknown key, a non-boolean `enabled`, an empty `host` or `username`, and a `port` that is a boolean or outside 1 to 65535, each with the loader's own message shape; `tests/test_config.py` gains a case per refusal, and the committed `zcrypto.toml` keeps no engine table. The password is read in `_node_builder` when `enabled`, by name through the trade credentials' shape — the variable named in the refusal, the value never stored on a module object, logged or interpolated — and its absence refuses to build the node, since a loader error would make every `zcrypto` command on the workstation, where the file is read too, need a password the config file must never carry. A flattened `cache_enabled`, `cache_host` set under `[zcrypto.engine]` was set aside: it fits the flat loader and contradicts 00118 D10's named table. `zcrypto.toml.j2` renders the table with `enabled = true`, `host = "cache-proxy"`, `port = 6379` and `username = "engine"` from the first boot, the rollout ruling (D16), each value explicit so a converge diff shows it, behind the role default `engine_cache_enabled: true`: `-e engine_cache_enabled=false`, a `converge.sh` key, renders no table at all, the way back to an engine that runs without the cache inside the same gap, since an engine image from before the table refuses a config that carries it and a compose re-pin alone leaves the rendered file in place; past the gap the same value is committed as `engine_cache_enabled: false` in `host_vars/zcrypto/vars.yml`, since the role default is `true` and the next engine converge, the rollout skill's shape naming no key, would render the table again, and the change that re-enables the cache reverts it. `enabled = false` renders nothing into the node and builds nothing (D5), and the committed default is `false`. The cost is the first nested table in `_build_engine` and one more environment variable on the engine host's env file.

**D4. Before the node builds, the engine registers the Kraken currency codes the basket's instruments carry, in the adapter's spelling, through `Currency.register`.** The store saves a currency as its bare code (`trader-SHADOW-001:currencies:ZEUR` holds `"ZEUR"`) and the loader resolves it against the process's registry, which in a fresh process knows no Kraken non-ISO code: the adapter mints `ZEUR`, `XXBT` and the rest only when it parses AssetPairs, after `Loaded cache`, so without the registration every instrument, order and position priced in such a code is dropped at load and the start goes today's way — measured on every Kraken instrument, and measured working with the two codes registered. `cli/engine/node.py` carries `_KRAKEN_CURRENCIES`, one entry per base and quote code the twelve `INSTRUMENT_IDS` name, with the code, precision, ISO number, name and type the adapter mints for it, and `build_shadow_node` registers each before `.build()` when the cache is enabled; a pin test parses the tree's AssetPairs snapshot through the adapter's own parser and asserts each entry equals the `Currency` the adapter mints, so a bump that reshapes a minted currency is red before a store carrying it loads, and a registration that disagreed with the adapter would poison the adapter's instruments, since a registered code is read rather than minted. Two options were set aside: reading the codes from the store before build, which holds the code alone and no precision; and registering nothing and taking the loader's failure as a cold start, which loses the restore on every instrument, the half's whole purpose. The cost is a table the nautilus bump re-reads and one pin test; the bare-code store is handed upstream (D18).

**D5. The wiring: two builder calls when enabled, the retry count ten under five-second timeouts, `load_cache` stated, `load_state` and `save_state` off, the interface pin widened.** `_node_builder` appends `.with_cache_config(CacheConfig(use_instance_id=False, flush_on_start=False))` and `.with_cache_database_factory(RedisCacheConfig(host, port, username, password, ssl=False, connection_timeout=5, response_timeout=5, number_of_retries=10))` only when `config.cache.enabled`; the two `CacheConfig` values equal the library's defaults and are stated because `True` on the first reloads an empty namespace and on the second issues `FLUSHDB`. The retry count departs from 00118 D10's six on the probes' numbers: the refused shape's delay grows about as 3.3 ms times 2^N — 0.22 s at six, 3.40 s at ten, 27.75 s at thirteen — and the silent-peer shape is one five-second timeout per attempt, 35 s at six and about 55 s at ten; the design makes the budget the start-order guard behind `service_started`, and at six the refused shape gives a proxy still marking its backends UP, about a second after both containers start, 0.22 s, which is no guard, where ten covers about three seconds and keeps the silent shape inside the minute 00118 D10 named, the inter-cycle gap and the harness's timeout; the count stays below the twelve past which the refused shape outruns the silent one. `.build()` connects nothing and `run()` creates the backing before any venue client connects, so an unreachable cache fails the start without the venue being touched, `RuntimeError: failed to create cache database backing` out of `run()`, which `cli/__main__.py` logs through the `zcrypto` logger and the unit restarts ten seconds on, `zcrypto-engine-error-logs` and the engine-dark rule paging as 00118 D10 said. `_exec_engine_config` states `load_cache=True` beside its five, the docstring counting six; `load_state` and `save_state` stay off, the ledger being the executor's state and the library's own warning that they have no effect without a backing now false; `tests/test_nautilus_interface_pin.py` gains `CacheConfig` and `RedisCacheConfig` with their fields, the two `CacheConfig` defaults, the two node-config defaults and `load_cache`, and `RecordingBuilder` gains the two methods `test_every_builder_call_exists_on_the_library` then holds. The `engine` ACL line admits `INFO`, the library's version check and its one `@dangerous` command, measured: a user without `+info` is refused at the first `INFO` and the start fails 0.3 s on; `+keys` is admitted and unused, the load using `SCAN`. Keys land under `trader-SHADOW-001:` in database 0, every TTL −1, no `SELECT` and no `FLUSHDB` issued. The cost of ten retries is a slower refusal, 55 s against 35, on a proxy whose backends never answer.

**D6. At `on_start` the executor attaches every restored own order, and the startup pass treats a restored order as it treats an adopted one, with the venue as the authority: the venue read covers every restored non-terminal order and every finished row with fills.** `on_start` runs after the cache load and reconciliation and before the first tick; there the strategy reads `cache.orders_open` under its own strategy id for the boot line (D11) — the restored own orders, under their own client order ids, strategy `000`, which reconciliation keeps (measured: matched by `venue_order_id`, nothing re-keyed, every later event on `on_order_event` and none on the EXTERNAL observer) — and the executor records as the restored set for the process's life every order the Cache holds for the venue — through `cache.orders`, open and closed copies alike, since a kept reducer's double-booked copy reads FILLED where the venue says partial (D7) and a set read from the open orders alone would trust exactly that copy at the next restart; and this engine's own and the EXTERNAL copies alike, since the store persists the EXTERNAL copy a previous process adopted by its txid and restores it with that process's fills, the same library state as a restored own order, which nothing tells from a copy this boot's reconciliation created, so the venue answers for both and both credit their fills nothing (D8), the lines naming a restored order, the boot line's and the classification's, keyed on the own strategy id — and attaches each whose row the ledger's two-day window carries, under its own id and its txid, at construction itself and not at the first tick, so a fill on it in the seconds before that tick lands in its row and never in the unknown-order trip. `_read_venue_orders`' population widens from rows the Cache cannot answer to every row whose Cache order is in the restored set, whatever its copy's status, and every finished row with fills whatever the Cache holds, so the venue is asked at every restart that restored anything, and `_reconcile_adopted_rows` and `_reconcile_finished_rows` take the venue's report over the Cache's restored copy where both answer: the Cache's copy is the previous process's view, and the withdrawal check compared against it would compare the ledger with itself. Trusting the Cache only for orders reconciliation touched this boot, read off event flags, was set aside as resting on the flags' meaning across shapes the loopback cannot script; widening to non-terminal restored orders alone, 00118 D11's text, was set aside because it leaves the withdrawal check on finished rows blind. The cost is one `ClosedOrders` and `QueryOrders` read at every restart that restored an order, a failed read refusing every plan as today, and a restored-id set held for the process's life.

**D7. A restored order the venue reports closed has its row written from the venue's report and no cancel sent; a restored order the venue reports open is cancelled by the pass unless it is a kept reducer, which is the defence against the double booking.** Mass status reads open orders only on this pin and `ClosedOrders` is never requested, so an order that filled, was cancelled or expired while the engine was down comes back open in the Cache and stays so for the process's life (measured over 60 s: status unchanged, no event on either topic); the pass's venue read answers it, `_reconcile_adopted_row` writes the row's state and fills from the report, and the classification loop sends no cancel for an order the venue holds closed — the library's stale open copy is left where it is, harmless to the ledger, and named as this pin's cost: it is listed by `orders_open` at every later read of this process, and upstream #5110 closes such orders on the next bump. A restored order the venue reports open takes 00119 D13's rule as an adopted one does: cancelled by the pass through the strategy handle, since the Cache holds it under its own id, unless its row is a ledgered reducer and the gate is not NONE; whether that cancel's venue answer is applied on a restored own order, or takes the adopted path's 31 s mint, is live-only and read at the proof window (D16). The cancel is the defence: a WebSocket trade frame on a restored open order is booked twice by the library — an inferred fill for the cumulative gap from the frame's status half, then the fill from its fill half — and the probe read the order at 0.8 where the venue said 0.6, `Ignoring fill decrease without explicit void evidence` at the cancel, the position LONG 0.8; every restored open order the pass cancels can no longer fill that way past the seconds before the venue's cancel, and the one it keeps is D8's. Cancelling the kept reducer too was set aside, since it is the live record of a close the previous process meant, and closing the stale copies through the strategy handle was set aside, since the handle's cancel of an order the venue holds closed answers nothing this engine applies. The cost is a fill racing the pass's cancel on a restored opener, booked twice in the Cache and once in the ledger by D8's credit rule.

**D8. The kept reducer's fills are credited to its row from the venue's cumulative figure at the next pass, not from the event's quantity, and its intent is written at the reducer's terminal on whichever path its events take.** A fill on the kept reducer reaches the detached path (D12); its `fill` line is journaled with the event's quantity and trade id, the stream's record, and `credited` 0, so a double-booked frame credits the row nothing and trips no false overfill, and a WARNING at the fill names the order and the flat restart its double-booked copy forces (D17), whatever the re-read pass's read later answers; the fill arms the re-read pass, whose population gains the rows of the restored set with a fill since their last read, and that pass's venue read repairs the row up to the venue's cumulative figure through the startup's repair arm, a `reconciled` line the ledger's readers count into `held` beside the zero credit (00119 D19 and D27). The reducer's intent, `pending` since the sweep leaves an open row the pass sent no cancel for (00119 D14), is written at the reducer's terminal by the path that carries it — the detached path's completion write for a fill that completes the row, or its terminal-state write for a cancel or expiry — `filled` with the row's `filled_qty` or `revoked` with `the kept reducer ended without filling`, through `update_plan_intent` at the row's own boundary without `self._plan`, the sweep's shape; D14's rule that no later pass revisits a written intent stands; D20's that the re-read pass writes no intent does not hold for this one row: with the credit at zero a fill never completes the row on the event path, so a reducer that fills whole would keep its intent `pending` for the process's life, and the pass's repair writes it, once, for a row of the restored set whose intent still reads `pending`, from the row's own state; and a row a fill reached since the pass last read it is not settled by the event's path at all — a terminal before the pass, the venue's cancel ack or an expiry, puts it among the window's closed rows, which the pass's population takes for such rows too, so the intent is written after the repair at the repaired quantity and never at the credit-0 one. A rule of the pass's own, settling the intent at startup with a new outcome, was set aside: its outcome would no longer say whether the reducer filled. The cost is a row whose `filled_qty` lags the venue by one re-read pass, the Cache's own copy of a partial fill double-booked for the process's life, and what the next restart's reconciliation makes of that copy — a difference against the venue's smaller figure, a diff fill at the cached average price, or a failed start where it crosses zero, the estimate's reading — which is bounded to a kept reducer's partial fills, settled on the gauge by 00119 D28's holdings read, and handed upstream (D18).

**D9. A restored order's fill state is read as `filled_qty` says, never as its status, wherever the executor reads a restored order.** Reconciliation appends `OrderAccepted(reconciliation=True)` to a partially filled restored order and reads `[PARTIALLY_FILLED] -> [ACCEPTED]`, since Kraken's `open` maps to ACCEPTED whatever `vol_exec` says (measured), so the boot line (D11) and the classification's cancel line read partial from `0 < filled_qty < quantity`; `_ADOPTED_TERMINAL_STATES` maps closed statuses alone, so the regressed status writes no state, and no third site reads the status. An unfilled restored order whose Kraken row carries `price "0.00000"` may be repriced to 0.00 by the same report path, an inference from the parser and not a probe, so the boot line prints each restored order's price and the proof window reads it. The cost is one predicate at two sites.

**D10. Realized PnL takes a per-instrument baseline at `on_start`.** `_realized_eur` sums the Cache's open and closed positions per traded instrument, and a restored closed position would carry a previous run's realization into a number presented as this window's, a restored open one its earlier partial closes; at `on_start` the executor stores, per instrument, the sum the Cache then holds, and `_realized_eur` subtracts it, so the gauge reads this process's realizations and a position restored at its entry price shows no phantom. The baseline is read after reconciliation's fills, the library's order, and before any fill of this process, the tick's. Two options were set aside: filtering positions by first-seen ids, which shows a restored open position's later close whole, `realized_pnl` being unsplit; and an own accumulator in `_publish_fill`, the library's PnL arithmetic and its commission currencies duplicated.
