## Intent and why

C3 (`docs/research/00.master-plan.md` §5 bucket C, line 168: "MVRV-Z / SOPR-style valuation bands as *additional regime-gate inputs* only") needs point-in-time data, a value for day D as it was knowable at D, and §8's data policy (line 243) wants every dataset "immutable + versioned … never 'latest'".
Every vendor studied restates history in place. Coin Metrics restated BTC `SplyCur` by exactly +100 BTC between its 2026-03 GitHub snapshot and today, and moved LINK MVRV on 298 of 327 overlapping days between that repository's 2026-05-24 archive and 2026-11-11, by at most 3.39e-05 relative, with no matching notice (the 2026-10-31 sourcing read and the 2026-11-11 off-list read, consolidated into T0016's findings, which PR #850 merged to `develop`). No free vintage archive exists: the `coinmetrics/data` repository stopped on 2026-05-24.
A recorder that stores, from its first tick, exactly what each free source served and when turns that defect into a measured one: as-first-published values going forward, and a diff of every later restatement.
Everything a source returns at the first tick is the vendor's history as revised up to that day, never point-in-time. That is why the start date matters and why each day of delivery is a day the record never holds.
It is research data. Nothing here feeds the live engine.

## Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals:
- From the first tick, every byte of each source's final answer, whatever its status, with the recorder's own fetch time, kept forever and unchanged in two copies, the ops node's and the NAS's, each copy re-verified on a schedule. Four exceptions, named: a body over 64 MiB and a body cut by its deadline are recorded as attempts without their bytes, an attempt that was retried keeps only its first 4 KiB (D4), and a BGeometrics body that holds the account key, or that D20's scan cannot read, is recorded as an attempt without its bytes (D20).
- Two ticks a day, so one failed tick never costs a day, and a page while a day at risk can still be saved by hand.
- Each tick a self-contained directory a reader can copy and verify alone, linked to its predecessor so a missing tick is detectable: in any copy once a later tick names it, and as a fork in a copy that pulled a head deleted before its successor ran (D15).
- A derivation, proven on fixtures in this change, that answers "first published", "restated" and "first appeared" for any series and day, and the as-of view, from the stored bytes alone, and a versions table on the ops node built from it after every recorder run: append-only, any head of it pinned by its hash, and every head rebuilt byte for byte from any copy of the record that still holds the head's ancestry as the head records it (D16).
- Boring to run for years: the ops timer pattern, bounded in time, bytes and requests, every hole loud.

Non-goals:
- Any C3 trial, its subject and the head it pins, and the registry arm and hub channel the first citation of the versions table needs: the first C3 trial spec's (D16).
- The BTC chain computation: its own topic, T0228.
- Any paid source, any account beyond the owner's free BGeometrics registration (D20), and every Kraken endpoint.
- Any input to the engine.

Anti-goals, what the recorder must never do:
- Synthesise, backfill or relabel a day: no code writes a tick whose stamp is not its own fetch time.
- Rewrite or delete a published byte, on ops or on the NAS. A hash-checked restore (D15) puts the published bytes back where a copy lost or damaged them, and is not a rewrite.
- Re-serialise a body.
- Let a judgement about a body stop it being stored. Two named exceptions bound it: D4's 64 MiB cap, and a BGeometrics body that holds the account key, or that D20's scan cannot read (D20).
- Write through the NFS mount.
- Carry a credential anywhere but a BGeometrics request's `Authorization` header, or a person's name on the request path; store or log a credential (D20).

## Operating model

- **Machines and processes.** One host, `zcrypto-ops`, which is both the research workstation and the venue-facing ops node. On it, one timer fires one oneshot unit at 07:37 and 19:37 UTC. The unit runs one root runner, and the runner runs one attached container. Every start of that unit pulls in a second oneshot unit, the derive, which starts once the first has ended and runs one attached container of its own (D16). The NAS's `zcrypto-archive-pull` container pulls the record hourly and re-verifies a slice of its copy each loop, and research reads it in place, on ops or through the NAS mount, read-only.
- **Shared state and its writers.** `vintages/record/` and `vintages/staging/` are written only by the recorder container, which holds `single_instance_lock` on `vintages/` for its whole run. Six sibling ops containers run as the same uid with the whole ops data root mounted read-write: `zcrypto-reconcile` and `zcrypto-trade-backfill` (`archive-pull.sh.j2:126` and `:159`), `zcrypto-panel-materialize` (`panel-materialize.sh.j2:36`), `zcrypto-tape-bars` and `zcrypto-rolling-extend` (`tape-bars.sh.j2:40` and `:141`), and the liquidations poller `zcrypto-ops-liquidations` (`compose.yaml.j2:46`, its user at `:16`); verify-replay mounts it read-only (`verify-replay.sh.j2:62`). Their code, not the permissions, keeps them out of `vintages/` and `vintage-versions/`; D5's 0555 tick directories and 0444 files, and the same modes on the table's generation, part and head directories and their files (D16), make a stray write fail rather than land. The census after the change is `git grep -l ops_vintages_subdir -- cli/ infra/README.md infra/docker/ infra/grafana/ infra/nas/ infra/ops/ infra/runbooks/ infra/scripts/ infra/systemd/ infra/ansible/roles/ infra/ansible/scripts/ infra/ansible/inventory infra/ansible/site.yml infra/ansible/bootstrap.yml infra/ansible/files/README.md 'infra/ansible/group_vars/*/vars.yml' infra/ansible/group_vars/observed/alloy.yml 'infra/ansible/host_vars/*/vars.yml'`, which names only paths outside the vaulted set, because bash-guard builds its file list from the positive pathspecs alone and refuses one that reaches a vaulted file, whatever the excludes say (`scoped_grep` in `.claude/hooks/bash-guard.sh`); bash-guard admitted it on 2026-11-11 and, with `ops_data_dir` as the control pattern, it listed that variable's ten files. Its purpose is that no runner template or compose file other than the recorder's and the derive's names the variable: among `infra/ansible/roles/ops/templates/` it prints `vintage-record.sh.j2` and `vintage-derive.sh.j2` alone, the derive's mounting `record/` read-only, beside `infra/ansible/roles/ops/defaults/main.yml`, `infra/ansible/roles/ops/tasks/main.yml` and any prose page that names it. `textfile/vintage.prom` is written by the record CLI alone and `textfile/vintage-derive.prom` by the derive CLI alone. `/volume1/ZhaoCrypto/vintages` is written by the NAS pull alone, and by a hash-checked restore (D15). `{{ ops_compose_dir }}/vintage-bgeometrics.token` is written by the ops role alone and read by the runner alone (D20). `/var/lib/zcrypto-ops/vintage-versions/` is written by the derive container alone, under its own lock, after each recorder run, and by a hand put-back of a damaged table file, which places bytes the chain's hashes decide; a hand rebuild writes only into an empty scratch root it is given, never there (D16).
- **What identifies a unit across copies.** A tick is identified by its directory name, the UTC start second (`20261112T073700Z`), and by the sha256 of its `tick.json` bytes. A body is identified by its tick, its source id and the sha256 of its raw bytes. All three are the same in the ops copy, the NAS copy and any research copy, because nothing rewrites a published file and the NAS hop never re-sends one. A head of the versions table is identified by its generation, its tick and the sha256 of its file's bytes; the table has one copy, on ops, and a rebuild from any copy of the record that holds a head's ancestry as the head records it reproduces that head's bytes (D16).
- **When the state can change.** `record/` changes only when a new tick directory appears by rename, at a timer slot, at a boot catch-up or at a hand start, and is then set to 0555; a hand restore (D15) is the only other change, and it puts back the bytes the tick's manifest already hashes, or, for a `tick.json`, the bytes its witness hashes (D5). `staging/` changes only while a tick holds the lock. A staging directory whose process has died is dead: it is never published, and the next tick names it in a WARNING. The table changes only when the derive publishes a generation, or a tick's part and then its head, after a recorder run. Nothing in it is rewritten, with one exception, as for the record: a hand put-back of a damaged table file, which places bytes the chain's hashes decide (D16).
- **A missed tick** publishes nothing. The gauges age and the next tick runs normally. **A double tick** ends one of three ways: a `systemctl start` while the timer's run is active joins that job and starts nothing; a second `docker run` under the container's stable `--name` fails in docker with docker's own name-conflict message and exit, before the CLI starts (D12); any other second writer meets the lock and refuses loudly, with exit 2 and the recorder's own message (D9). **A restart** loses nothing: the CLI keeps no state between ticks, and every gauge is recomputed from `record/`. **A reboot over a slot** runs one tick at boot (`Persistent=true`), never one per missed slot.
- **Who reads the page.** One operator, the owner, on the `metrics` receiver, attended or by phone; the missed-source page is critical, as the owner ruled (D11). A silence on one source's page is set by the owner, or by marco on the owner's word for that silence (D11).

## D1 — the recorder is an ops timer running one container per tick, not a daemon

**marco's call.** `zcrypto-vintage-record.timer` fires `zcrypto-vintage-record.service`. The unit is Type=oneshot, has `Requires=docker.service`, `Wants=network-online.target` and `After=docker.service network-online.target`, and carries no `RequiresMountsFor`. It also has `Wants=zcrypto-vintage-derive.service`, which pulls in the derive unit, ordered after this one, so the derive starts only once this unit's run has ended (D16). The network ordering is archive-pull's (`archive-pull.service.j2:10-12`), whose `Persistent=true` boot catch-up also dials out: without it, a `Persistent=true` boot tick taken before the uplink is up spends all eighteen requests on transport errors. Its ExecStart is the root runner `/usr/local/sbin/zcrypto-vintage-record`, rendered from `infra/ansible/roles/ops/templates/vintage-record.sh.j2`, mode 0700, rendered `no_log` and `diff: false` because it carries the dead-man URL (D12).
The runner first opens the BGeometrics token file on descriptor 3, `{ exec 3<"{{ ops_compose_dir }}/vintage-bgeometrics.token"; } 2>/dev/null`, and keeps it only when that open succeeds and `[ -f /dev/fd/3 ]` holds; otherwise it opens `/dev/null` on descriptor 3 instead and logs one parse-shaped ERROR naming the path, never a byte of the file (D20). An absent, unreadable or directory token file then costs the four `bg-*` sources alone, which read `no credential`, while every other source records (the measured basis, bash). It then runs one attached `docker run --rm -i --pull never --name zcrypto-vintage-record --user "{{ ops_uid }}:{{ ops_gid }}" -v "{{ ops_data_dir }}/{{ ops_vintages_subdir }}:/data" -v "{{ ops_textfile_dir }}:/textfile" --entrypoint zcrypto "{{ ops_image }}@{{ ops_image_digest }}" vintage record --data-dir /data --textfile /textfile/vintage.prom --clock-prom /textfile/clock-offset.prom --bgeometrics-key-stdin "$@" <&3`. `"$@"` is the runner's own arguments: the unit passes none, and the runbook's hand start of named sources passes `--source <id>` (D7). Its only input redirection duplicates a descriptor already open, so that redirect cannot fail before docker starts. It passes no `-e`, no `--env-file`, no network flag and no NAS mount, and mounts no secret: the BGeometrics key reaches the CLI on stdin alone, from a root-only file the root runner opens (D20). Its output goes to a `mktemp` file, which is replayed with `cat` because the runner has no pipefail. That output redirect can still fail before docker starts, an empty or unwritable path setting 1 with docker never run (the measured basis, bash), and what keeps such a run from pinging is D12's witness line, which only a CLI that ran prints. Every line the runner itself prints goes through the `log()` helper's parse shape, and the dead-man ping reads the replayed output for the CLI's witness line (D12).
The CLI lives in `cli/vintage/`, registered as `app.add_typer(vintage_app, name="vintage")` in `cli/__main__.py`. Its subcommands are `record`, `verify` (D15) and `derive` (D16). HTTP goes through stdlib `urllib` with an injectable `opener`, the idiom of `cli/liquidations/coinalyze.py:114`.
This corrects the brief's premise. The liquidations poller is a compose service the role renders and never starts, so the recorder takes the poller's CLI shape and the ops timers' runtime.
- Lost: a compose service with an in-process schedule, as the poller runs. Every start is attended, and the schedule is invisible to systemd, so a missed restart would be a silent, permanent loss.
- Lost: `ExecStart` as the `docker run` itself, with no runner (the minimal design). That leaves no root-only place to keep the dead-man URL.
- Lost: host cron. It has no `Persistent` catch-up, no unit for the journal keep-regex, and is not the fleet's pattern.
- Lost: an HTTP library. It would touch `pyproject.toml`, `uv.lock` and the image for what `urllib` already does.

## D3 — the fetch table is pinned in code, one URL per source id for ever, every free daily metric per asset

**The owner's rulings, 2026-11-11, verbatim.** On open question 2: Coin Metrics breadth (2): "Every free metric (Recommended)". On open question 4a: Coin Metrics licence, private copies before written permission (4a): "Record now, ask in writing (Recommended)" — store privately from day one and ask Coin Metrics for written permission before any C3 trial relies on the record.
The breadth, and recording Coin Metrics privately from the first tick, are the owner's rulings; the rest of this decision is marco's call. The CLI pins the table above in code (`cli/vintage/sources.py` in the suggested split): source id, exact URL, body file name and check. A changed URL is a new source id, never a redefinition, and an id is never reused. Requests carry `Accept-Encoding: identity` and a fixed `User-Agent: zcrypto-vintage/<package version>` that names no person, and never a conditional header. A BGeometrics request adds `Authorization: Bearer <key>` and nothing else (D20).
**Coin Metrics: one request per asset, with both `ignore_*` flags.** Each asset's request asks for every daily metric the catalog listed for it. The check (D8) fails any body from which a pinned metric is entirely absent, or in which a pinned metric froze or lost days it held before.
This reverses all three earlier designs, which refused `ignore_forbidden_errors`, and it does so by measurement:
- Without the flag, one forbidden pair turns the asset's whole request into a 403. That loses every metric of the asset at every tick until a code change ships through an ops re-pin.
- With the flag, the 200 carries every other metric.
- An unsupported pair is dropped silently with no flag at all.

Silence is the API's default either way, so only a presence check makes a lost metric loud. With the flag, the check pages while the other metrics' bytes keep flowing.
**Breadth.** The request asks for every daily metric, not only the six C3 names. A series not recorded today can never be recorded for today, and C3's features are not pre-registered. The cost is bytes, not requests: btc's 31 metrics are 8,341,605 raw bytes against 1,687,796 for the six.

- `dot`, whose MVRV, market cap and supply stopped in 2022. It is a cheap check that frozen history stays frozen, and its stopped metrics are D8's named exemptions from the freshness bound.
- `avaxc` and `avaxp`, Coin Metrics Labs experimental series and the only free AVAX valuation.

**Catalog and status feed.** The catalog and the status feed are recorded every tick, so coverage changes and recalculation notices sit in the record beside the bodies they explain.

## D4 — the exchange is stored as served, and judged second

**marco's call.**
**Storage.**
- The final attempt's body is stored as its raw bytes, whatever its HTTP status and whatever the check says, so a 403's or a 503's whole answer is kept as faithfully as a 200's history.
- An attempt that was retried keeps its first 4 KiB inline in its `attempts` entry, as `head`, the bytes in standard base64, so a reader decodes it and a byte scan of `tick.json` does not read it; the rest of its body is not kept.
- A body over D7's 64 MiB cap, or cut by the per-request or tick deadline, is not stored; its attempt records the reason and the byte count received.
- The bytes are gzipped at compresslevel 9 with mtime 0 and no file name, so the same body always yields the same file bytes.
- The sha256 and size of the raw bytes go in `tick.json`.
- Before publishing, each file is read back, decompressed and re-hashed. A mismatch refuses the tick with exit 2 and an ERROR.

**Exchange metadata.**
- Every response header is kept except `Set-Cookie`: an ordered list of pairs, duplicates kept.
- The record also keeps the final URL after redirects, the HTTP version, the status and the reason.

**Odd bodies.**
- A body served with a non-identity Content-Encoding is stored as served and graded as failing its checks; a `bg-*` body is stored only after D20's scan has read its decoded bytes.
- A non-2xx body is stored as its file like any other, and its source is graded not fetched (D8): the answer that broke a contract is kept whole.

**Modes.** Every file is created exclusive (`O_EXCL`, so an existing path is never truncated) with mode 0444. The tick directory is 0755 while it is assembled and 0555 from just after the rename that publishes it (D5); the date directories above it stay 0755. All are owned by `zcrypto-data`. Nothing parses a body to decide whether to store it.
- Lost: parsed rows or parquet. mempool's integers exceed 2^53, Coin Metrics numbers are strings, and `json` and `json_stream` treat nulls differently, so any re-serialisation changes the vintage. A parser bug found in a year would then be a year lost.
- Lost: storing an unchanged body as a reference to its hash, marco's earlier call. It never fires, because no body repeats between fetches, and it breaks the tick's self-containment.
- Lost: content-addressed `xz` blobs with one record file per exchange (the minimal design). `xz -6` is 6–26% smaller across the measurements above, 21% on the largest body (btc's 31 metrics: 1,390,008 against 1,750,182). But gzip reads with `zcat` on any host and with Python's stdlib without a second thought, and at about 7 GB a year on custody the bytes are not the constraint.
- Lost: delta compression against the previous tick. One corrupt file would poison every later one.
- Lost: a non-2xx body's first 4 KiB inline in `tick.json`, with no file (the earlier draft). It breaks the goal of every byte of the final answer, for bodies that are small and that the 64 MiB cap bounds anyway.

## D5 — the tick is one immutable directory, published by one rename, naming its predecessor

**marco's call.**
**Assembly and publication.**
- The tick stamp is the UTC start second, `YYYYMMDDTHHMMSSZ`.
- A tick is assembled under `staging/<stamp>/`, every file created exclusive and 0444 (D4), with `tick.json` written last.
- Each file and the directory are fsynced, then one `rename(2)` moves the directory to `record/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<stamp>/`, and the parent is fsynced. That rename goes through the tick-publish helper, one of the three renames D18 allows.
- The rename target must not exist. The check runs under the lock, so Python's `os.rename`, which has no no-replace flag, never meets one.
- Right after the rename, the tick directory is set to 0555, so a same-uid sibling container (the operating model) cannot add, rename or remove a file in it without a chmod first. A crash between the rename and the chmod leaves a published tick at 0755, and the next tick's scan sets it to 0555; a chmod changes no byte.

**Refusals and regressions.**
- A stamp whose target directory already exists (two runs in one second) is refused with exit 2 and an ERROR.
- A stamp at or below its predecessor's, a clock that stepped back or one corrected after a tick was published under a clock running ahead, is published with an ERROR naming both stamps. Publication order is the chain's, and the stamps are clock evidence like D6's, which the reader flags (step 9).
- A published directory is never opened for writing again.

A tick is published even when some or every source failed, because its attempt record is evidence.
**The chain.** `tick.json` names its predecessor in `prev`: the chain's head, the sha256 of its `tick.json` bytes, and `error`. The head is the published tick that no other tick's `prev` names, which D10's scan of every `tick.json` already finds. It is the newest stamp unless a regression was published. When a broken link leaves more than one candidate, the newest stamp among them is taken and an ERROR names the others. At an empty record, `prev` is null and `genesis_reason` says why.
**The chaining check.** Before computing `prev`, the recorder checks the head: its `tick.json` parses, its `fetches` name exactly the body files in its directory, and every listed body re-hashes to its entry. When the head fails, `prev` carries its stamp, the sha256 of its `tick.json` bytes as found (null when they cannot be read) and the failure in `error`, and an ERROR is logged; the tick is never refused for it, and a damaged head is never chained silently.
**The witness.** Each `tick.json`'s sha256, computed from the bytes written before the rename, is logged as `tick_json_sha256` in D9's witness line as soon as the rename returns, before the gauges and the re-verify, and repeated in the summary line. The ops journal keeps it, and so does Loki through the keep-regex (D17), for 90 days (`mon_retention_days` in `infra/ansible/roles/mon/defaults/main.yml`): outside both copies, so damage or a hand edit to a `tick.json` before its successor chains it cannot also rewrite the record of what was published. It is what a `tick.json` restore checks against (D15). A named hand start (D7) is the exception: it runs the runner from the operator's shell, outside the unit, so its lines print to that terminal alone, and the journal source's keep rule, keyed on the unit (`__journal__systemd_unit`, `infra/ansible/roles/ops/files/config.alloy`), ships none of them to Loki. Its witness is then the line the runner prints, which the runbook has the operator keep in that day's operations journal entry (`docs/reference/ops-journal/<YYYY-MM>.md`), outside both copies as the journal is; a hand-started tick whose line was not kept has its witness never logged (D15).
The chain lets `zcrypto vintage verify` (D15) detect, whatever the clock says, a tick lost after its successor chained it, in any copy. A head deleted before the next tick runs is chained around on ops; it leaves a fork in a copy that pulled it, which `verify` reports until code acknowledges it (D15), and its INFO line in the journal. The chain also gives a trial a one-hash pin (the reader section).
- Lost: a per-file rename into a shared directory, as `rolling extend` does. A pull or a reader could see half a tick.
- Lost: an appended JSONL manifest. `--ignore-existing` would freeze it on the NAS at the length it had when first pulled.
- Lost: a directory of per-exchange records named by their own hash (the minimal design). Every file is verifiable by its name, but a tick is no unit a reader can copy alone, and one exchange's attempts scatter.
- Lost: no chain. A tick missing from the NAS copy would read the same as a tick never taken.
- Lost: refusing on a broken `prev`. It would stop recording over a doubt that `verify` reports anyway.
- Lost: refusing a stamp at or below the newest (the earlier draft). One tick published under a clock running ahead, such as a boot catch-up before chrony has synchronised (`chrony-wait.service` is disabled on this host), would refuse every later tick until the clock passed its stamp, and no act the anti-goals allow could clear it, since a published tick is never deleted.
- Lost: files 0644 and directories 0755 after publication (the earlier draft). Six containers with the same uid mount the whole ops data root read-write (the operating model), so only their code kept them out.
- Lost: the successor's `prev` as the only witness of a `tick.json` (the earlier draft). It hashes whatever bytes were on disk when the next tick ran, so damage or a hand edit before then entered the chain, and the restore rule sent the operator to copy the damaged bytes over the other copy's good ones.

## D6 — the recorder's own fetch end is the stamp, the server's Date bounds knowability from below, and the host's clock is evidence

**marco's call.**
**Per attempt.** Each attempt records `started_at` and `ended_at`, UTC to the microsecond, plus its monotonic duration. `ended_at` is taken after the last body byte, and it is the observation's stamp.
**Knowable time.** An observation's knowable time is max(`ended_at`, the final response's server Date + 1 s), or `ended_at` alone where no Date header parses; the as-of view reads it (the reader section, step 6). A host clock running behind stamps a body earlier than it could have been known, and no stamp regression reveals it, so without this floor the as-of view would look ahead by the clock's error. The 1 s covers Date's resolution. A clock running ahead stays conservative, and a cached body's older Date leaves `ended_at` in force. The Date header stays inside the stored headers.
**Host clock evidence.** Each `tick.json` carries a `clock` block:
- `zcrypto_clock_offset_seconds` and `zcrypto_clock_synchronised`, read from `/textfile/clock-offset.prom`, which the ops `zcrypto-clock-offset` timer writes every 5 minutes;
- that file's age.

A missing or unparseable file is recorded as nulls with a reason, never a refusal, and so is a non-finite offset, which the writer publishes as `NaN` when it cannot read chrony (`infra/ansible/roles/ops/files/zcrypto-clock-offset.sh`), so `tick.json` never carries a token a strict JSON reader refuses.
**AssetEODCompletionTime** is stored like every other metric and is never read as a publication time.
- Lost: `started_at` as the stamp. A body is not knowable before it has arrived; btc's body is 1.7 MB.
- Lost: Last-Modified. No source sets a meaningful one.
- Lost: `AssetEODCompletionTime`. It is a last-calculated time, and it was itself seen restated.
- Lost: the server's Date as the stamp. It has 1 s resolution, and a cached body's Date can be up to its max-age old (mempool's is 120 s).
- Lost: `ended_at` alone as the as-of time (the earlier draft). A tick under a clock running behind, such as a boot tick before chrony has synchronised, put a value into the view before it was knowable, and the reader only flagged it.
- Lost: refusing on a clock doubt. That loses an unrecoverable fetch over a doubt a reader can resolve later, and the ops clock rule already pages the cause.
- Lost: an NTP query from inside the container. It duplicates chrony and adds a network dependency to every tick.

## D7 — politeness and bounds: every tick ends

**marco's call.**
**Pacing.** Requests run in sequence, at least 1 s apart per host, with at most 3 attempts per request.
**Retries.**
- Only transport errors, timeouts, 429 and 5xx are retried.
- Wait for Retry-After (seconds or HTTP-date), capped at 300 s; without one, wait 30 s, then 120 s.
- No other 4xx is ever retried: a 403 or 404 is a contract change.

**Per-request bounds.** A 30 s socket timeout, and a 120 s deadline on each attempt from its dial, since `urllib`'s timeout bounds one receive and a server trickling bytes would otherwise hold the tick open. The deadline never waits for a call to return: an interval timer (`signal.setitimer(signal.ITIMER_REAL, …)`, whose `SIGALRM` handler raises), armed as the attempt dials for the time left to the earlier of this deadline and the tick deadline, and disarmed once the attempt's last byte is read, ends the attempt wherever it blocks on its socket. Each socket step an attempt takes is bounded, one connect or one receive at a time, by the socket timeout alone, so every one needs the timer: the connect, the TLS handshake, the status line and headers `urlopen` reads, a chunked answer's chunk-size lines and their extensions, each chunk's closing CRLF and the trailer lines, which `http.client` reads by `readline` inside `read1` with no limit on the trailer's line count, and the body's data. Name resolution is outside it: `getaddrinfo` runs in C, Python runs a handler only once that returns, and the resolver's own timeout bounds it, not measured here. The body's data is read by `read1`, which returns once data bytes arrive, and the size cap and both deadlines are checked between those chunks as well. Measured on this host's 3.14.6 over loopback, the timer armed at 1 s ended at 1.00 s an attempt trickling its status line, its headers, a chunk-size line or its trailer, over HTTP and over TLS, and a TLS handshake that never answered; and a process that is PID 1 in its own PID namespace, as the CLI is in its container, took the timer's signal at 1.00 s. Python installs a signal handler from the main thread alone and runs it there, so the fetch runs in the CLI's main thread.
**Breaker.** A request that has used up its attempts trips a breaker for the rest of the tick, and the later requests behind it are marked not fetched without dialling. Its last attempt decides which. A transport error or a timeout, before any status, trips its host's: DNS, TCP and TLS fail for every path on it. A 5xx or a 429 trips its endpoint's alone, the scheme, host and path of its URL, the query aside: such an answer can belong to one endpoint while the host serves the others. `community-api.coinmetrics.io` serves two, the catalog's `/v4/catalog-v2/asset-metrics` and the ten assets' `/v4/timeseries/asset-metrics`, so a fault in the catalog alone costs the catalog alone, and a 5xx tied to one asset's query costs the assets after it in the tick.
**Order.** After BGeometrics' four (D20), the sources whose host serves one request each, `fng`, `cm-status` and `mempool-hashrate`, run before `community-api.coinmetrics.io`'s eleven, the ten assets first and `cm-catalog` last. Each endpoint that answers 5xx or 429 to every attempt spends one request's attempts and waits, up to 600 s under a capped Retry-After, so an outage of that whole host, two endpoints, can reach the tick deadline; it reaches it after every other host has answered. `community-api.coinmetrics.io` is the one host under these rules that serves more than one endpoint (`api.bitcoin-data.com`'s four paths take D20's rules), so it is the one place the order decides which endpoint a fault on the other can cost: after another host's fault of about 600 s in the same tick, one endpoint's 600 s leaves the endpoint after it too little of the deadline. The catalog runs after: it is no series (D16), and no request in the tick reads it, every asset's URL being pinned (D3). So a catalog fault never costs an asset, and an asset query's fault beside another host's can leave the catalog `tick deadline`.
**Named sources by hand.** `zcrypto vintage record` takes a repeatable `--source <id>`, and the runner hands the CLI its own arguments after its pinned ones (D1). A tick given ids dials those sources alone, in table order, under the same breakers and tick deadline; every other source reads `not selected`, outcome 1, with no request, so the tick still holds every source's fetch. An id not in the table refuses before any fetch, exit 2 (D9). The runner runs from the operator's shell, outside the unit, so its witness and its ERROR lines print to that terminal, page nothing, and are kept as D5's witness says. It records what a plain hand start cannot: a 5xx or 429 tied to one asset's query trips the timeseries endpoint for the assets after it at every tick and every hand start while it lasts, and a hand start naming those assets dials them and never the failing query; and a provider whose fault uses up the tick and has not answered again leaves the sources after it at `tick deadline`, where a plain hand start dials that provider first and meets the same fault, and a hand start naming the sources still owed, less that provider's own, records them (the runbook).
**Tick deadline.** At 20 minutes, the remaining sources are marked not fetched and the tick is published. A wait before a dial is cut at the deadline, never skipped for its length, so a request whose wait reaches it reads `tick deadline`.
**Size cap.** 64 MiB per response, about eight times the largest measured body (btc's 8.3 MB).
**BGeometrics.** `api.bitcoin-data.com` takes D20's rules in place of the retries and the breaker above, because every request spends a budget of 15 a day: its four requests run first in the tick; at most one retry per tick across the host, only after a transport error, a timeout or a 5xx, and after 30 s; a 429, 401 or 403 is never retried and trips the host's breaker; a response whose `X-RateLimit-Remaining-Day` or `X-RateLimit-Remaining-Hour` reads 0 stops the host's remaining requests; and a keyed request follows no redirect.
- Lost: retrying until success. A oneshot that never ends blocks the next tick and looks like a hang.
- Lost: a breaker tripped by transport errors alone (the earlier draft). The sources run in sequence under one tick deadline, so a host answering every request with a 503 spent each request's attempts and waits, up to 600 s of waits a request under a capped Retry-After, and left every later source on other hosts `tick deadline`, a hand start included.
- Lost: a 5xx or 429 tripping the whole host (an earlier draft). A 5xx can belong to one endpoint: a catalog fault alone cost the ten assets behind it at every tick and every hand start while it lasted, though the timeseries endpoint answered.
- Lost: `cm-catalog` before the ten assets (the draft before this one). A catalog fault beside another host's fault of about 600 s in the same tick ran the deadline out before `cm-btc` was dialled, and cost the ten assets though their endpoint answered.
- Lost: no hand start of named sources (the draft before this one). A fault tied to one asset's query cost the assets after it at every hand start too, so the runbook's one remedy could not record their day.
- Lost: `cm-status` and `mempool-hashrate` after `community-api.coinmetrics.io`'s sources (the earlier order). With a breaker per endpoint, an outage of that host spends two requests' attempts and waits, past the 1,200 s deadline, and left both `tick deadline`.
- Lost: parallel requests. They break Coin Metrics' per-IP limit, and that IP is also this workstation's.
- Lost: a unit `TimeoutStartSec` as the only bound. Killing the docker client leaves the container running, and the next tick then fails on the name conflict.
- Lost: the deadline checked between `read1` calls alone (the earlier draft). `http.client` reads a chunked answer's framing inside one `read1`, and `urlopen` reads the status line and headers before any, so a server trickling those bytes held one call, and the tick, open past both deadlines.
- Lost: a watchdog thread that shuts the response's socket at the deadline. `urlopen` holds the socket until it returns, so the thread could not reach the status line, the headers or the handshake.
- Lost: budgeting to the `x-ratelimit-*` headers' 6000 per 20 s on a "download" plan, which disagree with the documentation.

## D8 — the check is lenient, structural, and never gates storage

**marco's call.** A source's outcome is 0 (ok), 1 (not fetched: a transport error, a non-2xx status whose body D4 still stores, oversize, deadline or a tripped breaker, a source a hand start did not name (`not selected`, D7), and, for BGeometrics alone, no credential, an exhausted budget, or a body that echoed the key or that the scan could not read, D20) or 2 (a 2xx body fetched and stored, but the check failed). A source is ok when it answered 2xx with identity encoding and its body passes its check.

| Source | Check | Newest-data bound |
|---|---|---|
| `fng` | `data` is a list of objects with `value` and `timestamp`, and `metadata.error` is null | newest row no older than the tick's UTC date − 1 |
| `cm-<asset>` | every line is a JSON object with `asset` and `time`, every pinned metric appears in at least one row, and every pinned metric passes the per-metric rules below | newest row no older than date − 1; per metric, the rules below |
| `cm-catalog` | an object whose `data` list is non-empty and that carries no `next_page_url`, so a catalog that starts paginating is never stored as if page one were the whole | none |
| `cm-status` | the bytes open with an XML or feed prolog and contain `<entry`; no XML parser runs, so there is no entity-expansion surface | none |
| `mempool-hashrate` | `hashrates` is a non-empty list | newest row no older than date − 3 |
| `bg-<metric>` | a non-empty JSON array of objects, each carrying `d`, `unixTs` and the source's pinned value key (`sopr`, `asopr`, `sthSopr`, `lthSopr`); the newest `d` not earlier than in the last ok body; the oldest `d` exempt from never-shorter-at-the-bottom, since the 4-year window moves forward a day each day | newest `d` no older than date − 9: the free plan's 7-day hold, measured at date − 7, plus two days for an update that runs late after 03:00 UTC (D20) |

**Per metric, for `cm-<asset>`.** The check tracks, for each pinned metric, the newest and the oldest day on which it carries a value, and grades the body 2 when any of these rules fails:
- **Freshness.** The metric's newest day is no older than the tick's UTC date − 3. That absorbs the worst completion measured for the MVRV coins, btc's 90-day maximum of 43.25 h, at a 07:37 tick. Every `avaxc` and `avaxp` metric (Coin Metrics Labs experimental series) is held to date − 30 instead, about four times avaxc's completion p90 of 179 h, checked against the plan's recorded bodies, so a frozen AVAX series still grades 2. One named exemption carries no freshness bound: the metrics whose newest day in the bodies the plan's session records is more than a year old, `dot`'s stopped MVRV, market cap and supply family and `eth`'s `HashRate` and `SplyExpFut10yr`, whose newest day in those bodies is 2022-09-14, which the plan names from those bytes.
- **Never shorter at the top.** The metric's newest day is not earlier than in the source's last ok body. Exempt metrics are held to this too, so a frozen series may not shrink.
- **Never shorter at the bottom.** The metric's oldest day is not later than in the last ok body. The rolling-window `ReferenceRate*` metrics, which keep 7 points by design, are exempt.

The source-level bound is date − 1, because `PriceUSD` fills each row early (the btc row for 2026-11-10 carried it at 00:27:13Z on 11-11). For the full-history sources (`fng`, `cm-<asset>`, `mempool-hashrate`, `bg-<metric>`), the row count must also not fall below 90% of the last ok body of the same source.
A `json_stream` body carries no row count and no trailer, and over HTTP/1.1 it arrives chunked with no Content-Length, so a stream the server ends cleanly after a backend fault passes every line check; the per-metric rules are what catch it.
The outcome, its reason, the newest day, the row count, any absent metric and any metric that failed a per-metric rule go into `tick.json`. A failure is source-level when the whole body fails: a line or structure check, the source-level newest-day bound, a collapse or a non-identity encoding; every `fng`, `mempool-hashrate` and `bg-<metric>` failure is. A failure is per-metric when the check names the metric in `metrics_absent` or `metrics_failed`. The reader reads absences by that split (the reader section, step 2).
**Acknowledgment.** A failure stands, and pages, until code acknowledges it with a pinned entry in `cli/vintage/sources.py`: (source, metric, or `*` for a source-level rule, rule, the stamp of the tick from which it holds). From that tick on, the rule is not applied to that metric, or, for a rule that compares with the last ok body, the comparison restarts at the first body from that tick. The check names the entry it applied in `tick.json`, and the entry ships through an ops re-pin. The named exemptions above take the same form, holding from genesis.
- Lost: a strict schema check. Cosmetic vendor changes would page.
- Lost: "a 200 is ok". A redirect to an HTML page, a frozen series, a history quietly capped by new pagination or a revoked metric would read green for ever.
- Lost: comparing the row count with the previous stored body. A truncation would fail one tick and then read as normal; comparing with the last ok body keeps it failing, and paging, until code acknowledges it.
- Lost: one newest-row bound per source at date − 3 (the earlier draft). The newest row is `PriceUSD`'s, so a frozen network metric graded ok for ever, as `dot`'s 2022 stop shows, and a body that lost its newest one or two days, or up to a tenth of its oldest rows, graded ok.
- Lost: a per-metric bound at date − 1. btc's completion reached 43.25 h in 90 days, so an ordinary late day would grade a 07:37 tick 2.
- Lost: the date − 3 freshness bound on `avaxc` and `avaxp`. Their known completion lag would page as a fault.
- Lost: no freshness bound on `avaxc` and `avaxp` (the earlier draft). A frozen AVAX series graded ok for ever, the failure this check exists to catch; never-shorter-at-the-top catches a shrink, not a freeze.
- Lost: leaving "until code acknowledges it" unnamed (the earlier draft). The plan could neither implement nor test the end of a failing stretch.

## D9 — outcomes, exit codes and log levels

**marco's call.**
**Exit codes.** `zcrypto vintage record` catches every exception at its top level, and its exit code says whether a tick was published:
- 0 when it published the tick, every source is ok, the gauges were written and the tick's re-verify (D15) was clean;
- 1 only when it published the tick and something beside it is not ok: a source, the gauges or the re-verify;
- 2 whenever it published nothing. That covers every refusal (an unknown `--source` id, a held lock, a target that already exists, an unwritable staging or record directory, a read-back mismatch) and every exception raised before the publishing rename returned.

Python's own exit status for an uncaught exception is 1, the code that means "published", so no exception may leave the CLI uncaught, and D12's dead-man, pinged on 0 or 1, then means a published tick and nothing else.
**The lock's message.** `single_instance_lock` raises `CaptureError` on contention, with a message that names capture and `systemctl stop zcrypto-capture` (`cli/capture/command.py:56-59`), a unit `zcrypto-ops` does not have. The CLI catches it and logs its own refusal instead, naming `zcrypto-vintage-record.service` and the `docker ps -a --filter name=zcrypto-vintage-record` read, and exits 2. A lock the helper cannot take for any other reason (a read-only filesystem, no flock support) runs unlocked, named by the ERROR the helper logs itself (`cli/capture/command.py:61-65`).

**Log levels.**
- WARNING for each source that is not ok, naming the source, its outcome and the reason, because the not-recorded rule (D11) owns that event.
- ERROR for every refusal, a head that fails the chaining check (D5), a stamp regression published (D5), an unreadable `tick.json` met while scanning, a re-verify finding, and every unexpected exception. Those persist into the next tick and page now through `Ops · ERROR logs`, whose selector already admits `zcrypto-.*`.
- INFO for the witness line, `vintage tick published tick=… tick_json_sha256=…`, logged as soon as the rename returns and before the gauges and the re-verify, its hash computed from the bytes written before the rename: the tick's witness (D5).
- INFO for one summary line: `vintage record complete tick=… tick_json_sha256=… ok=… not_fetched=… failed_checks=… bytes=…`, which repeats the witness.

Every line goes through `cli.logging`, with no `typer.echo`. No line carries a request header, and an exception from the BGeometrics request path is logged by its class name alone; its class name is also all that the attempt's `error` in `tick.json` carries of it, and the top-level handler's ERROR carries that and the innermost frame's file, line and function, which name code and never the exception's text (D20).
- Lost: ERROR for each failed source. Every transient 503 would page twice, through the ERROR rule and later through staleness.
- Lost: failing the whole tick when one source fails. A partial failure must never cost the healthy sources.
- Lost: WARNING for refusals, as verified-replay logs them. A refusal repeats every tick until someone acts, and the staleness rule would take 25 h to say so.
- Lost: 1 for "a source failed" with exceptions left to Python (the earlier draft). An exception before publication then exits 1 too, and the dead-man stays green while no tick is published, exactly when Grafana's path may be dark.
- Lost: the imported lock's message as it stands. It would page an operator towards a capture unit that does not exist on this host.

## D10 — the gauges are a function of the record

**marco's call.** After publishing, the CLI scans every `tick.json` under `record/` (730 a year) and orders the ticks along the chain (D5). It then writes `/textfile/vintage.prom` atomically through the `.prom` publish helper, the second of the three renames D18 allows: a temporary file from `mkstemp`, `chmod 0644` (the collector is not root, and `mkstemp`'s 0600 would be unreadable), then a rename over the old file. Five families, labelled by `source`:
- `zcrypto_vintage_last_ok_timestamp_seconds`: `ended_at` of the source's latest ok fetch along the chain, capped at 19:57 UTC of that fetch's UTC day, 0 if there has never been one. Along the chain, not by the largest stamp, so a tick published under a clock running ahead cannot hold the gauge in the future and silence the rule. The cap is D11's timing fix: a fetch that ends after 19:57, a late hand start's or a boot tick's, reads as 19:57, so a late hand start never carries the next day's page past that day's midnight. It moves where the rule's 25 hours count from, for those fetches alone: a 23:30 fetch's age counts from 19:57, so the rule can page about 21 h after that fetch (D11). 19:57 is the evening slot plus D7's 20-minute tick deadline. A tick taken at its slot can start about a minute after 19:37:00, through systemd's default one-minute timer accuracy (`AccuracyUSec=1min`, read on `zcrypto-archive-pull.timer`; no ops timer template sets `AccuracySec`) and the container's start, so such a tick's fetch can end about a minute after 19:57 and read as 19:57; the cap moves that reading by under a minute, and otherwise changes only the later readings. It is a constant beside the tick deadline, held by a test to the timer's evening `OnCalendar` plus that deadline.
- `zcrypto_vintage_outcome`: this tick's 0, 1 or 2.
- `zcrypto_vintage_body_bytes`: the raw size of the body this tick stored for the source, whatever its status, or 0.
- `zcrypto_vintage_days_missing`: UTC days, from the source's first tick through yesterday, with no ok fetch. A day whose only bodies graded outcome 2 counts: its bytes are stored, but none was good.
- `zcrypto_vintage_changed_values`: for `fng`, `cm-<asset>`, `mempool-hashrate` and `bg-<metric>`, the two-body reading of the reader section's step 7, between the source's previous ok body P and this tick's ok body N, read from those two bodies alone, per series S. It counts each (S, D) where both hold D with different tokens (a restatement); where P holds D and N does not, D lying inside the range of days N holds for S (a retraction); and where N holds D and P does not, D lying at or before S's newest day in P (a backfill, or a value restored after a retraction, changed or not, since telling a restoration from a restatement needs the last non-null token, which two bodies do not hold). A day after S's newest day in P is a first publication or a completion and does not count. Only ok bodies are compared, so an outcome-2 body's absences never count, and a value withheld in outcome-2 bodies and restored changed counts once, as a restatement, when the next ok body arrives. The family is computed by D16's code. Its line is left out for a source whose body this tick is not ok, the panel showing the gap, and in any tick where either body fails to parse (a WARNING names it). Restatement becomes a series on the dashboard from the second tick.

An unreadable `tick.json` met in the scan counts as no ok fetch, and the CLI logs ERROR naming the file. A refusal (exit 2) writes no `.prom`. Every family is charted on a new row of `infra/grafana/data-integrity-dashboard.json` (uid `zcrypto-integrity`).
- Lost: the runner parsing a summary line and carrying values forward, as tape-bars does. That is eighteen labelled sources pushed through `sed`, and a wording drift would read as green.
- Lost: a state file of last-ok times. It can disagree with the record, and deleting it would reset the hole count.
- Lost: a runner `.prom` with an exit code and a last-run time (the operator design). Two more families for what D11, the journal and `ops-postverify.sh`'s freshness read already show.
- Lost: a count of failed ticks today (the point-in-time design). It resets at UTC midnight, so a lost day stops paging; D11 pages before the day is lost without a series that resets.
- Lost: counting every value that appears where there was none as a completion (the earlier draft). A value retracted in one ok body and restored with a different value in the next was counted once, as a retraction, and never as the change it carries.
- Lost: comparing each tick with every earlier observation of (S, D). It would parse every stored body each tick; two ok bodies per source bound the cost, and the derivation answers the full history (D16).

## D15 — integrity and arrival are proven by one read, `zcrypto vintage verify`, run on both copies on a schedule, and repaired only by a hash-checked restore

**marco's call**, beside one cost the owner accepted: open question 3 named a verify of a twenty-fourth of the NAS copy every loop among the custody channel's costs, and the owner ruled for that channel (D14). `zcrypto vintage verify --root <record root>` is read-only:
- it reads every `tick.json` and walks the chain from genesis to the head;
- it re-hashes bodies' raw bytes against their entries: every body by default, or, with `--newest N --slice K --slices M`, the bodies of the newest N ticks along the chain from its head (D5), never by stamp, so a tick published after a regression is among them, and of every tick whose position in stamp order is K modulo M;
- it reports, by path, the first broken link, a missing tick, a missing or tampered body, an unreadable manifest, a fork (two ticks naming one predecessor, both named: the trace a head deleted before its successor ran leaves in a copy that pulled it), and a file in a tick directory that its `tick.json` does not list, rsync's temporary `.<name>.XXXXXX` among them;
- it names a stamp at or below its predecessor's as a clock regression (D5), which is not a break;
- it names, and does not count as a break, a link whose successor's `prev` carries the chaining check's `error` (D5), and a link code acknowledges (the restore below), whose predecessor's `tick.json` it hashes against the acknowledged sha256; the predecessor's bodies are re-hashed either way; and, the same way, a fork code acknowledges (the restore below), whose tick off the chain it hashes against the acknowledged sha256, that tick's bodies re-hashed as any tick's; an entry, link or fork, whose named tick the copy does not hold (a link's successor, a fork's tick off the chain) is passed over, neither a break nor a note, since every entry ships to both copies, while the ops copy never holds a fork's tick off the chain and a copy taken before a successor arrived does not hold that link's successor, save one case: run with `--custody`, as every read of the NAS copy runs it (the loop, the rollout's arrival proof, the runbook's integrity read and the read after a restore) and no read of the ops copy does, a fork entry whose tick off the chain the copy does not hold is a `missing tick` break, since that copy is the tick's only custody and the old generation's heads through it are rebuilt from it (D16's `--through`), or, where the entry is marked lost (the restore below), a `lost tick` note naming it, the mark changing nothing over a copy that holds the tick; the derive applies a link entry only at the tick it names, so it reads no entry for a tick it does not derive;
- it verifies an empty record clean, and reports a root that does not exist as a break, never as an empty record;
- it ends with one summary line naming the ticks it read, the head and its `tick.json` sha256, and the bodies it re-hashed, so an arrival read compares the head by value, never by the exit code alone, which an empty or not-yet-pulled copy also gives;
- it exits 0 when clean and 1 otherwise.

**Where it runs.**
- **Every ops tick.** After the publish and the `.prom`, the record step runs the same check over `/data/record`, with the newest 4 ticks and the slice of 14 that the count of published ticks names, so each ops body is re-hashed within a week. A finding is an ERROR naming the path, and the tick exits 1 (D9).
- **Every NAS loop.** Right after the vintages rsync: `zcrypto vintage verify --root "$VINTAGES_DEST" --custody --newest 4 --slice "$slice" --slices 24`, `$slice` being the loop's existing 1/24 counter (`pull-entrypoint.sh`, spec 00102 D3), so the newest four ticks are hashed at once, and every other tick, a catch-up pull's older arrivals among them, within 24 loops. A finding logs `vintages verify failed (dest=…), continuing` at ERROR. This runs the archive-pull container's own `zcrypto`, so `nas_capture_image` must first move to a `-compat` build that carries the subcommand (the deployment order).
- **By hand, whole.** On the ops copy and, from the workstation, on the NAS copy in place with `--custody` (above): the rollout's arrival proof and the runbook's integrity read.

**Restore.** A finding is repaired only by hand, from the other copy, or, for a fork's tick off the chain, which the ops copy never holds, from any copy that holds it (below), and only with the published bytes. For a body, they are the bytes whose sha256 equals the one a verified `tick.json` records for it. For a `tick.json`, they are the bytes whose sha256 equals its witness, the `tick_json_sha256` its INFO line logged at publish (D5); the successor's `prev` stands in for the witness only where the two agree or, the witness past Loki's 90 days or never logged (a run killed between the rename and the witness line, or a named hand start whose printed line was not kept, D5), where both copies verified that link clean before the damage. Where the successor's `prev` and the witness disagree, damage or an edit the chaining check could not see entered the chain: the witness names the published bytes and `prev` the damaged ones, and which copy gets which bytes is the owner's decision against the witness, never a copy of whichever bytes match `prev`. That link then stays broken in every copy, since the successor's `tick.json` is never rewritten, so code acknowledges it in the form D8's acknowledgment takes: a pinned (successor stamp, the `prev` hash it recorded, the witnessed hash) entry in `cli/vintage/`, against which `verify` hashes the restored `tick.json` and names the link; the entry ships through an ops re-pin and a NAS re-pin, and with a new `DERIVE_GENERATION`, since a generation of the versions table applies only the entries the code pins for it (D16). A link whose `prev` carries the chaining check's `error` needs no entry: its predecessor is restored against the witness. A fork has no restore: its tick off the chain is a published tick the next tick chained around, and no published byte is removed (the anti-goals). It arises in a copy that pulled a head deleted on ops before the next tick ran, the NAS copy or a research copy taken from it, where `verify` reads it as a break at every run; on ops it would arise only from a restore of that head there, which the runbook never makes, since the ops copy verifies clean without it and the restored tick would stand as a second candidate head that every later tick names in an ERROR (D5). So code acknowledges the fork in the same form: a pinned (off-chain stamp, its `tick.json` sha256) entry in `cli/vintage/`, the sha256 checked against that tick's witness before it is pinned, against which `verify`, over a copy that holds the off-chain tick, hashes its `tick.json` and names the fork; the ops copy, which does not hold it, reads the entry as nothing (above). Where the off-chain tick has no witness (a run killed between the rename and the witness line, or a named hand start whose line was not kept, D5), or the copies that hold it disagree with its witness, the owner decides which sha256 to pin from the copies that hold it, as for a link whose witness and `prev` disagree. The entry ships through an ops re-pin and a NAS re-pin, with no new generation of its own, since the derive follows D5's chain, which leaves the off-chain tick out; a derive that took that head before it was deleted stops on the same fork, and that stop clears only with a new generation (D16). Once the entry has shipped, a NAS copy that loses the tick off the chain reads `missing tick` at every loop (above), and it clears one of two ways. The tick is put back on the NAS host from any copy that holds it (a research copy taken from the NAS), its `tick.json` hashed against the entry's `tick_json_sha256` and each body against that `tick.json` before it is placed. Where no copy holds it, the owner decides a code change marking the entry lost (`lost: bool = False` on the entry, set true), shipped through an ops re-pin and a NAS re-pin as the entry was, which `--custody` then reads as a `lost tick` note; the old generation's catalog line then names its heads through that tick as rebuildable by no copy (D16). Until the tick is back or the mark's NAS re-pin lands, the runbook's daily by-channel read of the archive-pull ERROR lines carries it, as it carries a fork awaiting its entry. The bytes put back are the published bytes, so a restore is not a rewrite (the anti-goals). The runbook section (Observability) gives the commands for each copy: on ops, the tick directory is made writable for the copy and set back to 0555.
Path drift between the role's `rrsync` root and the CLI's `record/` is held by a converge-guard test.
- Lost: verify only at rollout and by hand (the earlier draft). Nobody is scheduled to run it, a file damaged as it lands on the NAS stays damaged under `--ignore-existing`, and the other copy may be gone by the time a reader looks.
- Lost: no repair path. A damaged copy would fail `verify` and log ERROR every tick for ever, and readers would learn to step past a red `verify`, which the reader section's step 1 forbids.
- Lost: a fork with no acknowledgment (the earlier draft). No restore clears it, so the NAS copy that pulled a head deleted on ops before its successor ran failed `verify` at every loop for good, and its ERROR stood inside `zcrypto-nas-archive-pull-errors` beside the custody channels' own.
- Lost: a whole re-hash on every NAS loop. The Atom already stretched the hourly loop to about 103 minutes (the loop's own comment), and the slice bounds the cost to a twenty-fourth of the record per loop.
- Lost: a hand `sha256sum` of one tick (the operator design). It compares one tick, while `verify` proves a whole copy.
- Lost: a `verify` that read neither forks nor unlisted files and took `--newest` by stamp (the earlier draft). A head deleted before its successor ran left the NAS copy's fork unreported, a pull killed mid-file left its temporary file unreported, and after a published regression `--newest` skipped the tick just copied.
- Lost: a runner-side check of the hub copy through the automount, with a hub-lag gauge and rule. It adds an NFS read to the tick path for failures the NAS ERROR rule and dead-man already page.
- Lost: a daily-pass row reading the hub copy. Explicit drop: it touches `.claude/skills/zcrypto-daily-ops/SKILL.md`, a Fable-floor path, for one read `verify` already gives.

**The owner's ruling, 2026-11-11, on open question 13, verbatim:** BGeometrics' terms and the derived dataset: "Record and derive" — AGAINST the recommendation: store privately and also include BGeometrics in the derived dataset now, accepting the derivative-works risk for private research use.

Three rulings, each against marco's recommendation, set this decision. The derived dataset is materialised and cataloged in this change, not left to the first C3 trial spec (8). It is a table on the ops node updated after each recorder run, not frozen sets minted by hand at a named tick (15). The `bg-*` series are in it from its first run, the owner accepting, in the ruling's words, "the derivative-works risk for private research use" (13). The table's form below keeps the project's rule for a dataset a trial cites, immutable and pinned by hash (§8, line 243); that form, and everything else the rulings do not name, is marco's call.

**The derivation.** `cli/vintage/derive.py` holds pure functions from a record's ticks to observations and versions, by the rules in the reader section:
- each (series, day) keyed on the parsed UTC day;
- each new token judged against the last non-null token seen for that (series, day), and a first publication against the series' high-water day;
- inherited history set per series;
- an outcome-2 body's absences taken as no evidence for the series its check names, or for every series when the body failed at source level;
- Q3's lower end read from any earlier observation that is evidence D was not yet held;
- the as-of view read from the versions at each observation's knowable time.

The recorder uses it for D10's `changed_values` alone and never reads the table; the derive builds the table with it. In tests it is proven on fixtures to answer the three questions and the as-of view with no look-ahead.

**What runs the derive, and why the record never waits on it.**
- `zcrypto vintage derive --record <record root> --table <table root> [--textfile <path>]` is the table's one writer. On ops it runs in its own oneshot unit, `zcrypto-vintage-derive.service`, whose root runner, `/usr/local/sbin/zcrypto-vintage-derive` (0700, holding no secret), runs one attached `docker run --rm --pull never --name zcrypto-vintage-derive --user "{{ ops_uid }}:{{ ops_gid }}" --network none -v "{{ ops_data_dir }}/{{ ops_vintages_subdir }}/record:/vintages/record:ro" -v "{{ ops_data_dir }}/{{ ops_vintage_versions_subdir }}:/versions" -v "{{ ops_textfile_dir }}:/textfile" --entrypoint zcrypto "{{ ops_image }}@{{ ops_image_digest }}" vintage derive --record /vintages/record --table /versions --textfile /textfile/vintage-derive.prom`. It passes no `-e`, no `--env-file` and no stdin, and the container has no key and no network. The record is mounted at `/vintages/record`, the host's own shape, never at `/record`: the table-root refusal below reads paths as the process sees them, and a record root at `/record` would make `/` its parent and refuse every table root.
- The recorder's unit carries `Wants=zcrypto-vintage-derive.service`; the derive unit carries `After=zcrypto-vintage-record.service` and no `[Install]` (D1, the layout). So every start of the recorder's unit, whether a timer slot, a boot catch-up or a hand start, is followed by one derive, whatever the recorder's exit, and the derive starts only once the recorder's run has ended. A hand start of named sources (D7) runs the runner outside the unit, so the runbook starts the derive unit after it. The converge never starts it, as it never starts the recorder (D17).
- So the record never waits on the derive. The tick is published and the dead-man pinged on the recorder's own outcome (D12) before the derive begins. The derive mounts the record read-only and has no network. A derive that fails, hangs or runs for hours leaves the recorder's unit inactive, so the next timer slot starts it afresh. A second container in the recorder's runner would not: a slow derive would hold the oneshot unit through the next slot, which joins the running job and records nothing (the operating model).
- The derive holds `single_instance_lock` on its table root for its run. On contention it logs its own refusal naming `zcrypto-vintage-derive.service`, as D9 does for the recorder, ends with the stop line below, `at=none reason=lock held`, and exits 1; a second `docker run` under the stable name fails in docker, before the CLI starts, with docker's own exit (D12).

**The table: append-only parts and a head log.** The table root holds `staging/` and one directory per generation, `gen-<N>/`, each written by one value of `DERIVE_GENERATION`:
- **The generation**, opened by one rename. The derive assembles `generation.json` and empty `parts/` and `heads/` under `staging/gen-<N>.<run start second>.<8 random hex>/`, `generation.json` created exclusive, fsynced and 0444, and publishes the directory as `gen-<N>/` through the table-publish helper after a check that the target does not exist, then sets `gen-<N>/` to 0555. `generation.json` names the generation, the D15 entries the code pins for it, the frame hash's version (below) and the image revision that opened it. A torn write is then a staging leftover, never a generation.
- **One part per tick** on the record's chain, in chain order, at `gen-<N>/parts/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<stamp>/`. It holds `versions.parquet` (the versions first seen at that tick), `coverage.parquet` (that tick's observations and its outcome-1 fetches) and `part.json`. `part.json` names the tick's stamp, its `tick.json` sha256 and its chain position, and gives each frame's content hash (below) and row count. It also lists the facts that tick taught about an earlier one: the predecessor of a stamp regression, which may have run under a clock running ahead (step 9), and a link whose `prev` carries the chaining check's `error` or that an entry acknowledges (D15). A part is assembled under `staging/<stamp>.<run start second>.<8 random hex>.part/`, created exclusive, with `part.json` written last and every file fsynced and 0444. It is published by one rename after a check that its target does not exist, then set to 0555.
- **One head per part**, at `gen-<N>/heads/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>/<stamp>/head.json`: one canonical JSON line with sorted keys and a trailing newline. It names the generation, the sha256 of the generation's D15 entries as the code pins them, the frame hash's version, the tick and its `tick.json` sha256, the chain position, the part's path and the sha256 of its `part.json` bytes, and the rows through it by frame and by kind. It carries `prev`, the sha256 of the previous head's bytes, and `prev_tick`, the previous head's tick stamp, both null at genesis, and `parts_sha256`, the sha256 of the previous head's `parts_sha256` followed by this part's `part.json` sha256 (64 zeros before genesis). So a head commits to every part through it, in order, in a line of fixed size, and names where its predecessor lies. It is staged in a directory of its own, `staging/<stamp>.<run start second>.<8 random hex>.head/`, the file created exclusive, fsynced and 0444; that directory is renamed into place after the part, so that no head names a missing part, and set to 0555, so a same-uid process can neither unlink nor replace the head without a chmod first, as with a tick (D5). The heads directory is the append-only head log: one head a tick, each written once, as `ohlc-rolling`'s index lines are (`index/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>.json`, `cli/data/rolling.py:89-90`), each in a directory of its own as a tick is.
- **A head's identity** is the sha256 of its file's bytes. It commits through `prev` to every earlier head, through `parts_sha256` and each `part.json` to the content of every frame through it, through the entries' sha256 to the D15 entries it applied, and through its tick's `tick.json` sha256 to the record. No head or part carries a write time or a code ref, so neither moves a hash; `generation.json`'s image revision sits outside every hash, so a rebuild by another revision of the same generation reproduces every head.
- **The frame-content hash** is the table's own, in `cli/vintage/`, not `dataset_hash` (`cli/ohlc/dataset.py:55-57`), which hashes polars' `write_csv` output, so that a polars upgrade rendering a value differently would refuse every earlier head. It is the sha256 of canonical JSON lines: the column names first, then one line per row, each value rendered by the derive's own code from the Python value polars returns (a Datetime as ISO 8601 with microseconds and `Z`, a Date as ISO 8601, an integer in decimal, a boolean as `true` or `false`, a null as `null`, a string as JSON escapes it). The rendering is versioned: a head names its version, every version stays in code, and `read_table` applies the one a head names, so a later rendering never makes an earlier head unreadable.

**A pure function of the record, which decides.** The table through any head is a function of three inputs: the ticks on the record's chain through that head, `DERIVE_GENERATION`, and the D15 acknowledgment entries that generation applies.
- A part holds only what its tick showed first, judged against earlier ticks. A fact a later tick teaches about an earlier one goes into the later tick's `part.json`, never back into the earlier part. The reader joins it through the head it reads, so a head read today returns what was known at that head.
- A generation's D15 entries, wherever this decision and the catalog name them, are its link entries alone: a fork entry, which no derive reads (D15), belongs to no generation. They are pinned in code, in a per-generation map whose shipped entries are never edited, beside the head-pin map below. `generation.json` copies them when the generation opens, every head commits to their sha256, and the derive refuses, before reading a tick, a `generation.json` whose entries differ from the map's for that generation, so an entry added to a shipped generation in place stops the running derive. A new link entry ships with a new generation (D15), and a fork entry with none, so no acknowledgment changes a head already written.
- A run reads the state it needs from the table through its current head, checked as it reads, and never from a state file. From `versions` it takes each (series, day)'s last non-null token and whether that day stands retracted. From `coverage` it takes each series' high-water day, whether the series has been carried, and its newest observation whose absences are evidence.
- The record's chain is the one D5 and `verify` order: from the head D5's rule names, the newest stamp among the candidates when a broken link leaves more than one.
- **The table against the record.** Before deriving, a run checks that every tick its head's ancestry names is still on that chain, in that order, with the `tick.json` sha256 its heads recorded, and that each tick it is about to derive names in `prev` the sha256 the table recorded for its predecessor, or carries the chaining check's `error`, or is named by one of the generation's D15 entries. Otherwise it stops before deriving, with ERROR naming the tick: a fork, when a tick the table holds has left the chain (a head deleted on ops after the derive took it, which the next tick chains around, D5), or a moved tick, when its `tick.json` no longer hashes to what the table recorded (an edit, damage, or a restore to other bytes).
- A run catches up. It derives every tick on the chain after its head, oldest first, one part and one head each, so the next run takes the ticks a failed run missed, and which runs failed never shapes the parts.
- A rebuild is the same command run into an empty table root. It derives from genesis and reproduces a head's bytes from any copy of the record that holds the head's ancestry with the `tick.json` sha256 each of its heads records and whose bodies re-hash to them (a copy that verifies clean through the head's tick), because a head commits to frame content and never to parquet bytes, which a later library may write differently. Both copies hold that ancestry unless a tick was deleted, or a `tick.json` changed, after the derive took it. `--through <stamp>` rebuilds along the chain that ends at the named tick, in a copy that holds it, and is refused on a table root that holds anything but the derive's own lockfile, `.capture.lock`, which its lock creates first, so the running table only ever follows the record's own chain; a head past a fork is rebuilt from the copy that kept its branch, as the NAS copy keeps a tick deleted on ops after it was pulled (D5). A test holds three tables equal head by head: one built tick by tick, one that skipped runs, and a rebuild. The rollout compares a rebuild from the NAS copy with the ops table by value (the deployment, step 11).
- **After a fork or a moved tick.** Either stops the derive at every run until it is cleared, each stop an ERROR. A moved tick clears when its restore against the witness (D15) gives back the bytes the table recorded, and the next run resumes on its own. Otherwise a new generation ships, `DERIVE_GENERATION` plus one, built from genesis along the record's current chain, with the restore's D15 entry when a restore made the link disagree. The old generation is kept, readable through `read_table`, and its catalog line names its heads past the fork or the moved tick as rebuildable only `--through` a copy that holds their ancestry, or, when the derive took edited bytes the witness refutes, or a fork's tick off the chain is marked lost (D15), by no copy: the record decides, and those heads are the evidence of what it took.
- `DERIVE_GENERATION` is an integer in `cli/vintage/derive.py`. A change of derive logic is a new generation: the derive opens `gen-<N+1>/` and builds it from genesis in its first run, and keeps `gen-<N>/` as it stands, never appending to it again, every head in it still pinnable. A re-pin back to an image of generation N resumes `gen-<N>/` from its own head.
- A test pins, per generation, the head sha256 that the fixture record derives to, in a map whose shipped entries are never edited. It fails a rule change that moves the fixture's head until the generation or the pin moves. It cannot tell an edited pin from a bumped generation, so refusing a shipped entry edited in place is the review's job, and the map's diff shows the edit.

**Refusals, crashes and leftovers.**
- The run stops at a tick on any of these: a link that does not hash and that neither its successor's `prev.error` nor one of the generation's D15 entries names; a body that does not re-hash to its entry; one of the table's own heads, `part.json` files or frames that does not hash; a fork or a moved tick (above); or any exception. It refuses before reading a tick on a lock another run holds, a refused table root (below), or a `generation.json` whose entries differ from the code's. Every part published before the stop stays, and the record is untouched.
- A run that dies leaves its staging entries. They never block a later run: each name carries the run's start second and eight random hex digits, so a run after a clock step back cannot meet a dead run's name. Each later run names them in one WARNING, and the operator reads and deletes them by hand (nothing under `cli/vintage/` deletes, D18). A run that ends leaves nothing in `staging/`, since each staged generation, part and head directory is renamed away whole.
- A crash between a rename and its chmod leaves a published generation, part or head directory at 0755; each run sets any it finds to 0555, as the next tick does for a tick (D5).
- A crash between a part's rename and its head's leaves a part with no head. The next run derives that tick again in memory and, when the `part.json` it computes equals the published one byte for byte, stages only the head; otherwise it refuses. Nothing is staged for the part, so adopting it leaves no leftover.
- The derive refuses a table root inside a record root, inside that root's parent, or under the configured NAS mount (`nfs_mount_dir` in `zcrypto.toml`, `/mnt/zhao-crypto`), whichever copy of the record the run reads, comparing resolved paths as the process sees them. Its purpose is to keep the table out of the record and out of the recorder's own data root beside it. In the container the record sits at `/vintages/record` and the table at `/versions`, so the rule admits the production pair and still refuses a host run that puts the table under `vintages/`. The mount being read-only on ops (`docs/reference/fleet.md:66`) is a second wall, not the only one.

**How a failure shows.**
- **The run's summary line.** Every run ends with one line. When the table reached the record's head it is INFO `vintage derive complete generation=… head=… head_sha256=… parts_added=…`; otherwise it is ERROR `vintage derive stopped generation=… head=… at=… reason=…`, `at` naming the tick it stopped at, or `none` for a refusal before any tick. The derive exits 0 for the first and 1 for the second, catching every exception. Its runner logs a parse-shaped ERROR when the container exits with any other code or prints neither line (docker's own failures, whatever code docker gives, and a kill), and exits with the container's code.
- **The alert.** An ERROR pages at warning through the existing `Ops · ERROR logs` rule (`zcrypto-ops-error-logs`, `severity: warning`, `infra/grafana/alerts.yaml:2560` and `:2640`). Its selector admits `container=~"alloy|liquidations|zcrypto-.*"`, and the derive unit's lines carry `container="zcrypto-vintage-derive"` once D17's keep-regex ships its journal (`config.alloy` turns a unit's name into that label). Warning is the severity the file gives derived data that the next healthy run recomputes (`:2391-2392`, "a failed materialize costs freshness, not truth"), and a stopped derive loses nothing the record does not still hold. The derive has no rule of its own (below).
- **The metric.** On every run that read its table, the derive writes `/textfile/vintage-derive.prom` through D10's `.prom` helper. It holds one family, `zcrypto_vintage_derive_head_timestamp_seconds`: the Unix time of the table head's tick stamp, 0 while the table is empty. It is charted beside the record's ages on the dashboard row.
- **What no alert sees.** A derive that hangs: its unit stays active and each later start joins it. Only the head's growing age on the dashboard shows it, and the record and its pages are untouched.

**Reading a head.** `read_table(table root, generation, head)` in `cli/vintage/` takes a head by its file's path within the generation, as a citation stores it (below), by its tick, whose stamp gives the path, or by its sha256 alone, which lists `heads/` to find it. It walks back to genesis by `prev_tick`, computing each predecessor's path from its stamp, and checks each predecessor's bytes against the `prev` its successor records, then every `part.json`, frame-content hash (by the version each head names) and `parts_sha256` against what the chain records. It returns the head it read, the `versions` and `coverage` frames through it, and the later facts each part lists, joined to the rows they name. `head(table root, generation)` lists `heads/` and names the head at the highest chain position, which a published clock regression cannot displace; the derive takes its own head the same way. For a reader it is for looking only: a trial-registry record or a reading cites a head's sha256, never "latest" (§8's "never 'latest'"). Both frames carry a `provider` column. A reader leaves every `bg-*` row out of both frames by the one key `provider == "bgeometrics"`, and every Coin Metrics row by `provider == "coinmetrics"`.

**Citing a head.** A record's `datasets` block will carry the head file's path within its generation and the head's sha256, as `ObservedReader.read_rolling` records an index line (`cli/registry/observed.py:194`). Three things belong to the first change that cites a head: that reader arm; `conformance`'s check of a cited head (`tests/test_registry_conformance.py` already verifies a cited `index/` line through its chain); and the table's channel to the hub's `hot/`, which `tests/test_cited_sets_reach_the_hub.py` requires of every cited set. This change builds none of them. No record cites a head before a C3 trial spec exists, and that spec decides the channel; the ops outbox that carries `ohlc-rolling`'s write-once files is the precedent.

**No copy on the NAS.** The table stays on the ops node, as tape-bars does ("fully derived, and its source archive is NAS custody kept forever, so a lost copy costs a re-materialization, not data", `docs/reference/data-catalog-full.md`). The owner's ruling on open question 3 gave the record its own custody channel; marco's reason for that channel is that the record cannot be backfilled (D14). The table can be rebuilt, head for head, from a copy of the record that holds a head's ancestry as the head records it, which both copies do outside a fork or a moved tick (above), and `--through` reaches a branch the NAS copy kept; a head the derive built from edited bytes is reproduced by no copy, and the record refutes it. So a copy of the table on custody guards nothing the record's two copies do not, and, pulled under `--ignore-existing` with no delete, every generation a logic fix opens would stay on custody for ever beside the bytes that refute it. The `hot-out` route, which would carry the table to every research node, is the one that ruling declined for the record, and marco declines it for the table on the same ground.

**The frames.** No column is nested, so every value renders as one scalar in the frame-content hash's lines, and the frames export to CSV, which polars 2.1.0 refuses for a list column (the measured basis).
- `versions.parquet`: one row per version first seen at the part's tick, sorted by `ts`, `source`, `series`. Its columns, in order:
  - `ts`: the day D at 00:00Z, Datetime µs UTC;
  - `provider`: `alternative.me`, `coinmetrics`, `mempool.space` or `bgeometrics`;
  - `source`: the source id, `fng`, `cm-<asset>`, `mempool-hashrate` or `bg-<metric>`;
  - `series`: `cm/<asset>/<metric>`, `fng/value`, `fng/value_classification`, `mempool/avgHashrate` or `bg/<metric>`;
  - `kind`: `inherited`, `first_publication`, `backfill`, `restatement`, `retraction` or `restoration`, by the reader section's steps 4 and 8;
  - `token`: the value's text as served, null only for a retraction;
  - `tick`: the stamp of the tick whose observation first showed the version, the part's own;
  - `position`: that tick's place along the chain, genesis 0, which keeps the true order across a published clock regression;
  - `ended_at` and `knowable_at` (D6);
  - `appeared_after`: Q3's lower end, null for `inherited`;
  - `outcome`: 0 or 2, of the observation that showed the version;
  - `flags`: step 9's flags known at that tick, sorted and comma-joined, empty when none.
- `coverage.parquet`: one row per (source, series) observation at the part's tick, plus one row per fetch that was no observation (outcome 1) with `series` set to `*`, sorted by `source`, `series`. Its columns are `ts` (the tick's start), `provider`, `tick`, `position`, `tick_json_sha256`, `source`, `series`, `outcome`, `reason`, `ended_at`, `knowable_at`, `first_day`, `last_day`, `days`, `evidence` (whether its absences are evidence for the series, step 2) and `flags`. It tells "no tick at t" from "the source did not answer at t", and the as-of reader takes its last-seen time from it.

The catalog and the status feed are not series and stay in the record alone.

**The as-of reader.** `as_of(versions, coverage, later facts, t)` is a pure function in `cli/vintage/`. For each (series, day) it returns the version latest along the chain among those whose `knowable_at` is at or before t; a retraction comes back as a null token with its kind. Beside each version the reader returns three things:
- the version's first-seen `ended_at`;
- its last-seen time: the `ended_at` of the newest coverage row for that series that is knowable by t, lies at or after the version's tick and before the next version's, and is known to have held the day. A row is known to have held the day when the day lies within its `first_day`..`last_day` and either its absences are evidence (`evidence` true) or its days run unbroken (`days` equals `last_day` − `first_day` + 1). An observation graded 2 for that series with a hole inside its range is left out, since coverage cannot tell whether it held the day;
- both rows' flags, with the later facts joined.

It reads the table's frames through a head and the in-memory derivation alike, and a test holds the two results equal.

**Licences in the table.** The table holds Coin Metrics rows under CC BY-NC 4.0 and BGeometrics rows under terms that bar derivative works "without prior written permission". Both stay on the ops node alone, never committed or published. The owner's ruling on open question 4a gates any C3 trial's reliance on the `cm-*` rows on Coin Metrics' written permission. The ruling on open question 13 puts the `bg-*` rows in from the first run for private research use; nothing beyond private research use is ruled, whether an engine input or a publication. The catalog entry names both.

**Size.** Inferred from the measured bodies, not measured. btc's 31 metrics are 8,341,605 raw bytes over at most 6,521 days, so a cell costs at least 41.3 bytes, and a whole tick's 32,623,978 Coin Metrics bytes are then about 0.8 million cells.

  - **The scan.** Nothing of a `bg-*` exchange is stored until it has been scanned for the key in four forms: its raw bytes, its JSON-escaped form (`/` as `\/`), and its percent-encoded forms in upper- and lower-case hex, since the `b64token` alphabet's `/`, `+` and `=` are what JSON and URL encoders escape. The scan reads the status line's reason phrase, every response header name and value, and the whole received body: every field of the exchange the server's text fills, the status being an integer, the HTTP version `http.client`'s and the final URL the request's own. It reads the body decoded when its Content-Encoding is one the stdlib decodes (gzip, deflate, zstd; the measured basis), through a decoder whose output stops at D7's 64 MiB cap and that reads each layer to its end: a layer that ends before its input does (a second gzip member or zstd frame, or bytes after the stream), or that stops short of its own end (a truncated stream), does not decode, since one streaming call stops at the first member's or frame's end without raising. It reads the body before a retried attempt's 4 KiB `head` is cut, so a key that the cut would split is still found.
  - A reason phrase, or a response header whose name or value, holds the key in any of those forms is stored as `<redacted>`.
  - A body that holds it is not stored, and neither is any `head` cut from it. Its attempt records `credential echoed` and the byte count, and no hash, since a hash of bytes that hold the key is a fingerprint of it; the source reads outcome 1. A body in an encoding the stdlib cannot decode, or whose bytes do not decode under the encoding it names, or that decodes past the cap, is not stored either, reason `credential unscannable`, and none of the three raises out of the fetch. This is the one judgement about a body that stops it being stored, a named exception to the anti-goal beside D4's size cap.
  - For a `bg-*` source, the check's `reason` and its WARNING never quote a body byte.
  - An echo in a form the scan does not read would be stored on both copies for good, since no published byte is removed (D18). The remedy is a rotation (step 4 below), which makes the stored value a dead key.
- **Logs.** No line carries a request header. An exception from the keyed request path is logged by its class name alone, as `cli/logging/redact.py` keeps an exception's text "only where its class cannot quote the URL". On that path the attempt's `error` in `tick.json` carries the class name alone too, and the top-level handler's ERROR the class name and the innermost frame's file, line and function, which name code and never the exception's text, since `http.client` quotes an invalid header value whole (the measured basis).

**A missing or revoked key.**
- **Missing** (a token file that is absent, unreadable, a directory or empty, or a malformed value): the four sources read outcome 1, `no credential` or `malformed credential`, with no attempt and no dial, and one WARNING each. Every other source runs as before, and the tick exits 1 and pings the dead-man (D9, D12).
- **Revoked:** the server's 401 or 403 is stored unless it echoes the key, reads outcome 1 and trips the breaker for the other three, with a WARNING. Whether an invalid key is refused, or served as keyless, is not measured. The attended first tick sends a valid key and cannot show it; the plan session's one request with a made-up bearer value measures it before the plan is written (D19). If an invalid key is served as keyless, a revoked key reads ok and pages nothing: the residual, named here and in the failure modes. The record then keeps what a keyless request is served, which the BGeometrics read measured as the free plan's window.
- **Paging:** either way D11 pages each source critical at 25 h. A key never provisioned leaves `last_ok` at 0 and pages as soon as the rule is pushed, which the push's by-value preflight shows first. This is what the owner's rulings on open questions 5 and 6 cost together: a revoked key pages critical, and only the owner can fix it, in the account.
- **No keyless fallback.** It would mix two entitlements under one source id, spend the per-IP budget this host shares, and read a revoked key as ok.

**The check (D8's `bg-<metric>` row).**
- **Shape:** a non-empty JSON array of objects, each carrying `d`, `unixTs` and the pinned value key. A body holds one series, so every failure is source-level.
- **Freshness:** the newest `d` no older than the tick's UTC date − 9: the free plan's 7-day hold, measured at the date − 7, plus two days for an update that runs late after the documented 03:00 UTC.
- **Never shorter at the top**, against the last ok body.
- **The oldest day exempt** from never-shorter-at-the-bottom, because the 4-year window moves forward a day each day.
- **Collapse:** the row count at least 90% of the last ok body's.
- **An entitlement change** is recorded as served: a paid plan's body would show it, and its `X-Data-Restricted` and `X-Data-Available-Until` headers, stored with the rest, say so.

## How a reader derives vintages and restatement diffs

1. **Read and verify.** A reader opens a record root read-only, either the ops copy or the NAS copy, and runs `verify` (D15) first. It never steps past a broken link silently.
2. **Observations.** A fetch whose final status is 200 and whose body is complete is an observation, stamped by its `ended_at` and knowable from max(`ended_at`, its final response's server Date + 1 s), or from `ended_at` alone where no Date parses (D6). An outcome-2 body is an observation flagged with its check's reason, and the values it holds are evidence. Its absences are evidence for a series S, as an ok body's are, unless its check names S in `metrics_absent` or `metrics_failed` or it failed at source level (D8), since a body graded 2 for a metric gone, a lost day or a collapsed history can lack what the vendor still holds. An outcome-1 fetch is a non-observation, "the source did not answer at t", which is distinct from "no tick at t".
3. **Parsing.** Bodies are parsed token-preserving, with `json.loads(…, parse_int=str, parse_float=str)` (shown by an earlier design reading to keep `826267196366358300000` and `1.50` exactly). A `json_stream` body is one object per line, and an absent key reads as null. A series is `cm/<asset>/<metric>`, `fng/value`, `fng/value_classification`, `mempool/avgHashrate` or `bg/<metric>`. Its key is the UTC day parsed from the row's own `time` (an ISO time), `timestamp` (epoch seconds) or `d` (a date), never the token text, so a change in a vendor's time format does not re-key its history. Its value is the token text; a `null` value reads as absent, as an absent key does, and a key whose value in any row is an object or an array is no series of that body. A body the parser cannot read, which only an outcome-2 body can be (an HTML page, an empty body, a non-identity encoding, a shape the check refused), yields no value and is no evidence for any series, so it never stops a derivation.
4. **Versions.** For each (S, D), observations are ordered by `ended_at`, which is the chain's order except across a published clock regression, where the chain's order is taken (step 9). An observation is evidence about (S, D) only when it carries S. S's high-water day before an observation is the newest day S held in any earlier observation that carried S. Absence is read two ways, each only from an observation that carries S and whose absences are evidence for S (step 2):
   - evidence that D was withdrawn, for a retraction: the observation does not hold D, and D lies inside the range of days it holds for S;
   - evidence that D was not yet held, for Q3's lower end: the observation does not hold D, whether D lies inside its range or after its newest day.

   Each token is judged against the last non-null token seen for (S, D), never against the observation just before:
   - no earlier token, and D after S's high-water day: a first publication; per series, this covers what a row-level reading calls a completion, a Coin Metrics row whose network fields arrive after its `PriceUSD`;
   - no earlier token, and D at or before S's high-water day: a backfill, never a first publication, even where the observation just before, an outcome-2 body whose newest day went back, held less;
   - a token different from the last one: a restatement, whatever absences lay between;
   - the last token again after a retraction, an absence this step read as withdrawn: a restoration, a version first seen at that observation, which Q2 lists apart from restatements and the as-of view returns; the same token again after an absence that was no evidence makes no new version;
   - absent where the last token was a value, in an observation that is evidence D was withdrawn: a retraction.
5. **The three questions.**
   - First published, Q1: the token of (S, D)'s first publication by step 4, with its `ended_at`. A day inherited (step 8) or backfilled has no Q1.
   - Restated, Q2: every later version, each named for what it is (a restatement, a retraction or a restoration), with the `ended_at` at which it was first seen.
   - First appeared, Q3: the bracket (a, b], b the `ended_at` of the first observation that held D for S, and a that of the last observation before it that was evidence D was not yet held (step 4). For an ordinary new day, a is the `ended_at` of the tick before the one that first held it. An inherited day has no lower end.
6. **As of t.** The view reads step 4's versions, never raw observations: for each (S, D), the newest version whose first observation's knowable time (step 2) is at or before t. An absence counts only where step 4 reads a retraction, so an outcome-2 body's gaps that are no evidence leave the view unchanged. Each value is returned with its version's first-seen `ended_at`; its last-seen time, the `ended_at` of the newest observation of S knowable by t, at or after the version's first observation and before the next version's, that is known to have held D, which it is when D lies within the days it holds for S and either its absences are evidence for S (step 2) or its days for S run unbroken, so an observation graded 2 for S with a hole inside its range is left out even where it held D, as D16's coverage cannot tell; and any step-9 flag on either, so staleness and clock doubt show. The as-of path never does label arithmetic.
7. **Restatement diffs.** A diff between two ok observations of one source is the set of (S, D) that step 4 reads as a restatement, a retraction or a backfill. D10's gauge is its two-body reading between consecutive ok bodies: the same three kinds, plus a value restored unchanged after a retraction, which two bodies cannot tell from a backfill without the last non-null token.
8. **Inherited history.** Inherited is set per series: every (S, D) present in the first observation that carries S is the vendor's history as revised up to that observation. For a series carried from the source's first tick, that is the first tick. For one first carried later, withheld at genesis or granted later, it is that later observation, so its history is never read as first published. Inherited history is never point-in-time, and a backtest's years before the first tick stay revised.
9. **Clock evidence.** The reader flags, never drops silently, an observation whose tick reports the clock unsynchronised, an offset beyond 10 s (the ops clock rule's bar) or unknown, a `clock-offset.prom` older than 600 s (two periods of its 5-minute writer; at a boot tick taken before chrony has synchronised, it is the previous boot's file and still reads synchronised) or `ended_at` more than 300 s from the server's Date, and every observation of a tick whose stamp is at or below its predecessor's (D5) and of that predecessor, which may have been taken under a clock running ahead.
10. **Label conventions.** The first three matter only for inherited history, BGeometrics' for every day, and the catalog states all four:
    - Fear & Greed's label D appears at about D 00:00Z.
    - A Coin Metrics label D summarises [D, D+1), and its row first appears after D+1 00:00Z (the 2026-11-10 btc row carried only `PriceUSD` at 00:27:13Z on 11-11).
    - mempool's label is read from its code as the window [D−1, D). That is not measured, and the record's first-appearance times measure it.
    - BGeometrics' label D first reaches a free account at about D+7 03:00Z (the 7-day hold and the 03:00 UTC update), so a `bg/<metric>` series' "first published" means first shown to a free account; a day that leaves its 4-year window lies outside every later body's range and is never read as a retraction (step 4).
11. **Rolling windows.** The status feed (25 entries) and the `ReferenceRate*` metrics (7 points) are unioned across ticks. A restatement and a recalculation notice from the same window can be paired by reading that tick's `cm-status` body.
12. **Pinning.** A consumer pins a head of the versions table by the sha256 of its file and reads exactly the rows through it (D16). The head names its tick's `tick.json` sha256, which commits through `prev` to every earlier tick and through each `tick.json` to every body, and it commits through its own `prev` and `parts_sha256` to every earlier head and part. Reading the newest head is for looking, never for a record.

## Failure modes

- **A source answers a transport error, timeout, 5xx or 429.** Up to 3 attempts honour Retry-After; a retried attempt keeps its first 4 KiB, and the final answer's body is stored whole. Outcome 1, WARNING, and every other host's sources continue; a request that used up its attempts trips its host's or its endpoint's breaker (D7, the next two entries). The tick is published, the CLI exits 1, and a critical page comes only after 25 h with no ok fetch (D11). BGeometrics takes one retry per tick at most, and never after a 429 (D20).
- **A whole source host is down, or answers every request with a 5xx or a 429.** Down, its first request spends its three attempts and their waits, and the host's breaker marks its remaining requests not fetched without dialling. Answering 5xx or 429, each of its endpoints spends one request's attempts and waits, and that endpoint's breaker marks the rest of it; `community-api.coinmetrics.io`'s two endpoints can together reach the tick deadline, which is why its sources run last (D7). Either way the outage costs one request's attempts per endpoint rather than every request's, and every other host's sources are still reached inside the tick deadline.
- **One endpoint answers 5xx or 429 while its host serves the others** (the catalog alone, or one asset's query). Only the later requests to that endpoint are marked not fetched; the host's other endpoint is dialled as usual (D7). The catalog runs last, so a catalog fault never costs an asset, and an asset query's fault beside another host's fault can leave the catalog `tick deadline`. A fault tied to one asset's query costs the assets after it at every start while it lasts; a hand start naming them with `--source` records them (D7, the runbook).
- **A 403, a 404 or another non-429 4xx.** Not retried. The whole body is stored as its file, outcome 1, and the rule pages if it persists. It is a contract change: the owner drops or repoints the source, and a repoint is a new source id.
- **A Coin Metrics metric is revoked or unsupported.** The 200 still carries the rest of the asset's metrics. The presence check grades the body outcome 2 and names the absent metric. Every byte served is stored, and the rule pages at 25 h until code acknowledges the change with D8's pinned entry.
- **Format drift.** For example an HTML page after a redirect, a renamed field, an empty body or a non-identity encoding. The body is stored as served, outcome 2, with the reason and the final URL. No byte is lost while the code catches up.
- **A frozen metric** (its newest day past its D8 bound), **a body that lost days** (a metric's newest day earlier, or its oldest day later, than in the last ok body, as when a `json_stream` ends cleanly mid-history) **or a collapsed history** (rows under 90% of the last ok body). Outcome 2, the failing metrics named in `metrics_failed`. The page stands until code acknowledges it with D8's pinned entry, because the comparison is with the last ok body. The derivation takes that body's absences as no evidence for the metrics it names, and for every series after a collapse.
- **The catalog starts paginating.** A `next_page_url` in the body grades it outcome 2, so page one is never read as the whole catalog.
- **A response over 64 MiB.** Not stored. Outcome 1, `oversize <bytes>`.
- **A trickling server or the 20-minute deadline.** The per-request deadline's timer cuts the trickle, in the body or its framing. Sources not yet reached become outcome 1, `tick deadline`, and the tick is published.
- **The lock is held** (a second writer under another container name; a raced unit start or same-name `docker run` never reaches the CLI), **the target exists**, **staging or the record is unwritable**, **the read-back mismatches, or an exception is raised before the rename.** Exit 2, ERROR, nothing published, no dead-man ping. A held lock logs the recorder's own message, never the capture one. After a read-back mismatch, staging is left for inspection.
- **The stamp regresses** (a clock that stepped back, or one corrected after a tick published under a clock running ahead). The tick is published with an ERROR naming both stamps, `prev` names the chain's head, and recording never stops. The reader flags the tick and its predecessor, and the ops clock rule pages the cause.
- **A boot tick before chrony has synchronised.** `chrony-wait.service` is disabled on this host, so a `Persistent=true` boot tick can run on an unsynchronised clock while `clock-offset.prom` still holds the previous boot's reading. Its age, over 600 s, is in the tick, and the reader flags it (step 9).
- **A disk is full.** The write fails in staging, exit 2 and ERROR. `zcrypto-ops-disk-low` has already paged at 15% free.
- **A crash mid-tick** (OOM, kill or reboot). The partial staging directory is never published. The next tick runs normally and names the leftover in a WARNING, and the kernel releases the flock.
- **A leftover container after a dockerd crash, or the pinned image missing.** docker fails before the CLI starts, so the run prints no witness line, and the runner logs one ERROR naming the exit, whatever code docker gives (D12), which pages at warning through `Ops · ERROR logs`; `last_ok` stops advancing, and the rule pages. The runbook covers `docker ps -a --filter name=zcrypto-vintage-record`, removing the leftover and re-pulling the digest.
- **The timer is disabled, or the host is down through both ticks.** The rule pages, and so does noData if the textfile goes. The dead-man goes down at about 31 h, whatever Grafana's state. `Persistent=true` runs one tick at boot, and a tick never records the past.
- **Alloy, remote write or the observability node is dark.** NoData pages where Grafana lives. If Grafana itself is dark, the grafana-watchdog pair and this check's dead-man page.
- **A malformed or unreadable `.prom`.** `zcrypto-capture-textfile-unreadable` (`Node · node-exporter textfile unreadable or malformed (capture, ops)`) pages. The CLI chmods to 0644 before the rename, and a test holds that.
- **A corrupt `tick.json` met in the scan** (bit rot or a hand edit). It counts as no ok fetch, ERROR names the file, and the tick is never rewritten. The hash-checked restore (D15) puts the published bytes back from the other copy, checked against the witness.
- **The newest `tick.json` damaged or edited before the next tick.** The chaining check (D5) catches a head that no longer parses, lists other files than its directory holds, or names a body that no longer re-hashes: `prev` carries the error, ERROR pages, and the head is restored against its witness. An edit that keeps the head consistent is chained as found; the NAS copy pulled before it then disagrees with the successor's `prev`, the NAS verify logs ERROR, and the owner decides against the witness (D15).
- **A head deleted on ops before the next tick.** The next tick chains around it, so the ops copy verifies clean; the NAS copy that pulled it holds a fork, which its verify reports, and the INFO line in the journal names the lost tick. No restore clears the fork, and the tick is never restored on ops: code acknowledges it with D15's pinned entry, the off-chain tick's stamp and the `tick.json` sha256 its witness logged, or the owner's decision where it has none (D15), after which the verify of every copy holding the off-chain tick names the fork and reads it clean, and the ops copy's, which holds no such tick, reads the entry as nothing (D15). The ops derive, which took that head after its own recorder run, stops on the same fork until a new generation ships (the entry below on a tick the table holds leaving the record's chain). Should the NAS copy later lose that tick, its `--custody` verify logs ERROR at every loop until the tick is put back from a copy that holds it or the entry is marked lost (D15).
- **A pull killed mid-file** (a NAS recreate during the rsync). It can leave rsync's temporary `.<name>.XXXXXX` file in a NAS tick directory; verify reports it as a file the `tick.json` does not list, and the runbook removes it by hand.
- **A body or `tick.json` damaged on one copy.** On ops, the tick's re-verify finds it within a week and logs ERROR, and the tick exits 1. On the NAS, the loop's verify finds it within 24 loops, or at once if it arrived damaged among the newest four ticks, and logs `vintages verify failed` at ERROR. Either pages, and stays paging until the hash-checked restore from the other copy (D15).
- **The host clock jumps.** Every observation carries the chrony offset, the sync flag, the offset file's age and the server's Date, and the chain keeps the true order. The reader flags the observations, and the ops clock rule pages.
- **The NAS is down, or a pull fails.** The NAS block logs ERROR, which pages through `zcrypto-nas-archive-pull-errors`. Ops keeps accruing, the window of sole custody grows, and the next pull copies every missed tick.
- **The NAS channel is unwired.** ERROR every loop, which pages.
- **The NAS image lacks the verifier** (archive-pull pinned to a build from before this change). The block's verify fails every loop and logs ERROR, which pages; the deployment order moves `nas_capture_image` in the same NAS converge that deploys the block, so this is a wrong pin.
- **Path drift between the `rrsync` root and `record/`.** A converge-guard test fails in CI.
- **One of the two daily ticks fails systematically** (for example a vendor's maintenance at 19:37). Each day is still recorded, so nothing pages. This is the known residual, visible on the outcome panel and as a lower second-tick rate in the record.
- **A vendor restates history.** Not a failure: it is the dataset's purpose. The bytes are stored as served, and `changed_values` measures it the next tick.
- **A provider down for more than a day.** Each of its sources pages critical at 25 h, all in one message, and again every 4 h until the runbook's silence, time-boxed to the next 20:00 UTC, is set after triage (D11). The day is lost unless the provider answers again before 00:00 UTC and a hand start runs.
- **The per-IP budgets are shared with the workstation.** A research burst near a tick can meet a 429, which Retry-After and the backoff handle; BGeometrics' 15 a day are spent by the recorder alone, and nothing else on this host calls it (D20).
- **The BGeometrics key is missing or malformed** (never provisioned, an empty file, a value off its shape). Its four sources read outcome 1, `no credential` or `malformed credential`, with no dial and a WARNING each; every other source runs, the tick exits 1 and pings, and D11 pages each at 25 h (D20).
- **The token file is absent, unreadable or a directory** (a hand delete, a failed render). The runner gives the container `/dev/null` and logs one ERROR naming the path, which pages at warning; the four `bg-*` sources read `no credential`, every other source records, the CLI prints its witness line and the dead-man is pinged for a tick that was published (D1, D12, D20). An `--tags ops` converge renders the file again.
- **A late hand start** (after the evening tick's 19:57 deadline, as the runbook's remedy for a page). Its good fetches read as 19:57 in `last_ok`, so if both of the next day's ticks fail, that day still pages at about 21:02, while a hand start can record it (D10, D11). For a `bg-*` source the late start and the two failed ticks can spend one window's 15 requests, so the save waits for the reset the newest tick's `X-RateLimit-*` headers state, and a reset after 00:00 UTC loses the day for that source (D11).
- **The BGeometrics key is revoked** (a 401 or 403). The answer is stored unless it echoes the key, outcome 1, the breaker stops the other three, and D11 pages each at 25 h; the owner rotates the key (D20's steps).
- **A revoked key served as keyless.** Not measured until the plan session's made-up-bearer request (D19). If the server serves an invalid key as keyless, a revoked key reads ok and pages nothing, and the record keeps the free plan's window, as a keyless request is served; this residual is named, not guarded (D20).
- **BGeometrics' budget is spent** (a 429, or a `Remaining` header at 0, as when a hand tick puts a fourth tick in one window). The requests past it read outcome 1 and are never retried; one such tick never pages.
- **A BGeometrics answer echoes the key.** The scan reads the whole body, decoded where the stdlib can, the reason phrase and every header name and value for the key's raw, JSON-escaped and percent-encoded forms. A body that holds one is not stored, nor is any head cut from it; its attempt records `credential echoed` and the byte count; a reason phrase or a response header holding one is stored as `<redacted>`; and the source reads outcome 1. A body in an encoding the stdlib cannot decode, or that does not decode or decodes past the cap, reads `credential unscannable` and is not stored either. An echo in another form would be kept for good, and the remedy is a rotation (D20).
- **A derive fails for a passing cause** (its lock held, a full disk, an exception from a fault that clears). It stops, keeps every part published before the stop, logs ERROR, which pages at warning through `Ops · ERROR logs`, and exits 1; the record is untouched, and the next run, after the next recorder run, catches up from the record (D16).
- **A derive stops on the record or its own table** (a link that does not hash, a table file that does not hash, or a fork or a moved tick, the next entry). It stops the same way at every run, each an ERROR and a warning page, until the cause is cleared: a restore against the witness (D15), a hand put-back of the table file, or a new generation (D16). The next run does not catch up on its own.
- **A tick the table holds leaves the record's chain or changes its bytes** (a head deleted on ops after the derive took it, a `tick.json` edited, damaged or restored to other bytes). The derive stops before deriving, ERROR naming the fork or the moved tick. A moved tick's restore that gives back the bytes the table recorded lets it resume. A fork's does not: the next tick chained around the deleted one, so the restored tick stays off the chain (D5) and the derive stays stopped. Otherwise, and for every fork, a new generation ships, the old one kept and readable, and the catalog names which of its heads a copy can still rebuild with `--through` (D16).
- **A derive hangs.** Its unit stays active and later starts join it, so the table stops refreshing with no page; only the head's age on the dashboard shows it. Nothing is lost, and the recorder's unit and pages are unaffected (D16).
- **A derive dies mid-part** (OOM, kill, reboot). Its staging entries are left and named by each later run in a WARNING, for the operator to read and delete; they never block a later run. A directory published but left at 0755 is set to 0555 by the next run. A part published without its head is adopted by the next run, which stages only the head, when the `part.json` it computes in memory matches the published one byte for byte (D16).
- **A table file is damaged.** The next derive's check of its own table refuses, ERROR, warning, and so does every later run until the file is back. The same generation is rebuilt from the record into a scratch root, by a revision whose `DERIVE_GENERATION` is that generation, and the file is put back by hand: a head or `part.json` byte-equal to the rebuild's, which the chain's sha256 decides; a parquet file whose frame-content hash equals what its `part.json` records, though its bytes may differ from the published file's. That put-back is the table's one exception to append-only, and a cited head stays verifiable against such a rebuild from any copy that holds its ancestry (D16).
- **A derive container left over after a dockerd crash.** The next derive's `docker run` fails in docker on the name, before the CLI starts, so no summary line is printed; its runner logs ERROR and exits with docker's code, which pages at warning, and the runbook removes the leftover. The recorder's unit is unaffected (D16).

## The archive path to the NAS

D14 is the decision. In brief: ops never pushes, and the NAS pulls `record/` hourly over its own `rrsync -ro` key with `--ignore-existing` and never `--delete`, so the first bytes to arrive are kept for ever and a file is never re-sent. The block, in the hot block's shape:

```
if [ -n "${VINTAGES_SOURCE:-}" ]; then
	if ! rsync --archive --ignore-existing --chmod=D0775,F0664 \
			-e "ssh -i $VINTAGES_SSH_KEY -p ${VINTAGES_SSH_PORT:-22} -o IdentitiesOnly=yes -o StrictHostKeyChecking=yes -o CheckHostIP=no -o AddressFamily=inet -o UserKnownHostsFile=$ARCHIVE_SSH_KNOWN_HOSTS" \
			"$VINTAGES_SOURCE" "$VINTAGES_DEST"; then
		log ERROR "vintages pull failed (source=$VINTAGES_SOURCE dest=$VINTAGES_DEST), continuing"
	fi
	if ! zcrypto vintage verify --root "$VINTAGES_DEST" --custody --newest 4 --slice "$slice" --slices 24; then
		log ERROR "vintages verify failed (dest=$VINTAGES_DEST), continuing"
	fi
else
	log ERROR "vintages channel unwired (VINTAGES_SOURCE unset): custody is not acquiring the recorder's ticks"
fi
```

A tick directory appears on ops by one rename, so the pull sees whole ticks. A transfer interrupted mid-file leaves no file under its final name (rsync writes a temporary file and renames it), and the next loop completes the copy; a killed one can leave the temporary `.<name>.XXXXXX` file behind, which `verify` reports as unlisted and the runbook removes by hand. The verify runs after a failed pull too, since what is already on the NAS still needs its slice re-hashed. `zcrypto vintage verify --custody` on `/mnt/zhao-crypto/vintages`, whole, proves the copy at rollout.

## Invariants

- No file under `record/` is ever written after the rename that publishes its tick, and nothing transient is ever named under `record/`. A published tick directory is 0555 and its files 0444, and the only bytes ever placed into one afterwards are a hash-checked restore's, equal to what its manifest hashes for a body or to its witness for a `tick.json`.
- A tick's `tick.json` lists every body file in its directory and hashes each one.
- Every tick but the first names its predecessor, the chain's head when it was published. Publication order is the chain's; no two published ticks share a stamp, and a stamp at or below its predecessor's exists only with the ERROR that named it.
- No tick carries a stamp other than its own start second, and no body bytes other than those the source sent.
- Nothing under `record/` or `staging/` is deleted, replaced or rewritten by code under `cli/vintage/`, whose only renames are inside the tick-publish, `.prom`-publish and table-publish helpers and whose every write opens a path it creates exclusively, or, for the part writer's parquet files, writes inside a staging directory it created exclusively (D18).
- No request but a BGeometrics one carries a key, and no byte the recorder writes or logs holds it (D20).
- No table file is written inside a record root, its parent or the configured NAS mount, and no generation, part or head is opened for writing after its publishing rename; the only bytes ever placed into one afterwards are a hand put-back's, a head or `part.json` equal to what the chain hashes, or a parquet file whose frame-content hash equals its `part.json`'s. A generation's heads only grow, one per tick along one chain, and a rebuild from a copy of the record that holds a head's ancestry as the head records it reproduces that head byte for byte. Every version names the tick whose body holds its token (D16).
- The recorder's unit never waits on the derive: the derive runs in its own unit after it, with the record mounted read-only and no network (D16).
- `zcrypto vintage record` exits 0 or 1 only when it published a tick, and the dead-man is pinged only on 0 or 1 with the CLI's witness line in the run's output (D12).
- Each copy is re-verified on a schedule: every ops body within a week, every NAS body within 24 loops.

## How this design was chosen

Three designs were weighed against the brief and four maps of the tree, the ops pattern, the archive path, the conventions and the sources.
- **The base: the fleet operator's design.** It was the strongest whole. It brings the timer pattern, the tick directory published by one rename, store-first-judge-second, the bounds and breaker, the 25 h rule timed to save a day, the dead-man, the custody channel with `--ignore-existing`, and gauges read off the record.
- **Grafted from the minimal archiver.**
  - The exchange kept whole: headers as ordered pairs, request headers, every attempt.
  - The fetch time as the stamp, with the server's Date as evidence and as the floor of a value's knowable time.
  - The bounded claims below.
  - The point that a runner `.prom` adds nothing the record does not already say.
- **Grafted from the point-in-time design.**
  - The three questions and the as-of view as the record's acceptance test, with the derivation shipped as code and tests.
  - Token-preserving parsing, the inherited flag and host clock evidence in every tick.
  - The chain, which also gives a one-hash pin.
  - Tick slots set 23 minutes before an engine cycle.
- **Rejected wholes.**
  - The minimal archiver's `hot-out` route (the hot charter and `data fetch` to every node), its content-addressed blobs, its unshipped journal and its lack of a dead-man.
  - The point-in-time design's six ticks with two cadence classes, its parquet records through `zcrypto archive pull` (no `--ignore-existing`), its versions dataset written inside the tick (the table is refreshed in its own unit after each recorder run instead, D16) and its count of failed ticks today.
- **Changed against all three.** `ignore_forbidden_errors` turned on, with a presence check, after the 01:08Z measurement showed silence is the API's default for an unsupported pair anyway. And breadth: every free daily metric.
- **Changed by the owner's rulings of 2026-11-11, against the recommendation.** BGeometrics recorded with the owner's key, against not now (D20); the missed-source rule at critical, against a warning (D11); the derived dataset materialised and cataloged in this change, against leaving it to the first C3 trial (D16); BGeometrics in the derived dataset from its first run, against asking BGeometrics first (D16); and the derived dataset a table refreshed after every run, against frozen sets minted by hand (D16).
- **Where it departs from marco's earlier calls.**
  - The runtime is the timers', not the poller's.
  - Unchanged bodies by hash reference are dropped.
  - No header dates a body, so the fetch time does.
  - "Once a day" becomes two ticks.
  - The dead-man is kept, because the daily peers carry one.
  - There is no live test at all.

## What the record does not hold

- Intermediate redirect hops: only the final URL is kept.
- The peer address and the TLS details.
- Anything between ticks: first-seen precision is the tick interval, 12 hours.
- A retried attempt's body beyond its first 4 KiB, and any body over 64 MiB or cut by its deadline.
- Any source not in the table, and any metric not in an asset's pinned list, though the recorded catalog shows what was available.
- Proof that a Coin Metrics `json_stream` body was served from one consistent snapshot. A recalculation mid-stream would read as a later restatement.
- The BGeometrics key: its request header is stored as `Bearer <redacted>`, and a body or head that echoed it is recorded by its byte count alone (D20).
- A BGeometrics day in its last 7: the free plan holds them back, so each day enters the record about a week after its label.
