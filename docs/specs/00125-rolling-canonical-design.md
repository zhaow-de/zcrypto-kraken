# One growing OHLC canonical: `ohlc-rolling`, the Q3 dump's 15-minute frame extended day by day with tape-bars' finals from 2026-10-01, a pin naming an as-of day by the hash of that day's chained index line

## Problem

The quarterly Kraken OHLCVT archive is finished as a source: the owner's decision of 2026-10-09 is that research does not rely on it from here on.
Today a search chooses between three kinds of name, and every ingest and every reach round adds names: the frozen canonical `ohlc-full` (12 pairs × {1440, 240, 60}, every leg ending 2026-03-31, `docs/reference/data-catalog-full.md:16-53`) and its 15-minute twin `ohlc-15m`; a stamped sibling per dump ingest (`ohlc-full-20261007` and `ohlc-15m-20261007`, through 2026-09-30, the Q2 siblings having left `hot/` for the NAS's `backup-ohlc-squash-2026-10-09/` today); and a REST-joined `ohlc-reach-<stamp>` round that carries the newest frozen sibling forward (`cli/ohlc/reach.py`).
The readers find their set by name pattern — the newest `ohlc-full-<stamp>` (`resolve_canonical_root`, `cli/data/rebuild.py:141-163`), the newest `ohlc-reach-<stamp>` (`resolve_ohlc_source`, `:166-181`) — or by a fixed frozen name (`cli/engine/command.py:86`, `cli/portfolio/record44_legs.py:24`).
Spec 00087's materializer publishes 15-minute daily finals from the captured trade tape since 2026-07-08 (`/var/lib/zcrypto-ops/tape-bars/<BASE>/<QUOTE>/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>.parquet`, a `.sha256` sidecar each, final-once-written, 60/240/1440 derived exactly by `derive_bars`), and left "whether a future re-freeze unions `tape-bars`" to that freeze (`docs/specs/00087-tape-bars-materializer-design.md:95`); this spec is that decision.
The goal: one obvious canonical that reaches the present and is pinned by hash, in place of a frozen frame, a stamped sibling per ingest and a reach round.
The decisions are the shape of one growing dataset on the Q3 base, and what a pin means for a dataset that grows, under spec 00056 D1c's rule that the hub's transport is append-only at file level (`docs/specs/00056-ops6-loop-design.md:21`).

## Decisions

### D1 — The shape: a 15-minute base copied from the Q3 frame and a daily extension copied from tape-bars, the seam at 2026-10-01T00:00Z

A new hot set, `ohlc-rolling`, holds one grid, 15 minutes, in three trees:
- `base/<BASE>/<QUOTE>/15.parquet`: the 12 files of `ohlc-15m-20261007`, copied byte for byte (each file's sha256 equal to its source's), every bar through 2026-09-30.
- `days/<BASE>/<QUOTE>/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>.parquet`: tape-bars' daily finals from 2026-10-01 on, copied byte for byte, each hashing to its tape-bars sidecar (`cli/tick/materialize.py:129-135`); the sidecars are not copied, D2's index being the set's one attestation.
- `index/`: D2's lines.
The dump owns every bar before the seam, the tape every bar from it; 60, 240 and 1440 are derived on read by `derive_bars` (`cli/tick/materialize.py:52-72`) over the base and the day files together, as tape-bars' own consumers derive them (spec 00087 D1).
Why the tape can continue the archive, and in which columns, from the overlap (basis 1): outside each pair's first tape day, the two sources share 91,399 15-minute bars from 2026-07-09 (2026-07-24 for the two `/BTC` legs) to 2026-09-30, with no timestamp on one side only; open, high, low and close are equal on every bar, and close on every bar of the first days too; volume is equal to float summation rounding on all but 3 bars and count on all but the same 3; vwap differs on 83,368.
The 3 are the slot 2026-09-10 08:00Z on ADA/EUR, ETH/EUR and SOL/EUR, all inside the minute 08:11, where the tape holds 4, 2 and 2 trades the dump's 1-minute rows lack, their trade ids contiguous with their neighbours' (basis 1c): where the two disagree on volume, the tape is the more complete. Kraken's REST bars side with the tape there: T0065 recorded the Q2 round's REST bars against this dump as count-unequal on 3 bars per grid with maximum relative volume differences of 1.24e-03 at 1h, 2.78e-04 at 4h and 2.16e-05 daily (`docs/open-topics/archive/T0065-data-pipeline-maturity-for-strategy-research.md:63`), the tape's own three maxima against it (basis 1d).
Vwap is two different quantities: the dump's is the close-weighted proxy Σ(close·volume)/Σvolume over 1-minute rows (`cli/backfill/aggregate.py:5`, `:23`), the tape's the tick-weighted mean (`cli/tick/aggregate.py:35-37`); they are up to 2.09% apart on a 15-minute bar and 1.20% on a daily one.
At 1440, deriving the tape's 15-minute bars gives the same reading — 978 shared daily bars, open/high/low/close equal on all, count unequal on 3, volume beyond rounding on 3, vwap unequal on all 978 (basis 1d).
Why the dump wins the overlap: the base then stays the Q3 frame byte for byte, so its identity is the one already written (`set_sha256` `8c05c1a892f6e0bc…`), and the tape contributes only the days no dump will reach.
Lost: starting at 2026-07-08 with the tape preferred — it would gain the 8 trades and a tick-weighted vwap for 84 days (69 on the `/BTC` legs), but rewrite the 12 base files into bytes nothing has pinned, and splice inside each file, because each pair's first tape day is partial: the dump holds 616 bars the tape lacks, every one before the pair's first tape bar, 12:45Z to 13:45Z on 2026-07-08 and 11:45Z and 12:45Z on 2026-07-23 (basis 1g).
Lost: starting at 2026-07-08 with the dump preferred — the same bars as the decision, plus 990 day files no reader would read.
Lost: materialising 60, 240 and 1440 — `derive_bars` over `ohlc-15m-20261007` reproduces `ohlc-full-20261007` at all three grids, 36,656, 219,093 and 864,621 bars, with identical timestamps, open/high/low/close and count equal on every bar, and volume and vwap within 1.9e-15 relative (basis 1e); materialised files would add nothing a derivation lacks, cost the 40,171,394 bytes of the Q3 coarse files, and give each grid two paths, a materialised base and a derived extension, that could disagree at the seam — the class spec 00087 D1 removed. Deriving all three grids for all 12 base files takes 0.42 s (basis 3).
Lost: referencing the base by hash instead of copying it — the set would span `ohlc-15m-20261007` and its own directory, and its base would live only as long as a stamped sibling the owner may retire as the Q2 ones were today; the copy costs 108,740,563 bytes on the hub and each node, the twelve files without the sibling's manifest, and lets the Q3 siblings retire later.
Columns: the base's order is `FRAME_SCHEMA`'s, `ts, open, high, low, close, vwap, volume, count` (`cli/ohlc/dataset.py:15`); the day files' is `ticks_to_bars`', `ts, open, high, low, close, volume, count, vwap`; the bytes are copied as they are, and the reader selects by name into `FRAME_SCHEMA`'s order, as spec 00087 D2 asks of any union (`docs/specs/00087-tape-bars-materializer-design.md:37`), so every frame a reader returns has one schema across the seam.
Vwap changes meaning at the seam, and the base line (D2) and the catalog (D8) say so; one reader consumes it, the universe's quote volume, `volume × vwap` (`cli/universe/volume.py:13`, `:30`); a reader that needs one definition across the seam reads close.
Pairs: the base's 12 and the tape's 12 are the same set (basis 2); a pair the tape stops carrying keeps its base and, with no later final to carry a day past the materializer's re-scan window, holds the head at its first day without a final until the owner closes the pair (D6), which D7's head check reports and, past 14 days, escalates; a pair the capture adds is not in the set, and joining it is a new set, never an edit of this one.
The name: `ohlc` alone was the v0 set's, and the legacy pins' evidence still names "the retired v0 data/ohlc" (`docs/reference/legacy-dataset-pins.jsonl:1`), so reusing it would make that evidence ambiguous; `ohlc-live` borrows the engine's live store's word.

### D2 — A pin is an as-of day, identified by the hash of that day's index line, which chains to every line before it

`index/` holds one JSON line-file per UTC day, each written once.
`index/2026/09/30.json` is the base line: `day` 2026-09-30, `prev` null, `files` the 12 base files' relpaths, each mapped to the sha256 of its bytes, `holes` empty, and the set's notes — the seam, the source of the base (`ohlc-15m-20261007` and its `set_sha256`), the vwap change.
Every day from 2026-10-01 has `index/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>.json`: `day`, `prev` (the sha256 of the previous day's line's bytes), `files` (each of that day's files, relpath to the sha256 of its bytes) and `holes` (D6), and, while the owner's close of a pair is in force, `notes` recording that close (D6); `files` and `holes` together name the 12 pairs exactly.
A pin is an as-of day D; its identity is the sha256 of D's line's bytes, which commits through `prev` to every earlier line and through each line to every byte it lists; the set at D is the base plus every day file listed in the lines up to D, and an as-of of 2026-09-30 pins the base alone.
The registry: a run that reads the set through `ObservedReader` records, for `ohlc-rolling`, the one file `index/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>.json` and the sha256 of its bytes, with the rows and span it returned, summed and widened once per pair and grid it read, as today's are once per file (`cli/registry/observed.py:120-126`); `dataset_hash` stays `compute_hash(datasets)` (`cli/registry/store.py:171`, `cli/registry/record.py:35-36`); the schema-4 checks (`record.py:208-216`) and the disk conformance pass (`tests/test_registry_conformance.py:59-80`) accept such a block unchanged, since it is a relpath under the set and the sha256 of a file's bytes.
A trial's `dataset_hash` is therefore `compute_hash({"ohlc-rolling": {"files": {"index/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD>.json": <its sha256>}, "rows": <rows returned>, "span": [<first>, <last>]}})`, beside any other dataset the run read.
A run that reads the set at two as-of days is refused, as a second window on one file is today (`observed.py:73-77`): one record, one as-of.
Proving a pin reproduces: `zcrypto data rolling verify --as-of D` walks from D's line down to the base line, checking that each line's bytes hash to its successor's `prev` and that every listed file's bytes hash to its listing, and prints D's line's sha256 and exits 0 — the reader comparing that sha256 with the pin — or names the first broken link and exits 1; the rolling read verifies every file it opens against its listing and the chain from D down before it returns a bar; the conformance pass walks the chain from every line a record cites.
The registry's existing pins do not move: nothing in this design writes to `ohlc-full`, `ohlc-15m`, `ohlc-holdout-2026-07-10` or the registry; the four legacy hashes the first 46 records carry and trial 47's 24 `ohlc-full` files (basis 4) resolve where they did, the inferred `data/ohlc-full daily` referent (`legacy-dataset-pins.jsonl:1`) and the reproduced pin's `data/ohlc-15m/manifest.json` (`tests/test_legacy_dataset_pins.py:14`, `:50-63`) stay where they are, and no existing citation resolves to `ohlc-rolling`.
Lost: today's recipe as it stands, the block listing every file read — correct, and no new code in the registry path, but a record's block grows with every pair and day read: 1,116 entries and 113,001 bytes for 12 pairs over one quarter of extension, 4,392 entries and 444,696 bytes over a year, against 193 bytes for a line pin and 2,258 bytes for trial 47's whole block (basis 4), on a committed file of one line per record.
Lost: per-day lines without `prev` — the same files, but a pin is a set and a day rather than one hash, and an earlier line edited in a local copy goes unseen by any check that reads only the head; `prev` costs one field.
Lost: one growing `manifest.json` — a file whose content changes every day, which `--ignore-existing` never transmits after its first copy (`cli/data/sync.py:81`, spec 00056 D1c), so it is ruled out by construction.
What this gives up, stated: spec 00086 D2 keeps the per-file hashes in the record "so the referent is recoverable from the record alone, with no side table" (`docs/specs/00086-verifiable-dataset-provenance-design.md:42`); here the index is that side table — content-addressed, so it cannot drift unseen, but a record whose set's index is lost from every copy no longer names its files; and the block no longer says which pairs a run read, which the subject's declaration does (`cli/research/subjects.py:72-75`).

### D3 — Ops authors the set through its outbox, and the NAS's hot pull carries it to the hub

The set has one author, the ops node, writing through its `hot-out` outbox (`/var/lib/zcrypto-ops/hot-out`, `zcrypto-data:zcrypto-hot`, mode 2775, empty today, basis 6), which the NAS already pulls into `hot/` with `--archive --ignore-existing` every `ARCHIVE_PULL_INTERVAL`, 3600 s by default (`infra/nas/pull-entrypoint.sh:196-202`, `infra/nas/compose.yaml:66-67`, `:81`).
The base and the base line are staged once into `hot-out/ohlc-rolling/` by `zcrypto data rolling base`, reading `ohlc-15m-20261007` from the hub's read-only mount — an attended act taken with the owner's word, since what lands on a hub that never overwrites is final, as spec 00099 took its hub conversion (`docs/specs/00099-manifest-contract-design.md:86`).
The research workstation and the ops node are one host, `zcrypto-ops` (`hostname`), which holds the checkout, `uv`, the data root and `hot-out` — `docs/reference/fleet.md:11` describes its container role and `:19` the same machine's research role — so the one-time base staging and the daily pass run here.
Every day after, a second step in the tape-bars runner (`infra/ansible/roles/ops/templates/tape-bars.sh.j2`), after `tick materialize` and in the same pinned image, runs `zcrypto data rolling extend`: for the day after the set's head, once each of the 12 pairs has a final in tape-bars, has passed the re-scan window without one, or is closed by the owner (D6), it copies the finals in, checks each against its tape-bars sidecar, and only then writes the day's line; no day's line is written before its predecessor's.
Nothing transient appears under the outbox: each file is written in a staging directory outside `hot-out` on the same filesystem and renamed in, because the pull pins `hot-out` alone (`infra/ansible/roles/ops/tasks/main.yml:327-332`) and a temporary name it copied would stay on the hub forever — tape-bars' own temporaries sit beside its finals (`cli/tick/materialize.py:126-130`), harmless there only because nothing pulls that tree.
The hub then carries the new files in `hot/ohlc-rolling/`, and `data fetch` carries them into `data/ohlc-rolling/` on each research node; `ohlc-rolling` is not in `zcrypto.toml`'s `authored_sets` (`zcrypto.toml:12`), so no `data push` writes it.
`data fetch`'s verification learns the set: today a fetched set whose parquet neither its manifest nor the vouched sidecar attests is refused (`cli/data/sync.py:206-216`), which `ohlc-rolling`, having no manifest, would be on its first fetch; it gains a chained arm beside `_attestations_for_set` (`:126-150`), whose hashes are frame-content grade (`_attestation_failure`, `:153-166`) where the index's are byte grade: a file a line names must hash, by its bytes, to that line's value, bound to its path; a day file no line names yet, pulled before its line, is reported pending and left unread, since every reader reaches files only through the index, and is compared when a later fetch brings its line; a line whose listed file has not arrived, pulled before that file, is reported pending the same way, the file compared against it when a later fetch brings it.
What carries the index: the same channel, each line a new file; the head is the newest line whose files are present, its chain then walked from it — a broken link refuses, never stepped past to an older line — so a pull interrupted between a day's files and its line, whichever arrived first, reads as the previous head, never as a short day.
The daily pass reads the head on the hub, `/mnt/zhao-crypto/hot/ohlc-rolling/index/`, over the read-only mount (`docs/reference/fleet.md:65`), with D7's check.
Head lag: day D is published at D+2 02:52Z at the earliest — the 26-hour settle (`cli/tick/materialize.py:78`) and the `:52` timer; 2026-10-07's finals at 2026-10-09T02:52:03Z (basis 2) — the line follows in the same run, the hub within the next pull.
The runner's comment that "the NAS re-acquires the output through its own rrsync pull" (`tape-bars.sh.j2:9-10`) describes no channel: the NAS compose names none for tape-bars and the ops node installs pull keys for liquidations, panel, reconciled and `hot-out` alone (`tasks/main.yml:306-332`); the runner's edit corrects the comment and no NAS copy of tape-bars' own tree is built, so the rolling set's day files become the only tape bars on the NAS.
Lost: the workstation authors it, `data rolling extend` then `data push` — the head moves only when someone runs it, and the owner's goal is a canonical that reaches the present unattended.
Lost: the materializer writes into the hub directly — the ops node never pushes (spec 00056 D2) and nothing writes through the NAS mount (`docs/reference/fleet.md:65`).
Lost: tape-bars' own tree published as the canonical — it holds the overlap's days, the partial first days and per-file sidecars, and has no base and no index.

### D6 — A day the tape refuses is a permanent hole the day's line names, and a read across it refuses unless the caller accepts it

None exists yet: 1,074 finals, every day of every pair from its first to 2026-10-07, `days_gap` 0, `days_unhealed` 0, errors 0 (basis 2).
`rolling extend` waits on day D while a pair's final is absent and D is still inside the materializer's re-scan window (`RESCAN_DAYS` 3, `cli/tick/materialize.py:79`, the window that turns an unpublished day into `days_gap`, `:193-201`), whose floor for a pair is `min(newest settled − RESCAN_DAYS, newest final + 1)` (`:199`): the window leaves D for good at the runner's default re-scan once the pair has a final dated `RESCAN_DAYS` + 1 days after D, its newest settled day being no earlier than its newest final, and then D's line names the pair under `holes` and the chain moves on — a heal and a hand-widened `--rescan-days` (`infra/runbooks/ops.md:122`) can still publish D into tape-bars afterwards, never into the set, whose line has named the hole (D8); a pair whose newest final is before D — its healer stalled, its capture stopped — is still retried by the materializer however late the clock, and holds the head.
A pair the tape stops carrying is closed only by the owner's explicit act, `zcrypto data rolling extend --close-pair <PAIR> --from <day> --owner-word <text> --owner-date <day>`, which writes that pair's days from `<day>` on as holes in each later line until a final reappears, or for good, each such line's `notes` recording the close with the owner's word and its date; the close is refused when it names a pair that is not one of the set's 12 as the base line spells them, since a line naming another pair breaks D2's rule that `files` and `holes` together name the 12 exactly, on a hub that never rewrites it, and when the pair has a final for `<day>` itself, never for a later one, which is that reappearance and ends the close at its own day, since a pair whose finals stop within `RESCAN_DAYS` days after its first day without one never carries that day past the window and leaves the head only by a close from it; the code never cuts a pair on its own, because the materializer retries a pair's missing day for as long as it is the pair's newest final + 1 (`cli/tick/materialize.py:199`) and the custody archive is kept forever (`docs/reference/data-catalog-full.md:3`), so an automatic cut after any number of days would write holes for days the tape would still publish after a long outage.
A hole is permanent: no later line or file fills it, and a dump that someday covers that day lives in its own stamped sibling, never spliced into the chain.
The set's holes are stricter than `days_gap`: the materializer counts only days its archive calendar holds (`materialize.py:152-154`, `:201`), so a pair-day with no trade segment at all is never a `days_gap`, while `rolling extend` walks the calendar for the set's 12 pairs and names a hole for every pair-day without a final once the pair's later final carries the day past the window, whatever the archive holds.
How a reader is told: a rolling read whose window covers a hole of a pair it reads refuses, naming the pair and the day; the caller accepts holes explicitly, `--accept-holes` on `research eval` and on `engine seed`, and the read then returns the frame without that day and the command prints each hole; the universe rebuild reads only the 30 daily bars its volume median takes, so a hole refuses it only while inside them, and a pair the head line records as closed is absent from its candidates for as long as the close runs, refusing it only as a mandatory pair below, the universe file's provenance naming the pair — a pair the owner has closed has no data and cannot be a candidate — until a final ends the close and the pair is a candidate again, its holes then refusing while inside those 30 days; a closed mandatory pair — BTC/EUR or ETH/EUR, the EUR leg of a `MANDATORY` base (`cli/universe/rules.py:18`, `:37-40`) — is the one close that refuses the rebuild, naming the pair, because the selection rule promises that every universe it publishes selects both mandatory legs — its override keeps a failing mandatory pair selected (`:75`, `:82`, pinned by `tests/test_universe_rules.py:43-49`), but only a pair among the candidates it is handed (`:58`), and `escalate` counts names alone (`:32-34`) — so a mandatory close could only publish a universe that breaks that promise unflagged, while a non-mandatory close completes with its pair absent, as the rule lets any non-mandatory pair be; the basket concordance pin (`tests/test_basket_concordance.py`), which any closed basket pair trips alike, does not set the two apart; and a closed BTC/EUR leaves ETH/BTC and SOL/BTC unpriceable in EUR as well (`cli/data/rebuild.py:200`, `:210`), which the refusal covers; the record's line pin commits to the holes with everything else.
A pending hole holds the head four days past the day's nominal publication when the pair's later days publish on time — D7's head check failing on the three or four daily passes between — and for as long as the materializer retries the day otherwise, which the same check reports and, once the day is 14 days past its nominal publication, escalates to the owner (D7).
Lost: a marker file per hole — a second record of what the line says. Lost: filling a hole from a later dump day — it would change what a pin committed to.

### D7 — The guards: one daily read, and tests each with a fixture it fails on
