# The bridgehead mirrors Caddy's apt repository with debmirror, amd64 and its one suite, serves the copy over public HTTPS through its own Caddy, installs its own Caddy from the copy on disk, and the edge nodes' Caddy source returns pointed at it, retiring the interim of 2026-10-11

## Problem

Cloudsmith's Caddy repository, `https://dl.cloudsmith.io/public/caddy/stable/deb/debian`, answered 402 five times in thirty days (the owner's word of 2026-10-11, carried in `1f13bef5b`'s message), and every apt refresh on a node that names it fails while it does: a converge, an Alloy wave's apt refresh, a monthly patch pass.
The interim merged in PR #687 (`3d54c79fd`) took the source off the three Caddy nodes — `zaccess` (the access role), `zcrypto-mon` and `zcrypto-hc` (the edge role) — with a `deb822_repository` `state: absent` (`infra/ansible/roles/access/tasks/main.yml:44-47`, `infra/ansible/roles/edge/tasks/caddy-source-absent.yml`), refused a node without Caddy before Debian's 2.6.2 could replace it (`access/tasks/main.yml:49-63`, `edge/tasks/main.yml:6-21`), and wrote the role-tagged-converge-first order into three runbook pages and the bump-alloy skill, and a rebuild stop into two of those pages and drill W3's preconditions; it froze Caddy at its installed version and left a fresh node no way to install it, both accepted "until the owner's debmirror replaces the source".
The owner did not converge the interim: the nodes still carry Cloudsmith's source today, and any role converge of the three before this change lands removes it, since the interim's removal is unconditional (`edge/tasks/main.yml:3-4`, `mon/tasks/main.yml:35-36`, `access/tasks/main.yml:44-47`).
The owner's word of 2026-10-11: "build an apt mirror for caddy's repository at zaccess using debmirror tailored for amd64 architecture and trixie only, https-exposing it via caddy … we introduce a bootstrapping deadlock here, because the mirror's endpoint is served by caddy which does not exist at a fresh host. If the resolution is trivial enough, apply it. Otherwise, i can do a manual installation when the next new zaccess host needs to be build (which likely will never happen)."
This spec settles the mirror's scope, its exposure, the key, the schedule and what a failed sync leaves, the nodes' source, the interim's retirement, the bootstrap and the rollout order; it supersedes the interim, whose every carrier this change re-trues or removes (D6).

## Decisions

### D1 — The scope: one suite, `amd64`, `main`, no source, the whole history

The repository has one suite, `any-version`: its `Release` names `Suite: any-version` and `Codename: any-version`, and no `trixie` suite exists, so "trixie only" is not expressible against it; it reduces to that one suite on `amd64`.
Nothing is lost by it: the 39 `caddy` records of `main/binary-amd64/Packages` carry no `Depends` or `Pre-Depends` field, so the package is the same file for every Debian release, and the three nodes run Debian 13.
The mirror is `debmirror --dist=any-version --section=main --arch=amd64 --nosource`: of the `Release`'s four components — `main multiverse restricted universe` — the other three list an empty `amd64` index (size 0, the empty file's sha256), and `main/binary-amd64/Packages` lists 39 records, every one `Package: caddy` and `Architecture: amd64`; that index list is the closed world the mirror copies, and the unit's argv, held by a test, is its enumeration.
The whole history stays: 39 versions, 2.3.0 to 2.11.7, 567,935,364 bytes (541.6 MiB), on a 25 GB root filesystem (`infra/runbooks/zaccess.md:118`); each release adds one `.deb` of about 18 MB (the last five 17,038,576 to 18,142,322 bytes).
Why: the copy keeps the vendor's signed `Release` byte for byte (D3), so its index names all 39 files whatever the pool holds; with every file present, every version apt lists is fetchable, and a rollback is `apt-get install caddy=<the previous version>` from the mirror.
Refused: an `--exclude` regex on the pool, or a prune after the sync — debmirror has no newest-only mode, and either leaves the signed index naming files the mirror answers 404 for, so a rollback by version fails and a version a node holds back is gone; and a re-signed, rewritten index that would list the kept files alone (D3 refuses re-signing).

### D2 — The exposure: public HTTPS at `apt.zaccess.zhaow.me`, the two apt trees alone

The bridgehead's Caddy gains a site, `apt.zaccess.zhaow.me`, with no `import mtls`: `@apt path /dists/* /pool/*` served by `file_server` from `/var/lib/caddy-apt-mirror/served` (no `browse`), every other path `respond 404`; its certificate is the same ACME HTTP-01 issue the two existing sites take on `:80` (`docs/reference/fleet.md:43`).
The name's `A` and `AAAA` records are a precondition the owner creates by hand in the `zaccess.zhaow.me` hosted zone, copies of the apex's, as the `tmux.` and `nas.` records are (`fleet.md:25`), a rollout slot.
Why public, in one line: the content is the vendor's own public, signed repository re-served byte for byte, and apt's signature check against the vendored key (D3) is what makes it trustworthy, not who may fetch it.
Refused: mTLS — a client leaf on `zcrypto-mon` and `zcrypto-hc` would be admitted by the shared `(mtls)` snippet's `verifier leaf` to the `tmux.` and `nas.` sites as well (`infra/ansible/roles/access/templates/Caddyfile.j2:6-18`, `:21`, `:26`), and its private key would sit on two nodes that hold no key to another host today (`fleet.md:17-18`); and a `remote_ip` allowlist of the two nodes — a rebuilt node on a new address would be refused the one install it needs.
The closed world: the site's routes are the three lines a test holds — the matcher, its handle, the 404 handle — so a third tree or a listing is a failing test.

### D3 — The key: the vendor's key, vendored as a binary keyring, never fetched at converge, never re-signed

`infra/ansible/files/caddy-archive-keyring.gpg` is the vendor's `gpg.key` dearmored: 5,024 bytes, sha256 `c17cd5298a0bab02fda439fff278d9a55df2120cf9dac790c6ce71930db90b37`, primary `65760C51EDEA2017CEA2CA15155B6D79CA56EA34` and signing subkey `2F5C3BE9886ACD2913299EFBABA1F9B8875A6661`, neither with an expiry; the armored source as fetched is 6,884 bytes, sha256 `783dfee04b19e851a928cd87b34710213ebbe7628f98d9f34595ab83be578c00`, and its text is carried in the plan, so no executor reaches the vendor for it.
Each of the three roles copies it to `/etc/apt/keyrings/caddy-archive-keyring.gpg` (`caddy_mirror_keyring` in `infra/ansible/group_vars/all/vars.yml`), where apt's `Signed-By` reads it on all three and `debmirror --check-gpg --keyring=` reads it on the bridgehead; a test holds the file to its sha256, and another the copy's path in the access and edge roles.
Binary, not armored: gpgv 2.4.7, which debmirror verifies with, reads no armored keyring (`invalid packet (ctb=2d)`, `No public key`, rc 2), and apt 3.0.3 reads a binary keyring in `Signed-By`; one file serves both.
The mirror keeps the vendor's `Release`, `Release.gpg` and, where debmirror copies it, `InRelease` as they are (a premise for the pin, below): `InRelease` and `Release.gpg` both verify with this keyring (gpgv, Good signature by `2F5C…6661`, made 2026-10-03 05:56 UTC), `InRelease`'s signed body is the `Release` byte for byte, and apt takes `Release` with `Release.gpg` where `InRelease` is absent (measured).
Refused: re-signing with a key of the fleet's — it would make the bridgehead a signing authority whose key must be guarded, for no gain over the vendor's signature; fetching the key from the vendor at converge — the outage this change answers would then fail the converge; the armored file as the vendored form.
Given up: a vendor key rotation fails every sync at the signature check until a change vendors the new key (D4 keeps the old copy served meanwhile).

### D4 — The schedule and a failed sync: daily, into a hard-linked copy exchanged with the served tree only when debmirror exits 0

`zaccess-caddy-mirror.service` (oneshot, as the system user `caddy-mirror`, `UMask=0022`, `ProtectSystem=strict` with the mirror directory its one writable path) runs `debmirror --method=https --host=dl.cloudsmith.io --root=public/caddy/stable/deb/debian --dist=any-version --section=main --arch=amd64 --nosource --check-gpg --keyring=/etc/apt/keyrings/caddy-archive-keyring.gpg --rsync-extra=none --diff=none --postcleanup --verbose /var/lib/caddy-apt-mirror/staging`; `zaccess-caddy-mirror.timer` starts it daily at 03:41 UTC with up to 30 minutes' random delay, `Persistent=true`, so a day the bridgehead was down syncs at its next start.
The mirror directory is `/var/lib/caddy-apt-mirror`, and the tree Caddy serves and the bridgehead's apt reads is `/var/lib/caddy-apt-mirror/served`, both mode `0755` and owned by `caddy-mirror`, so Caddy's user reads them; the vendor's host and path are named in the access role's defaults and in no other role file (a test, D6).
Daily, because the vendor releases about monthly and a security release the owner reads that day is pulled by hand: `systemctl start zaccess-caddy-mirror.service` (the runbook section, D9).
A failed sync leaves the previous copy served, by a two-directory swap, the amendment this decision named for a refuted premise. debmirror 2.47 does not hold a new index back when a pool file fails: it moves the new `Release`, `InRelease` and `Packages.gz` into `dists/` and runs `--postcleanup` with no check of its error count, then exits 1 (the measured basis), so no sync writes the served tree. The unit's `ExecStartPre` removes the last staging tree and hard-links a copy of `/var/lib/caddy-apt-mirror/served` to `/var/lib/caddy-apt-mirror/staging` (`cp -al`), `ExecStart` is debmirror over the copy, and `ExecStartPost`, which systemd runs only after `ExecStart` exits 0, exchanges the two trees in one `mv -T --exchange` (coreutils 9.7, `renameat2`'s exchange). debmirror writes each file it fetches or moves by unlinking and re-creating it, so a write to the copy reaches no file of the served tree; its trace file under `project/`, which the site does not serve, is the one it rewrites in place. A failed sync leaves the served tree as the last sync that passed, and its own tree in staging until the next sync's start removes it.
A client is never left on a half-written index for long: the vendor's `Release` carries no `Valid-Until`, so a copy days old keeps verifying; a client that fetches its index files across an exchange reads a hash mismatch and retries at its next refresh.
The vendor's `Release` says `Acquire-By-Hash: yes`; whether debmirror copies the `by-hash` trees does not matter: apt 3.0.3 reading a copy without them asks the `by-hash` path, takes the 404, and falls back to the plain name (measured), so the nodes' source sets no `By-Hash` option.
Refused: debmirror's staging alone, the first design — after a pool file fails it serves an index naming a `.deb` the pool lacks until a later sync passes, and a first sync that failed so leaves a `Release` the converge reads as a complete copy; a symlink renamed over the served path, the swap this decision first named — `mv -T --exchange` keeps both names real directories, so neither Caddy nor apt resolves a link and no step chooses which of two trees to sync; an hourly sync — 24 vendor reads a day for a monthly release.
Given up: a pool file the vendor's index names and its pool answers `404` for fails every sync on that file until the vendor serves it, so the served tree stays at the last sync that passed and every later release stays off the nodes; the section reads that `404` in the sync's journal and sends it to the owner the day it is read (D9).

### D5 — The nodes' Caddy source: the mirror, Debian's own `caddy` pinned out, a mirror that does not answer refused before the source is written

`zcrypto-mon` and `zcrypto-hc` (the edge role's new `caddy-source.yml`, in place of `caddy-source-absent.yml`): a `deb822_repository` named `caddy`, `uris: https://apt.zaccess.zhaow.me`, suite `any-version`, component `main`, `signed_by` the keyring path; the same name keeps the one file `/etc/apt/sources.list.d/caddy.sources` (`deb822_repository.py`'s `make_sources_filename`), so the converge rewrites Cloudsmith's source in place, or writes it where an interim converge removed it.
Before the source, a read of `https://apt.zaccess.zhaow.me/dists/any-version/Release` (`ansible.builtin.uri`, `HEAD`, `check_mode: false`) and an assert that it answered 200: a node whose mirror does not answer is refused in the preview of a converge that selects the role — role-tagged or un-tagged — before the source, the key, the pin or Alloy's pin file move; a `--tags alloy` converge selects neither mon's include of the file nor hc's edge include, so it reads no mirror: while the mirror does not answer, its preview passes and its apt refresh fails on the source, and the read before it is the pages' (D9); the read names `Release`, which debmirror always copies, not `InRelease`, which it may not.
The mon role writes the source before its first apt refresh, as the interim wrote the removal there (`mon/tasks/main.yml:35-36`, its `include_role` of the edge's file); the hc role's edge include already precedes its first refresh (`hc/tasks/main.yml:152`, `:185-190`).
On all three, `/etc/apt/preferences.d/caddy` pins `Package: caddy`, `Pin: release o=Debian`, `Pin-Priority: -1`: Debian's 2.6.2-12+deb13u1, from `trixie/main` and `trixie-security` alike, is never a candidate, so the mirror's is the one apt installs, and a node whose mirror held no Caddy fails its install loudly rather than taking Debian's — the interim's refusal's property, kept without refusing a fresh node (measured: with the pin and the mirror's lists the candidate is 2.11.7, Debian's at -1; without the mirror's lists the candidate is `(none)`).
The edge role's pre-interim preview pair returns: `edge_repo_previewed` (`ansible_check_mode and edge_caddy_repo is changed`) skips the install in a preview the source would change — a fresh node, or the first converge onto the mirror, whose unrefreshed cache lacks the mirror's lists — and `edge_units_previewed` reads it beside the install; it is the gate the mon role carried as `mon_repos_previewed` until `4f2f7f8ce` moved its Caddy into the edge role on 2026-10-05, and drill W3's must-hold "the preview passed on the fresh host" (`docs/reference/drill-log.md:129`) read that gate on a fresh `zcrypto-mon` on 2026-10-04.
Refused: no preflight, leaving the refresh to fail — the preview passes (apt refreshes no cache in check mode) and the real pass fails after the source and, on mon, after Alloy's pin file has moved; a `By-Hash: no` source option (D4); a version pin on Caddy — it stays followed from apt, never forced or held.

### D7 — The bootstrap: the bridgehead installs its own Caddy from its copy on disk — trivial, applied

A fresh bridgehead has no Caddy, so it cannot serve itself the mirror over HTTPS; it does not need to: the access role's order is debmirror and gpgv from Debian main → the key → the user, the directory and its served tree, the two units, the timer → a read of `/var/lib/caddy-apt-mirror/served/dists/any-version/Release` → the first sync, `systemctl start zaccess-caddy-mirror.service`, only while that `Release` is absent, as it is until a sync has passed (D4), run as that command, which joins a sync already running and fails when the sync fails, where the systemd module's `state: started` returns at once on a unit already activating (`ansible/modules/systemd_service.py:292-293`, `:557-558`) → the Caddy source as a `file:` source over the served tree (`uris: file:/var/lib/caddy-apt-mirror/served`, the same suite, component and `Signed-By`) → D5's pin → `caddy present`; its Caddy then serves that tree to the other two.
Verified: apt 3.0.3 over a deb822 `file:` source with `Signed-By` a binary keyring verifies the copy's `InRelease` (it runs `sqv --keyring <keyring> … --policy-as-of 2027-10-11`, rc 0) and offers 2.11.7; with another keyring it refuses the source as not signed (rc 100); with `InRelease` absent it verifies `Release` with `Release.gpg`; with one byte of `Packages` changed it refuses a hash mismatch (rc 100) — all over the vendor's own index files, read locally.
So the deadlock reduces to this: a fresh bridgehead needs the vendor's repository reachable once, for its first sync.
In check mode the chain holds on the established bridgehead, the rollout's case: the timer and the first sync are skipped by `access_caddy_mirror_previewed` (`ansible_check_mode and access_caddy_mirror_units is changed`), the timer because systemd refuses a unit a preview has not written, in any mode (`ansible/modules/systemd_service.py:509`, `:549`, `ansible/module_utils/service.py:106-117`, ansible-core 2.21.4), and the first sync, a command ansible runs in no preview (`ansible/modules/command.py:333`, `:342-344`), with it; the template and copy tasks change nothing in a preview and never reach the module's missing-directory check (`ansible/plugins/action/copy.py:293`); the `file:` source's `deb822_repository` needs `python3-debian` in a preview (`deb822_repository.py:540-542`), which the bridgehead holds since its `--tags alloy` converge of 2026-10-07 ran `alloy_apt`'s Grafana source with `install_python_debian: true`; and `caddy present`, already installed there, reads as unchanged.
On a fresh bridgehead the preview stops before the chain, at three tasks the access role carries today — `probe timer enabled` (`access/tasks/main.yml:19-20`), `wireguard tunnel enabled` (`:30-31`), `ssh relay socket enabled` (`:41-42`), each enabling a unit a preview has not written — before and after this change alike; the chain's own fresh-host gaps (the Caddy install without the mirror's lists, the Caddy unit) sit behind them and are not gated here, since a gate nothing can reach is untested code.
Verdict: trivial, applied — the deadlock is resolved in the real pass by the `file:` source, and no Caddy is installed by hand; what a fresh bridgehead's first converge lacks is a passing preview, a gap older than this change and outside it, left on the owner's answer to question 1.

### D8 — The rollout order: the bridgehead first, and the window between the merge and the last node

The mirror is synced and served before mon or hc points at it: R3 converges `zaccess` (`--tags access`, the first sync inside it), R4 reads the site from the workstation, R5 and R6 converge `zcrypto-mon` (`--tags mon`) and `zcrypto-hc` (`--tags hc -e hc_image_tag=<the pinned tag>`); D5's preflight refuses mon or hc in its preview while the mirror does not answer, so a wrong order changes nothing.
The merge is taken in a sitting that takes R3 to R6 after it, once R0's read of the vendor has passed immediately before it, so the window D6 names closes in that sitting, and a stop before R6 goes to the owner there. From the merge until R3 has passed, a `--tags access` converge of the bridgehead — R3, a revocation (`zaccess-revoke-client-cert` step 2), a relay's reopen — refreshes apt over the bridgehead's Cloudsmith source and runs the first sync from the vendor in its real pass, before the pinned leaves and the Caddyfile, so in that window a revocation waits on the vendor, and the rollout takes no access converge but R3. From the merge to R6, no `--tags alloy` converge of the three runs: before its role-tagged converge a node may still carry Cloudsmith's source, on which Alloy's apt refresh fails while the vendor is out (`infra/runbooks/hc.md:302`'s own reading); and no Alloy wave is open (`alloy-version.py off-fleet` reads `0` today), since each role-tagged converge lands the committed Alloy.
The monthly patch passes fall due on 2026-11-04 (mon) and 2026-11-07 (hc), a month after their last un-tagged converges, 2026-10-04T19:27:11Z and 2026-10-07T16:11:06Z in the committed `docs/reference/deploy-log.jsonl`; the rollout lands before them. No pass is taken on mon from the merge until R5, nor on hc until R6: the merged pass starts the mirror's unit, which R3 writes, and upgrades by hand before its converge reads the mirror, so a pass taken earlier changes the node and stops half done; a pass before the merge runs the interim's removal, which R5 or R6 later overwrites with the mirror's source.
The bridgehead's `--tags access` converge reopens a cut SSH relay (`zaccess-cut-ssh-relay` step 3): R3 is taken outside a relay cut, or the cut is taken again after it.
None of the three is venue-facing (`grep -rli kraken infra/ansible/roles/*/` names `capture`, `engine` and `ops`), so no Kraken maintenance window bounds the rollout.

The rebuild procedures (`hc.md:37`, `mon.md:100`, `drills-telemetry.md:656`) read that the mirror answers before a rebuild, in place of the interim's stop, another answer sending the reader to the section. The Alloy-dark re-converges of mon and hc (`mon.md:274`, `hc.md:332`) and the bump skill's edge-node step, its *Rollback* included, read the same before a `--tags alloy` converge of either node, since that converge selects no preflight (D5).

### D10 — What this spec does not do

- No architecture but `amd64`, no suite but `any-version`, no component but `main`, no source packages: the three nodes are `amd64` Debian 13.
- No re-signing (D3), no rewritten index, no prune (D1).
- No mirror of Grafana's, Docker's or Debian's repositories: Grafana's source has not failed this way, and each would be a decision of its own.
- No alert on the mirror's age or on the `apt.` certificate, dropped on the owner's answer to question 2: a stale copy is the last sync that passed (D4), Caddy held at that version and nothing broken, read monthly by the patch passes and on demand (D9); the certificate is renewed by Caddy like the other two, and an expired one refuses mon's and hc's role-tagged and un-tagged converges in their preview at the mirror read, and fails the pages' read before a `--tags alloy` converge (D5).
- No preview of a fresh bridgehead (D7), dropped on the owner's answer to question 1.

## Invariants

- No apt source on the three nodes names the vendor's repository; the sync is its one reader — held by `test_the_vendors_repository_is_named_by_the_mirrors_upstream_alone` over every `.yml` and `.j2` under `infra/ansible/roles/`.
- apt on the three verifies what the mirror serves against the vendored key, and the mirror serves the vendor's signed files unchanged — held by the source tests (`signed_by` the keyring path), the keyring's sha256 test, and R4's byte comparison of the served `Release` with the vendor's.
- The served tree's pool holds every file its index names — a sync reaches the served tree only when debmirror exits 0 (D4), with no exclude in the unit's argv; a test holds both.
- A node's Caddy candidate is the mirror's; Debian's is never one — held by the pin's content test in both roles.
- A failed sync leaves the previous copy served — the exchange after debmirror exits 0 (D4), held by the unit's test.
- mon and hc write no Caddy source while the mirror does not answer — held by the preflight's test (statuses 200 admitted; 404, 402, -1 refused; read, refusal, key, source in that order).
- The mirror serves `/dists/*` and `/pool/*` alone, without a listing, without mTLS, and the `tmux.` and `nas.` sites keep theirs — held by the two Caddyfile tests, and read on the bridgehead's own Caddy by the rollout's R4.

## What stays manual

- The DNS record `apt.zaccess.zhaow.me`, created once by the owner in Route53.
- The rollout's converges, each attended (D8).
- A sync before the daily one, and the reading of a failed one (D9).
- A vendor key rotation: a new key vendored by a change of its own.

## Lost

What the interim gave up that returns: Caddy upgrades on the three nodes, held at their installed versions while the source was off — mon's and hc's by their monthly passes, the bridgehead's by `zaccess-caddy-mirror`'s upgrade step (D9); a fresh `zcrypto-mon` or `zcrypto-hc`, which the interim's refusal stopped after Grafana and Loki were installed, installs Caddy again, so drill W3 and the dead-man rebuild run; a Caddy security release taken the day it is read, on each of the three; the monthly passes' Caddy upgrade lines mean what they say.
What this design gives up:
- A node's Caddy now depends on the bridgehead: while `zaccess` does not answer, mon's and hc's role-tagged and un-tagged converges refuse in their preview, a `--tags alloy` converge of either passes its preview and fails at Alloy's apt refresh, and every apt refresh on them errors on the Caddy source — a dependency on the fleet's own VPS, watched by `zcrypto-alloy-dark-zaccess`, in place of one on a vendor that failed five times in thirty days; an Alloy wave or an Alloy-dark re-converge on mon or hc reads the mirror first and waits for the bridgehead (D9).
- Upgrades lag the vendor by up to a day, or a hand sync.
- A public site anyone may read: 568 MB of `.deb` files against the bridgehead's transfer allowance, and a name in the certificate transparency logs.
- The interim's named refusal of a node without Caddy: its replacement is apt's own "no installation candidate" under the pin, a plainer message.
- 568 MB on the bridgehead's disk, growing about 18 MB a release.
- `/etc/apt/keyrings/caddy.asc`, which the module fetched for Cloudsmith's source on each node, stays there, referenced by no source: apt trusts a keyring under `/etc/apt/keyrings/` only through a `Signed-By`, so it is inert.
- A fresh bridgehead's preview (D7), as before.
