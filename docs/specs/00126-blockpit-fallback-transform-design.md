# Kraken's ledger and trades exports become Blockpit manual-import rows: an offline, deterministic transform that books the conversion pairs and splits each close's fee from its PnL, into a manual depot that replaces the connector's

## Problem

Rung 1's T2 probe failed two of Step 7's five criteria (`infra/runbooks/engine-procedures.md:341`; the verdict is `[iter-172]`, `docs/research/14.phase6-decisions.md:512-515`; the measured import table is `docs/reference/blockpit-t1-memo.md:12-21`): Blockpit's Kraken connector drops the `collateralconversion` pairs Kraken books to charge a margin fee in EURC, so EURC runs negative and EUR reads high; and it imports a margin close with PnL as one Margin Profit row carrying the fee, which Blockpit capitalises into the received EUR, where it reaches no summed figure.
Master plan §11's T3 (`docs/research/00.master-plan.md:298`) is the fallback: a deterministic, unit-tested pre-transform of Kraken's ledger and trades exports into Blockpit's manual-import file with explicit margin rows, run monthly as the bookkeeping of :299; before the sleeve exceeds 25 %, T2 passes or T3 is deployed (:397).
`docs/open-topics/T0215-blockpit-fallback-pre-transform.md` states the requirement: the transform reproduces the memo's table row for row and changes two shapes — a conversion pair becomes a Trade EUR → EURC at the pair's amounts, and a `margin` row with a non-zero amount becomes a Margin Profit (or Loss) at the amount plus its own Margin Fee row — into a depot that, Blockpit computing FIFO per integration (`blockpit-t1-memo.md:27`), carries the whole history.
The owner's export of 2026-07-01 to 2026-10-09 (the measured basis) holds shapes the memo's window did not: a close at a loss charged in EURC with a conversion pair of its own, Auto Earn's reward credits, a small-balance conversion, a crypto deposit, and a trade the ledger booked one leg of.
This spec settles the input, the depot, the mapping of every row type that export holds, the position link, determinism and provenance, the command, the monthly procedure, the tests, and what the owner does once it is built; the owner's rulings of 2026-10-09 close it, and the plan follows them.

## Decisions

### D1 — The input: Kraken's ledger CSV export joined to its trades CSV export; the ledger decides every row

The input is the pair of CSV exports Kraken serves for one window, requested with `kraken export-report` and fetched with `kraken export-retrieve` on the workstation — the files D7 archives raw: the ledger (`txid,refid,time,type,subtype,aclass,subclass,asset,wallet,amount,fee,balance,amountusd,feeusd,balanceusd,feecurrency`) and the trades (`txid,ordertxid,pair,aclass,subclass,time,type,ordertype,price,cost,fee,vol,margin,misc,ledgers,posttxid,posstatuscode,cprice,ccost,cfee,cvol,cmargin,net,trades`).
The ledger is the source of every output row: its `time`, `type`, `subtype`, `asset`, `amount` and `fee` decide each row's label, assets and amounts; `wallet` and `feecurrency` are checked (D3's refusals); `balance` is the per-asset chain D5 checks; `amountusd` is read for a small-balance conversion alone (D3).
The trades export is read for three things: the `pair` of a trade whose ledger booked one leg (D3), the position a margin-family row belongs to (`misc`, `posttxid`; D4), and the cross-check that the two files describe the same activity (`ledgers`).
The join key is the ledger's `refid` against the trades' `txid`: on the sample, every one of the 100 ledger rows of types `trade`, `margin`, `rollover`, `settled` and `collateralconversion` has a `refid` naming a trades row whose `ledgers` column names that row's `txid`, and the trades export's `ledgers` columns name those 100 rows and no other; the 13 rows no trade names — `deposit`, `earn`, and the `spend`/`receive` rows — carry refids naming no trade and need nothing from the trades file.
The cross-check refuses a `trade`, `margin`, `settled` or `collateralconversion` row whose `refid` names no trades row, and a trades row none of whose `ledgers` ids is in the ledger export; a `rollover` row is exempt, its `refid` naming the position's opening trade, which a month boundary puts in an earlier export; and since an opening trade's `ledgers` lists its rollover rows (both opens that rolled, on the sample), a trades row may name ledger ids past the window's end, so one present id is enough.
One export covers the whole history, so a window is one export and nothing is stitched: on 2026-10-09 a ledger export requested from `starttm` 2024-01-01 came back over its 2.8-year window byte-identical to the export from 2026-07-01 (the same sha256, `.tmp/spec-00126/span-probe/`); Kraken's support article on exporting account history states no maximum period, so what is shown is a request far past the account's history served whole, not the absence of every cap above it; and an export expires 14 days after its creation (`expiretm` − `createdtm`) — the orchestrator's readings, each in the measured basis. D5's `--after` chain is the monthly cadence's mechanism, never a stitching one; a consolidation step would be owed only if Kraken introduced a span cap, as the owner's previous exchange had at 90 days.
Alternatives weighed:
- The API's shape, `kraken ledgers` and `kraken trades-history` — the read-only key's view. Lost: the ledger arrives 50 rows a page (the sample's `ledgers.json`: `count` 113, 50 rows), under other spellings (`ledger_type` for `type`, `staking` where the export writes `earn`/`reward`, `XDG` where it writes `DOGE`, pairs as `XXBTZEUR` where it writes `BTC/EUR`), and the CLI's trades rows carry no `ledgers` field, so no trade names its ledger rows; a pull is no artefact Kraken serves for the bookkeeping to archive; and a transform that fetched would hold a key and reach the venue rather than be a function of two files. The tree's one ledger reader already reads the CSV export's header names (`_LEDGER_COLUMNS`, `cli/engine/tracking.py:426`), not the API's.
- Kraken's PDF statements: four decimals and no `refid` (`blockpit-t1-memo.md:41`).
- Kraken's ledger CSV uploaded to Blockpit's Kraken integration, which Blockpit's Kraken page accepts: that is Blockpit's own mapping, the connector's, with both failures, and the same page lists the small-balance conversion as unsupported.
- The ledger alone: it decides every label, but a trade it booked one leg of names no counterpart asset, and the position link and the cross-check exist only with the trades export.
Ruled 2026-10-09 (Ruling 1): the two CSV exports, the ledger deciding every row.

### D2 — The depot: a fresh manual integration carrying the whole history replaces the connector's

The transform's first run covers the account's whole history, from the window holding its first row (the deposit `T0215` dates), and its output is uploaded into a new Blockpit manual integration; each later month's output is uploaded into the same integration (D7). The connector's integration is not touched by this change: hiding or deleting it is the topic's decision step, after the re-read (D9).
What replacing means for the five criteria: FIFO runs per integration, so the conversion pairs' EURC lots and the Margin Fee rows that spend them sit in one integration and EURC never runs negative (*no phantom balances*); each close's fee is a row of its own (*fees attached as costs*); the rows the connector labelled re-enter under D3's labels, which for every row type the memo measured are the connector's own, so *bucket assignment*, *FIFO lots intact* and *the disposal's gain off the settle's basis*, which passed, are expected to read as they did and are re-read all the same (D9).
What it means for the historical rows: the connector's depot and the 2026 Steuerbericht taken from it (`blockpit-t1-memo.md:3`) stay as the evidence of `[iter-172]`; a label the owner set by hand in the connector's depot does not carry over — `[iter-172]` rejected hand edits (`14.phase6-decisions.md:515`), and the crypto deposit's label there is unread (Ruling 3); the connector's automatic sync gives way to a monthly upload.
Alternatives weighed:
- Supplement the connector's depot with the rows it drops, in a separate manual integration. Lost: FIFO per integration leaves EURC negative in the connector's integration, which still spends EURC its own rows never received, and the fee split cannot be supplemented — a Margin Fee row added beside the connector's Margin Profit row, which already carries the fee, debits the fee twice.
- Supplement into the connector's own integration, which Blockpit's upload path allows (the upload icon beside an integration's Sync). Fixes the conversion pairs, not the split: that needs the connector's row edited, the hand edit `[iter-172]` option 3 rejected for its scale, its missing record and the re-sync over it; Blockpit's upload article asks that synced timeframes be removed before an upload overlaps them; and Blockpit's Kraken page now lists "Margin Trades as PnL" in its import scope (updated the week of 2026-10-09), so what the connector imports may change under a supplement.
Ruled 2026-10-09 (Ruling 2): replace.

### D3 — The row mapping: every type the export holds, mapped or refused

Conventions for every output row: an amount is the export's own decimal string with its sign dropped, never a float's rendering, except a small-balance conversion's allocation below; a fee is the row's `fee` in its `asset`, entered beside the amount as Blockpit's Trade and Staking articles describe, so the asset moves by the incoming amount less the fee or by the outgoing amount plus it — Kraken's own form, where `balance` moves by `amount` − `fee` (the chain D5 checks); the date is the ledger's `time`, UTC, rewritten `dd.mm.yyyy HH:MM:SS`, the template's own format (its `Date (UTC)` column, number format `dd.mm.yyyy hh:mm:ss`); the decimal separator is `.` throughout, as the upload article asks one separator; the `Trx. ID` is the Kraken id the row derives from — the `refid` for a row built from a group, the ledger `txid` for a row built from one ledger row or from one spend of a small-balance conversion (its spends share one `refid`), `<txid>-fee` for a fee split off it — unique across the output (D5); the `Comment` names the Kraken `type`/`subtype`, the trade's `pair` where the trades export names one, a small-balance conversion's `refid`, and `position <opening trade id>` on a margin-family row (D4).
The output's header is the template's first row, `Date (UTC),Integration Name,Label,Outgoing Asset,Outgoing Amount,Incoming Asset,Incoming Amount,Fee Asset (optional),Fee Amount (optional),Comment (optional),Trx. ID (optional)`; `Integration Name` carries one constant, which an upload into an existing integration ignores; every `Label` is one of the 33 values of the template's `Label` dropdown — the data validation on `C2:C986` of its one sheet (the measured basis) — and no other, the mapping using 7 of them: Trade, Deposit, Non-Taxable In, Staking, Margin Profit, Margin Loss and Margin Fee. The template's own example rows put Margin Profit, Staking and Deposit on the incoming side and Margin Loss and Margin Fee on the outgoing side with no fee, as the table does. The template and its first sheet's CSV are committed as the tests' reference (D8).

A refusal names the ledger `txid`s, the `type`/`subtype` and the reason; a run with any refusal writes nothing and exits 1 (D5). The shapes refused beside an unknown `type` or `subtype`: a `wallet` other than `spot / main` (the sample's one wallet); a non-zero `fee` whose `feecurrency` is not the row's `asset` (on the sample it is the asset on every fee'd row and empty on every other); a `trade` group of more than two rows, or of two rows not one negative and one positive, or of one asset twice, or with a fee on both legs; a one-row `trade` group whose asset is neither side of its trades row's `pair`; a `collateralconversion` group that is not EUR out and EURC in; a `settled` group not two rows of opposite sign; a group whose rows carry different `time`s (each group on the sample shares one); a small-balance conversion without exactly one `receive`, or whose spends' `amountusd` sum to zero; the cross-check refusals of D1; and every row type Kraken documents that no export has shown here — `withdrawal`, `transfer`, `adjustment`, `staking`, `sale`, `invite bonus`, `earn` under `allocation`, `deallocation`, `autoallocate` or `migration` — whose mapping is written when an export first carries one (Ruling 7).
A row moving nothing — `amount` 0 and `fee` 0 — whose `type` and `subtype` the table maps writes no Blockpit row and is counted in the provenance (D5), never dropped unrecorded; one whose `type` or `subtype` the table does not map is refused as above, moving nothing or not; the sample holds none.
Measured on the sample with a throwaway simulation of this table outside the tree (2026-10-09): 113 ledger rows become 72 Blockpit rows — Trade 46, Margin Fee 13, Staking 8, Margin Profit 2, Margin Loss 1, Non-Taxable In 1, Deposit 1 — every `Trx. ID` unique, and for each of the 13 assets the rows' incoming less outgoing less fee equals the ledger's sum of `amount` − `fee`, exactly.
Alternatives weighed for the margin rows: Blockpit's preferred chain for spot margin — Receive Loan, Margin Trade, Repay Loan (its Margin Trade article and its trading-bot import guide). Lost: Kraken's ledger books no loan and no position-asset movement for an open or for a close by an opposing order, only the PnL and the fees, so the chain's amounts would be built from the trades export's `cost`, `vol` and `margin` and reconcile to no ledger row; Blockpit's Margin articles warn against mixing the chain with Margin Profit/Loss/Fee in one position or platform; and *bucket assignment*, which passed on the connector's Margin Profit, would be re-decided.
The four sub-decisions, as ruled 2026-10-09:
- The crypto deposit (Ruling 3): Blockpit's unlabeled incoming type values the asset at market on arrival until it is labelled, and lets Blockpit merge it into a Transfer with the owner's outgoing withdrawal in another integration, which carries the original acquisition date and cost (Blockpit's Unlabeled and Transfer articles); Non-Taxable In fixes the basis at market on arrival and the holding period there. Material on the sample: the deposited coin was sold on the same day, so its gain turns on that basis. Ruled: `Deposit`, the owner labelling it in Blockpit from what they know of its origin.
- Rewards (Ruling 4): Staking (Blockpit's Kraken page lists Kraken's earn rewards with Staking among their kinds), with the gross `amount` incoming and Kraken's commission as its fee — Blockpit's Staking article's second form — so the commission is on the record; the alternative, the net amount alone, loses it; Other Income or Interest are the other labels Blockpit offers for an incoming credit. Ruled: Staking, gross with the fee.
- The small-balance conversion (Ruling 5): the receive is one amount for all spent coins, and a Trade has one asset a side, so the receive is shared out; `amountusd`, Kraken's own valuation of each row, is the only per-coin value the export carries, and on the sample every share is non-zero at ten decimal places and the shares sum to the receive exactly. Lost: one Trade against the largest spend with the rest at a zero incoming (Blockpit's acceptance of a zero amount is unread), and a refusal (the owner's own history holds a sweep, so a whole-history run would never pass). Ruled: the `amountusd` allocation.
- The one-leg trade (Ruling 6): writing the absent leg at 0 keeps every asset's balance equal to Kraken's ledger, which is what *no phantom balances* reads; the trades row's `cost` as the absent leg gives the lot its true cost but leaves a sub-precision EUR difference against the ledger; merging the leg into another fill of the same order (the sample's has one) fails at a month boundary that separates them. Ruled: the absent leg is written at 0; the template's file says nothing of a zero amount, so the first import reads whether Blockpit takes the row (a rollout reading), and if it does not, the row becomes a refusal by a change of its own and the owner rules again — no silent second choice.

### D4 — Position linking: the trades export carries the link, and no row depends on it

On the sample, the ledger alone links a position's open-side rows — its opening `margin` row, its `collateralconversion` pair and its `rollover` rows carry `refid` = the opening trade id — but not its close: a close's `margin` row, a settle's `settled` pair, and a close's own `collateralconversion` pair (the EURC-charged loss) carry the closing trade's id.
The trades export supplies the link: each of the 5 closing rows (`misc` holding `closing`) carries `posttxid` = its opening trade's `txid`, a row of the same export; each of the 5 opening rows carries `posstatuscode` `closed` and `trades` naming its one closing row; the 43 rows that close nothing carry one and the same `posttxid` value, which names no row of the export. The CSV column is `posttxid`; the API's field is `postxid`.
What the transform does with it: the `Comment` of every margin-family row carries `position <opening trade id>` — the `refid` for an open-side row; for a row whose trades row is a closing row, that row's `posttxid` — and the provenance lists each position's rows (D5). No Blockpit row's label, asset or amount depends on the link: Margin Profit, Loss and Fee are attached to no lot (`blockpit-t1-memo.md:17-19`), and an open's fee and a zero-PnL close's fee map alike.
When the link cannot be made: a closing row whose opening trade lies in an earlier export still names it, and that is no refusal; a closing row whose `posttxid` is empty is refused, as an unmeasured shape; a `margin` row whose trades row is missing is D1's cross-check refusal.
Alternatives weighed: the ledger alone, with a close standing unlinked as a bare Margin Profit or Loss — the rows would be the same, but the audit trail would not name which position a close ended; the open's `trades` column instead of the close's `posttxid` — it lies in the opening month's export, absent when the open precedes the window.
Ruled 2026-10-09 (Ruling 1): the link from `posttxid`, carried in the `Comment` and the provenance, never a precondition of a row.

### D5 — Determinism and provenance

The transform is a pure function of the bytes of its two inputs and, from the second run on, the previous run's provenance: no clock, no network, no environment variable reaches its output.
Arithmetic is `decimal.Decimal` on the export's strings; the one output value computed rather than copied is the small-balance allocation, at ten decimal places, the widest the export carries (BTC, SOL, ETH, DOT and AVAX rows on the sample).
The output is a CSV — UTF-8 without a byte-order mark, `\n` line endings, `,` delimiter, minimal quoting, `.` as the decimal separator, the template's header row first — its rows sorted by `time`, then the position in the ledger export of the first row of the row's group — the export's own order, in which each asset's balance chain holds, since Kraken's `txid`s carry no booking order (the measured basis: a `txid` order puts one of the sample's five EURC-charged `margin` rows before the conversion pair that funds it) — then the row's ordinal inside the group (an amount row before its fee row, spends in `txid` order); the same inputs give byte-identical output, and the trades export's row order does not reach it.
Beside it the run writes `<out>.provenance.json`, keys sorted: each input's file name, sha256 and row count, and the ledger's first and last `time`; the output's file name, sha256 and row count; per label and asset, the row count and the sums of incoming, outgoing and fee; each asset's opening and closing balance; each position's rows; the count of rows moving nothing; the previous provenance's sha256 when one was given; and the `zcrypto` version. It carries no wall-clock time, so the same run writes the same file.
Checks, all before anything is written, each failure a refusal: the header carries every column D1 reads; every row is mapped (D3); the cross-check (D1); per asset, in the export's own row order, each row's `balance` equals the asset's previous `balance` plus `amount` less `fee` (the chain holds on all 100 non-first rows of the sample); per asset, the output's incoming less outgoing less fee equals the ledger's `amount` less `fee`; per asset, in the written order, each output row applied whole, the balance from its opening never runs below zero — an export interleaving two groups' rows inside one second, which the sample never shows (the measured basis), would otherwise list a debit before the credit that funds it, and like every shape no export has shown it refuses; every `Trx. ID` unique; and continuity — without `--after`, every asset's opening balance (`balance` − `amount` + `fee` on its first row) is zero, which a whole-history run reads (on the sample all 13 assets open at zero); with `--after <previous provenance>`, every asset's opening balance equals that provenance's closing balance (an asset it never saw opening at zero), and this ledger's first `time` is later than its last.
A run that refuses prints every refusal and writes nothing; a run whose `--out` or provenance path exists is refused, so no run overwrites an archived file.
Alternatives weighed: writing the mapped rows and listing the refused ones — a partial file imports as phantom balances, the failure this exists to remove; a timestamp in the provenance — two runs over the same inputs would differ; the engine's ledger reader (`read_ledger_export`, `cli/engine/tracking.py:444`) — it parses `amount` and `fee` as floats (:473-474) and reads none of `wallet`, `balance` or `amountusd`, so the exact checks above cannot run on it; continuity left to the operator — a month exported twice duplicates its rows in Blockpit, whose upload articles disagree on whether it deduplicates (D7), and a month skipped shows only as balances that stop reconciling.
Ruled 2026-10-09: `--after` is required on every run but the first (Ruling 8); the output is CSV alone (Ruling 9) — the owner read Blockpit's upload dialog that day offer csv and xlsx, so no spreadsheet writer is built, and whether its Blockpit Template tab parses the `.csv` is read at the first upload (D9).

### D6 — The command: `zcrypto tax blockpit`

`zcrypto tax blockpit --ledgers <csv> --trades <csv> --out <csv> [--after <provenance.json>]`: a new Typer app `tax` in `cli/tax/command.py`, wiring and exit codes only, as `cli/snapshot/command.py` is, registered in `cli/__main__.py` beside the others (`app.add_typer(tax_app, name="tax")`); the readers in `cli/tax/kraken_export.py`, the mapping, checks and writers in `cli/tax/blockpit.py`, the refusal type in `cli/tax/errors.py`.
Exit 0 when the output and its provenance are written, printing the per-label counts, each asset's closing balance and both paths with their sha256; 1 when anything is refused, printing each refusal and writing nothing; 2 on a usage error.
It reads two local files and writes two; it holds no key and reaches nothing, so no rule of `.claude/rules/fleet-deploys.md` or CLAUDE.md's secrets section touches it; it runs where the exports land, on the workstation, because `kraken` is workstation-only; no unit or timer runs it.
Its help strings carry no internal term — `tests/test_internal_terms_not_operator_visible.py` scans every string literal under `cli/` — and no file of it carries the string `kraken-cli`, which `infra/scripts/count-list.sh kraken-cli-on-infra-surfaces` counts in every non-Markdown file under `cli/` (`count-list.sh:257`).
`README.md` gains a `### zcrypto tax` section under `## Usage`, as `### zcrypto snapshot` has (:390-402): the command line, an option table, the exit codes and the refusal rule.
Alternatives weighed: a subcommand of `engine` — that app is the trading engine's (`cli/engine/command.py:96-98`), and this reads no journal and no engine state; of `data` — that app moves the hot cluster's datasets (`cli/data/command.py:18-20`); the name `books` — `tax` names the domain master plan §11 heads, and the one consumer is the tax tool.
Ruled 2026-10-09 (Ruling 11): `zcrypto tax blockpit`.

### D7 — The monthly bookkeeping: raw exports, the transform's output and its provenance, archived on the NAS

A new runbook page, `infra/runbooks/bookkeeping.md`, carries one PROCEDURE section, `kraken-monthly-bookkeeping`, in numbered steps, with no internal token in its text (`infra/runbooks/README.md`'s scope); a new top-level page, so its plan task loads `zcrypto-refine-rules` first, as that skill's description requires for one.
The custody path: `/volume1/ZhaoCrypto/kraken-statements/<start>_<end>/` on the NAS, one directory per export window named by its UTC start and exclusive end dates — the first run's window from 2026-07-01, each later one a calendar month — holding the two zips as the retrieve wrote them, the two CSVs they hold, the transform's output and its provenance. Written by `scp` without the `/volume1` prefix, never through the mount (`infra/runbooks/nas.md:233-240`, `docs/reference/fleet.md:65`); read back through the mount at `/mnt/zhao-crypto/kraken-statements/`; `nas.md`'s list of the archive's trees (:21) and its never-delete step (:43), and `fleet.md`'s list of the mount's trees (:66), name it.
The steps, per window: request the ledger and the trades export for the window with `kraken export-report --report ledgers` and `--report trades` from the window's `--starttm` to its `--endtm` (the flags its `--help` lists, the measured basis), each answering a report id; once `kraken export-status --report <report>` reads each `Processed`, and before its `expiretm`, fetch it with `kraken export-retrieve --output-file data/kraken-statements/<start>_<end>/<report>.zip <id>`; accept each zip when `zipfile`'s `testzip()` finds its member's CRC intact and its CSV's data rows equal the `count` that `kraken ledgers` or `kraken trades-history` prints for the same window with `--start` and `--end` (the measured basis), and unzip it; a zip that fails its test, or whose row count disagrees, is refused and its retrieve run again; run `uv run zcrypto tax blockpit --ledgers … --trades … --out data/kraken-statements/<start>_<end>/blockpit-<start>_<end>.csv --after /mnt/zhao-crypto/kraken-statements/<previous window>/blockpit-<previous window>.csv.provenance.json`;
