# Rung 1's execution findings: a reprice waits for its tick and crosses through the IOC, the ledger reader matches margin rows, and a restart settles the intents it orphans

## Problem

Three findings from Rung 1's attended window, measured on 2026-09-25 and 2026-09-26, are one build-list item on T0018 (the line of 2026-09-26 under its build sequence), and this spec delivers that item; no new topic is registered by it.
The reprice ladder. On 2026-09-25 the disposal plan's first post-only sell was accepted and then cancelled by the venue as crossing (`OrderCanceled`, reason `Post only order`, the surface `docs/reference/adapter-verification/2.0.0rc6.dev20260921.md` records for a crossing post-only), and `_reprice` in `cli/engine/executor.py` re-submitted it five times off the same stored quote: six orders at 73792.0 between 20:34:05.459Z and 20:34:05.665Z, 206 ms, then `unfilled` with `reprice budget exhausted`, and no IOC, because the fallback is reachable only through the 15-minute time-box; a fresh plan placed by hand filled at 20:44:53Z. The cause is on the page: `on_quote` stores a tick's two sides and submits only in `awaiting_quote`; `_reprice` runs synchronously inside the cancel or rejection dispatch and prices from the stored touch; no field records which tick priced the live order, so the ladder cannot tell a new tick from the one it just crossed on.
The ledger reader. On 2026-09-26 `zcrypto engine tracking-report --ledger-export` read Kraken's real ledger CSV for the first time: it matched two `trade` rows to journaled fills, the disposal's pair, and placed `collateralconversion 4, margin 5, settled 2` nowhere. The window's two margin opens and its margin close had no `trade` row at all, each having only a `margin` row whose `refid` is the fill's own trade id, its `amount` the realized PnL and its `fee` the fill's fee; a position's `rollover` and `collateralconversion` rows carry the opening trade id as `refid`; a close's `margin` row and a hand settle's `settled` pair carry ids of their own. The reader's `rollover fees` printed `0.02` against a hand read of 0.016, four rows of 0.0040, so the equality the runbook's §6 item 3 demands could not be read from a two-decimal print. The export's columns are `txid,refid,time,type,subtype,aclass,subclass,asset,wallet,amount,fee,balance,amountusd,feeusd,balanceusd,feecurrency`; the types seen are `trade` (subtype `tradespot`), `margin`, `rollover`, `settled`, `collateralconversion` and `deposit`.
The adopt pass after a restart. On 2026-09-26, drill G, a rest-hold order was resting when the engine was stopped and started; the boot reconciled `external=1, open=1`; the pass cancelled the adopted order at 09:59:55.7Z; the venue never acknowledged on the stream, nautilus queried the order four times and minted `OrderCanceled` at 10:00:26.556Z, and the executor logged that the row keeps its state. The venue had in fact cancelled the order: no open order at 10:01:22Z by the read-only key. The row stayed `accepted` and the intent `pending` through the hold's 45-minute expiry at 10:42:37Z and through the 12:00Z boundary, because the hold's timer lived in the stopped process and the pass writes nothing to the row or the intent; `zcrypto_exec_external_events_total{disposition="matched"}` read 2, both cancel events keyed back through the row the pass attached by its txid. §6 item 1 of `infra/runbooks/engine-procedures.md` requires every intent of a window terminal, and a restart now leaves one `pending` for good.
The change deploys with T0213's engine rollout as one image, unless T0213's own plan stretches past a week; the plan says so in its rollout paragraph and plans no converge of its own.

## Decisions

The decisions fall in three clusters. D1 to D6 are the reprice ladder, D7 to D12 the ledger reader, D13 to D16 the adopt pass after a restart; the third cluster is the one the owner may strike at the spec review, and nothing in the first two depends on it.

**D1. A reprice prices off a tick newer than the one that priced the order it replaces.** `_ActiveIntent` gains `quote_seq`, advanced by every tick `on_quote` stores (both sides usable), and `priced_seq`, set by `_place` to the `quote_seq` the order was priced at. `_reprice` resubmits at once when `quote_seq > priced_seq` and otherwise enters a new phase, `awaiting_reprice`, from which `on_quote` resubmits on the next stored tick. Two other identities were read and set aside: the tick's `ts_event` or `ts_init`, which the executor never reads, the test fixture hard-codes to 0 and the node test's ticks do not carry, so a timestamp needs a tolerance for a missing field that a counter does not; and a touch that differs from the priced one, which strands the wait until the time-box on a feed that ticks on size alone, when a new tick at the same touch is the venue's book re-read, which is what the crossing asked for, and the count bound covers a second crossing. The cost is one tick of latency per reprice on a resting instrument, and seven existing tests gain the quote line the reprice now waits for.

**D2. While a reprice waits, the intent is bounded as a resting one is: the gate level, quote silence and the time-box, checked in `resting`'s order.** `_poll` gains an `awaiting_reprice` arm. Nothing rests, so a level the intent no longer clears, or `_QUOTE_SILENCE` (30 s) since the last stored tick, ends the intent on the tick with no cancel: `revoked` with the verdict's reasons or `quote_silence`, and the plan halted, as every revoke halts it. The time-box takes `_on_cancel_ack`'s requested arm less the cancel, `_time_box_with_nothing_resting`: `execute` crosses through the IOC fallback, `rest-cancel` ends `rest_cancel_ok` or `partial`, `rest-hold` ends `rest_hold_expired` or `partial`. A bound of its own for the wait was set aside: the silence bound already says when the feed is dead, and a second constant is a second number to keep true. `_publish_resting_age` counts `resting` alone, so the gauge reads 0 while a reprice waits, which is true of the book.

**D3. In `execute` mode the crossing that spends the maker budget crosses through the bounded IOC fallback; the rest modes keep `unfilled`.** The sixth crossing (`reprices > _MAX_REPRICES`, 5) calls `_fallback`: at most `_MAX_IOC_ATTEMPTS` (3) limit-IOC orders at the stored opposite touch, each sized to the remainder and gate-evaluated in `_submit`, and the intent ends `unfilled` with `the bounded fallback did not fill` only once both budgets are spent. `rest-cancel` and `rest-hold` end `unfilled` with `reprice budget exhausted` as today, since a mode built never to fill may not take. Ending `unfilled` on the maker budget alone was set aside: the T0090 ruling is maker-first with a taker fallback because an unfilled leg strands the tax probe, and the measured window needed a second plan placed by hand. Waiting for a fresh tick before each IOC was set aside too: an IOC is price-bounded, so a stale opposite touch costs an attempt and never money, and the time-box path fires its IOC off the stored quote today. The operator text's `unfilled` now means both ladders were spent, and the runbook line says so.

**D4. No plan field bounds a taker order; `mode` stays the one switch.** `probeplan.py`'s `_INTENT_KEYS` is unchanged. The IOC's limit is the opposite touch the tick showed, so a buy pays at most that ask and a sell receives at least that bid, and the fill is never worse than the book the tick showed; the notional beyond the declared one is the remainder times the spread, and the plan cap (`exec_max_plan_notional_eur`, 100 EUR) was checked on the declared notional at pickup. A per-intent slippage or taker-permission key was set aside: it would be a new `_INTENT_KEYS` entry an older engine refuses whole, for a bound the limit already gives. Re-running `_over_cap_reason` at the IOC price was set aside: it runs for `qty` intents alone, a spot disposal, a sell, whose IOC at the bid is bounded by the book.

**D5. Each order's row records the quote that priced it: `bid`, `ask` and `quote_seq` inside the row's `order` payload.** `_ROW_KEYS` in `execledger.py` is unchanged, so the schema version stays 2 and no readers-before-writer deploy is owed; `validate_exec_record` does not read inside `order`. A reader sees two rows sharing a `quote_seq` as two orders priced off one tick, the reading that took a hand replay of the 2026-09-25 log. A top-level row key was set aside: `EXEC_SCHEMA_VERSION` 3 and the ordered deploy, for three numbers.

**D6. `_on_cancel_ack`'s venue-cancel arm keeps repricing an `execute` order whatever the cancel's reason.** Keying on `payload["reason"]`, `Post only order` against `User requested`, was read and set aside: the reason is the venue's text, unpinned across adapter versions; an operator's stop for an `execute` order is the kill file, which revokes within one tick; and a hand cancel followed by a reprice is bounded by D1's tick and D3's count. This is an explicit drop, not a deferral.

**D7. `margin` rows are matched by `refid` exactly as `trade` rows are.** `_MATCHED_LEDGER_TYPES = {trade, margin}` in `cli/engine/tracking.py`; a matched row counts under `matched` (rows, one per asset leg, as before); an unmatched one FAILS the reconciliation with its id named, the runbook's rule that every unmatched id is explained or is a stop; a matched or unmatched margin row counts as a comparison, so a window whose engine activity is margin alone reads `ok` or `FAILED`, never `insufficient-data`. Carrying leverage onto `Fill` so that a margin fill must match a `margin` row was set aside: type discipline the check does not need, since the reader's job is every venue row explained.

**D8. A matched row contributes its `fee`, and never its `amount`, as a reported figure beside the rollover total; the blend stays on the journal's fills.** `matched_fees_eur` sums the `fee` of matched rows whose `asset` is a euro code, and the report prints it as the venue's own fee total over the window's journaled fills, beside the journal's cent-rounded fees the blend carries (0.32 EUR journaled against the venue's 0.3269 in the window). A `margin` row's `amount` is the position's realized PnL, a result, summed nowhere. Two options were set aside: replacing the journal's fee with the ledger's inside `cost_blend`, which would have a run without an export propose a different rate from one with, and which needs to know which row carries an open's fee when the venue charges it in EURC, a question the window's two `collateralconversion` pairs raise and one export does not settle; and nothing, which leaves the venue's exact figure nowhere to be read beside the journal's.

**D9. `settled` and `collateralconversion` are known no-fill types, counted under `known` and printed on their own line; `ignored` keeps the types the reader has not met.** `_NO_FILL_LEDGER_TYPES` gains both. `settled` is a hand settle's delivery pair, which the journal holds no row for by design (a hand settle is counted, logged and nothing else); `collateralconversion` is the venue's own currency swap for a margin fee, keyed to the position's opening trade, a swap and not a cost. Failing on either was set aside: a sanctioned hand act would fail every window that settles by hand. The rendered block gains `rows with no fill behind them by construction: <type> <count>` for `known`, and its `row types this reader places nowhere` line keeps `ignored` and names it as the types to settle against the export that carries them.

**D10. The rollover and matched-fee figures print at four decimals.** The export's euro `fee` column carried four (0.0040, 0.0795, 0.0794), and the runbook's comparison demands equality with the hand read, which two decimals cannot give: 0.016 printed 0.02. The JSON carries the float, and the sum stays a float sum, since four rows of 0.0040 sum to 0.016000000000000000333 and the print rounds it; Decimal arithmetic was set aside as a conversion the JSON value would need for a difference below the print. The `basis` text and the matched line read `ledger trade or margin row(s)`.

**D11. The rollover arm keeps summing every euro `rollover` row regardless of `refid`.** The hand read it is compared with is the whole export's, so the two agree by construction; splitting engine positions from hand-opened ones by the opening trade id is a reading the `refid` makes possible and this spec does not take.

**D12. The closed world of row types is a fixture, `test_every_row_type_of_the_real_export_lands_in_exactly_one_place`.** It carries the six types the export carried, in the sixteen-column header, one row per arm: `trade` (`tradespot`, one row per asset leg), `margin`, `rollover` and `collateralconversion` under the opening trade id, a `settled` pair under an id of its own, and `deposit`; it asserts `ignored` empty. A seventh type is a fixture row, Minor. The shape is the export's and the figures are synthetic: the real export carries account figures and is not committed.

**D13. A rest-hold intent does not survive a restart.** The pass cancels a non-reducer (spec 00108 D3 and D4 ruled the restart's adopt pass unchanged and its cancel the drills' expectation), and `restart_hold` holds the gate at `REDUCE_ONLY`, where `_level_permits` refuses an open intent; re-arming the hold from the row's `OrderAccepted` stamp would contradict both. The cost is a drill whose induction spans a restart losing its subject, which is drill G's own expectation.

**D14. After classification, the pass settles every `pending` intent of the two-day window from what its rows show; an intent whose order the pass kept resting stays `pending`.** A new `execledger` accessor, `pending_plan_intents`, lists every (boundary, plan_id, index) still `pending`; no process runs those plans, since this one starts with `_plan` None and the plan wall refuses a re-drop, so nothing else would ever end them. Each is written through `update_plan_intent` at its own boundary, without `self._plan`: `filled` when the sum of its rows' `filled_qty` reaches the first order's quantity, taken as the largest `order.qty` among its rows since every later order is a remainder, within `_OVERFILL_TOLERANCE`; `revoked` with `the engine restarted while the intent was in flight` when it ran and did not fill, the cancelled opener's case and the closed-while-down case alike; `refused` with `not run -- the engine restarted before it ran` when it has no row, `_halt_plan`'s precedent. `filled_qty` is the sum over the intent's rows. The sweep runs whether or not anything rests, and is skipped whole when the venue read failed, since the rows' fills were then never compared and are no figure to journal. Written at classification time, the kill trip's precedent, which journals the active intent `revoked` before any acknowledgement. The kept reducer's intent stays `pending` beside its open row: the order is live and the row its record, and the case is forbidden by the operating rule (no restart with a position open) until T0213's engine half lands, whose D11 redesigns the startup pass over a cache that holds the engine's own orders. Writing on the venue's acknowledgement was set aside, since in drill G it never came; a new outcome word was set aside, since `revoked` is the runbook's word for an intent taken mid-rest and the reason names the restart.

**D15. A terminal the engine minted for an adopted order writes the row `ambiguous`, at CRITICAL, naming Kraken's open orders as the discriminator.** `_venue_terminal_state`'s reconciliation arm returns `ambiguous` instead of `None`: the active path's word for the same event (`_strand_ambiguous`'s row write) and `_mark_unmatched`'s, a claim about nothing at the venue, and a member of `_OPEN_ORDER_STATES`, so the next startup's sweep re-attaches and settles the row against the venue's order, which it reads before this process sends anything. What the pass cannot tell, a cancel the venue executed unacknowledged (drill G's case) from one that did not reach it, is the operator's read of the venue's open orders, the runbook's discriminator, until the next startup reads them. Three options were set aside: a second `read_venue_orders` after the cancels, a nonce hazard (a second HTTP client racing the execution client's in-flight queries, the four that ran in G); `query_order` on the strategy handle, whose answer arrives as the same flagged terminal since nautilus keys reconciliation on the mechanism, and G's four queries did not resolve the order; and a deadline of the pass's own, since nautilus's in-flight budget (threshold 5 s, five retries at 2 s, 31 s measured) is the deadline and the minted terminal its signal. The old rule's `accepted` claimed the order still rested when the venue had cancelled it.

**D16. The operator pages follow.** `infra/runbooks/engine-procedures.md`: `revoked`'s causes gain the restart, with the reason text the pass writes and the `not run` text on the plan's later intents; the pre-probe step on minted terminals gains the adopted path's row; §6 item 3's two bullets state the widened match, the known no-fill types, the matched-fee figure and the four-decimal print; the `Nothing retries itself` rule keeps its text, `unfilled` now meaning both ladders spent. `infra/runbooks/drills-order-path.md`: A1's step 3, G's step 4 and G's Record gain the row's two readings after the pass's cancel and the intent's. `README.md`'s tracking-report row states the widened match. No page carries a spec serial, a decision number or a topic id: `tests/test_internal_terms_not_operator_visible.py` walks them.

## Out of scope

- A taker bound in the plan (D4): the IOC's limit at the opposite touch is the bound, and `mode` the switch.
- Waiting for a fresh tick before an IOC attempt (D3): price-bounded, a stale touch costs an attempt.
- Reading a cancel's reason on the venue-cancel arm (D6): the venue's text, unpinned.
- Replacing the journal's fee with the ledger's inside the blend (D8): a run's proposal must not depend on an export it may not have.
- Failing on `settled` or `collateralconversion` rows (D9): sanctioned hand acts and venue swaps, not fills.
- Splitting rollover by the opening trade id (D11): the hand read is the whole export's.
- The reverse direction, a journaled fill with no ledger row: the export's time coverage is not compared to the window (`LedgerRow.at` is parsed and unread), so a list of unconfirmed fills reads as a finding on every export cut short of the window.
- Re-arming a rest-hold's timer after a restart (D13): the restart ends the intent.
- The kept reducer's intent after a restart (D14): forbidden by the operating rule until T0213's engine half, whose D11 owns the startup pass over a cache.
- A post-cancel venue read inside the pass (D15): the nonce hazard; the next startup's read is the settlement.
- Cancel-on-stop and re-cancel-on-reconnect: T0018's other build-sequence items, each its own spec.
