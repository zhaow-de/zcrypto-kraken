# Three of §10's four whole-book limits have no caller

## Context — what

`apply_gross_leverage_cap`, `apply_net_exposure_band`, and `apply_margin_floor` are implemented and tested — and called by nothing outside their own test file. Only `apply_position_caps` is wired into the live target pipeline. Verified 2026-07-30 in the capability audit: the §10 risk layer that the master plan treats as standing between the book and a margin blow-up exists as dead code for three of its four limits.

## Why this matters

Stage 6b's rungs put real capital under the book for the first time; the ramp (25 → 50 → 100 % of $10k) multiplies whatever the limits do not cap. A margin floor that is implemented-and-tested-but-unwired reads as "we have a margin floor" in every design document while enforcing nothing — the worst shape of gap, invisible until the day it is needed. Wiring must land before rung 3 puts full weights on.

## Findings so far

- The wiring point is a design decision, not a mechanical edit: the limits compose (position caps → whole-book caps → governor?) and the order of application changes the result — e.g. a gross-leverage cap applied before vs after the governor multiplier caps different books. The composition order needs the owner's ruling, then a pinned test.
- Shadow-mode neutrality is checkable: on the 20-day journal the long-only ~5 % gross book should pass all three limits untouched, so wiring them must be verdict-neutral on the replay ([[T0117]]'s decomposition rebuild is the natural harness to prove it on).

## Done so far

- **The composition order is ruled and wired (owner, 2026-08-02; iter-119).** The order is `combined → apply_position_caps → gross_leverage_cap → net_exposure_band → margin_floor → × governor`: per-asset shaping first, then whole-book ceilings on the shaped book, with the governor left **outermost**. The reason the governor stays outside is load-bearing and easy to "correct" later — it is a pure returns overlay by construction, so clamping after it would silently re-scale the multiplier's own effect and partially undo a de-levering governor. Both target builders (the verified path and the bit-identical fast path) call one shared helper, and costing moved to the limited book in the same change so the governor is fed the returns of the book that would actually trade. Order *among* the three is deliberately unpinned: all three are proportional whole-book scalings, so on a breaching fixture the ruled order and its reverse produce the same book — the tests pin the observable part (after the non-proportional per-asset caps, inside the governor) and assert no order-sensitivity property, because none holds.
- **Verdict-neutrality measured, and the neutrality claim proven non-vacuous.** The committed frozen figures are unmoved (Sharpe 1.5609, maxDD 0.1357/0.1866, `cap_breach_bars` 1318, `governor_engaged_bars` 7302) and all **137** journaled cycles replay with no stage-identity break — **0 of 137** cap-bound. Nothing binds because the **capped** book, which is what the whole-book limits see (they run before the governor multiply), has a median gross of **10.74 %** against a 150 % soft cap; the familiar ~5.4 % is the *post-governor* final gross and a different book (10.740 × 0.500 = 5.370 on the median cycle). Neutrality alone cannot distinguish correct-and-inert wiring from inert code, so the converse was constructed: choking `soft_cap` to 0.01 fails every replayed cycle, and on the cycle checked in full the factor lands where predicted — 0.13724 measured vs 0.13725, that cycle's capped gross being 7.286 %. The factor is per-cycle, not one constant across the 137.
- **The wiring exposed a worse defect than the one this topic named, and it was fixed here.** Three recomputations *outside* the builder — `cli/engine/feeders.py`'s stage-identity check and two sites in `cli/engine/soak.py` — rebuilt the book with `apply_position_caps` alone. They agreed only while nothing bound; the first binding limit would have raised `stage identity broken` and dropped `reconcile_ok` to False, i.e. **a tooling false-positive firing precisely when the risk layer does its job**. That inversion is worse than a silent clamp, because the alarm accuses the engine at the moment it is correct. All book-rebuilding sites now share the one public helper. **Three cap-breach mirrors were deliberately left pre-limits** — `cap_bound` in `feeders.py`, `cap_breach` in `_net_live_from_result`, and the `breach` term in `realized_internals` — because each mirrors `cap_breach_bars`, which the builder computes *before* the limits run; applying the stack to them would have introduced the divergence rather than removed it. Only `realized_internals` is a whole call site left untouched; the other two are the breach term inside a site whose other quantities did move to the limited book, so the split is per-quantity, not per-file. Both directions of that judgment were re-traced site-by-site in review against the specific builder variable each mirrors.

- **The limit-breach counter ships (iter-140, spec/plan `00090` D12).** A binding whole-book limit is now a visible event rather than a silent clamp: `cli/engine/cycle.py::_limits_bound` recomputes the §10 stack over the forming row exactly as `replay_stages` does and answers a new `CycleResult.limit_bound`, which `_CycleGauges` in `cli/engine/command.py` turns into `zcrypto_engine_limit_bound_total`. The name is **intent-prefixed on purpose** — the whole-book limits bind on the intent book this cycle just built, not on anything the executor did with it — so it sits with the engine families rather than under the `zcrypto_exec_*` prefix, and it was admitted through the capture keep-regex and the Alloy required-list in the same change (both directions of the T0051 trap). `limit_bound` is a `bool | None`: a recomputation that raises answers **`None` — "no answer" — never a `False` that would read as a quiet book**, and the cycle still succeeds with its record on disk, because the §10 stack validates its input and raises on anything non-finite where the sleeve-gross sum beside it does not. Both halves are pinned: `tests/test_engine_cycle.py` drives the three verdicts including the raising recomputation, and `tests/test_engine_metrics.py` parametrizes the counter so it moves only when a limit actually bound. Commits `d9030365` (counter + admission) and `39ba1603` (the raising-recomputation proof). **Landed, green, and DEPLOYED 2026-08-19 with the `00090` image — and its first live readings are in**: `zcrypto_engine_limit_bound_total` reads `0` on host `zcrypto` with `changes(...[12h]) == 0`, which is the expected healthy value (the engine is disarmed, so these are intent-book cycles; the counter is about what the §10 stack did to the book, not about submission). The remaining sub-item is unaffected: it is the decompose table, not this counter.

## Resolution

**Resolved 2026-08-29 (iter-154).** The wiring this topic was opened for landed in iter-119; the last sub-item — the decompose table attributing the whole-book limits' share to the governor — is closed here.

`final` is `multiplier * limited` by construction, and `_check_stage_identity` enforces that exactly. The table stepped `capped` straight to `final` and labelled the transition "fraction left by the volatility governor", so on a binding cycle it would have charged the §10 limits to the governor's row — misreading the one event `zcrypto_engine_limit_bound_total` exists to surface, and doing it in the report an operator would open to understand that counter.

`replay_stages` already computed `limited` and discarded it after the identity check. It is now carried: a **required** field on `CycleStages` (a default would let a future construction fall back to a book that is only correct while nothing binds — the defect's own shape), `limited_gross` / `limited_ratio` in the payload, a `limited` column in the table, and the summary split into `capped -> limited` and `limited -> final`.

**Proven where the shares differ.** Every pre-existing fixture has no limit binding, so `capped -> limited` is 1.000 and the merged label read correctly there — a test built only on those would have passed before the change. `test_a_binding_whole_book_limit_is_attributed_to_the_limits_not_the_governor` gives the limits a quarter of the book and the governor a half, asserts each share lands on its own line, and asserts the two are unequal so the fixture cannot decay into a degenerate one. Under the old column the governor would have read 0.375 — de-levering twice as hard as it did.

**The trigger was the finding, not the work.** This sat behind `ripe_when: zcrypto_engine_limit_bound_total moves off zero, or the table is touched for any reason`. The first half waited for the failure before fixing the thing that reports it, and the counter has read 0 since it deployed; the second is not a condition anyone evaluates. The work was gated on nothing and took one commit.
