## What A2 is<a name="what-a2-is"></a>

Per master-plan §5: **A2 — per-asset symmetric TSMOM ensemble with vol sizing (4h–1d)**: a Donchian / multi-lookback ensemble per asset, long/flat/**short**, inverse-vol aggregated, on the 10–12 majors. It is **an A/B *inside* the A family, not a separate family** — A1 (SMA-gate + trend-agreement book) and A2 (Donchian-channel ensemble) are two parameterizations of the same "vol-targeted per-asset trend book" hypothesis, sharing one trial budget (A = 40; 24 spent so far on A1). The published upper bound is strong (Zarattini–Pagani–Barbon Donchian ensemble, Sharpe > 1.5 net / ~19 % maxDD 2015–2025) — an **optimistic** bound to haircut hard for our fee tier and 10-asset universe.

## The five A1 lessons that are binding constraints on A2<a name="the-five-a1-lessons-that-are-binding-constraints-on-a2"></a>

A1's five-iteration arc (iter-044→050, reports `06`/`07`) produced lessons that A2 must design around from the start — several hit A2 *harder* than A1:

1. **Judge A2 net-of-cost, not zero-fee — from the first verdict.** A1 cleared the pre-registered kill bar (zero-fee SPA p=0.011) yet lost net-of-cost to gated-B1. Use **`net_of_cost_verdict`** (`cli/alpha/killbar.py`, iter-050) — the net-of-cost head-to-head — alongside `a1_kill_bar`, so A2 can't be credited on a zero-fee edge its turnover erases. The pre-registered bar's SPA-on-zero-fee is a known **cost-asymmetry gap** (`07`) — folding a net-of-cost SPA into it is a human Phase-5 call, but A2's *analysis* should lead with net-of-cost.

2. **Turnover is the dominant cost, and A2 is higher-frequency.** A1's daily 10-asset rebalancing drag alone sank its net-of-cost edge (~1.35 → ~0.81 spot-only). A2 at **4h–1d is higher-frequency → more turnover** — this is the binding risk. Design for it: Donchian channels are naturally lower-turnover than daily inverse-vol reweighting (positions change only on channel breakouts), which is a point *for* A2; but the 4h band multiplies rebalancing opportunities. Test the rebalance cadence / holding explicitly (iter-048's offset-averaged method), and haircut the published Sharpe for realistic maker-fill + turnover.

3. **The short's borrow carry (~5 %/yr) is a structural, cadence-insensitive killer for a shorting book.** A1-with-short was net-of-cost-dominated purely by the short leg's daily borrow carry (iter-048); A1-long/flat then *beat* gated-B1 net-of-cost but **failed the 2014 worst-slice** (−1.8) with no short to hedge the crash (iter-049). A2 is symmetric long/flat/**short** — so it inherits *both* horns: the short costs ~5 %/yr to carry, but dropping it re-exposes the bear-year tail. Budget the margin carry into A2's cost model from the start (`cli.costs.margin_carry`), and treat "short on / off" as an explicit A2 toggle whose net-of-cost *and* worst-slice are both reported.

4. **The worst-slice leg is where the short earns its keep — and where early-data 2014 bites.** gated-B1's 200-day gate did hold it ~87 % flat through 2014, and it lost **less** than A1-long/flat (−5.5 % / 6.0 % maxDD vs −8.4 % / 8.4 %) — that economic point stands. **But (iter-053, `09`) the worst-slice leg cannot express it**: the leg is Sharpe-based and **exposure-blind**, so gated-B1's 2014 Sharpe (**−2.07**) is *worse* than A1-long/flat's (−1.80) despite the smaller loss, and gated-B1 — with five negative years — **fails the leg itself**. Treat a worst-slice failure as **uninformative** until the leg is made benchmark-relative / drawdown-based (a human Phase-5 call). Also beware the leg's asymmetry: it *skips* zero-variance slices, exempting a book that sits a regime out while judging one that trades it.

   **Added (iter-053):** a **benchmark warm-up asymmetry**. gated-B1 is structurally flat for its first 199 periods (200-day gate). A2's Donchian warms up in 20–100 periods and trades that window — which inflated A2's first-pass net-of-cost significance (K=8 SPA p 0.034 full → **0.057 post-warmup, not significant**). Any challenger without a long warm-up must be evaluated on the **post-warmup window**, or it is credited for a stretch where the benchmark cannot trade. (A1 shares the 200-day gate, so it is unaffected — verified.)

5. **gated-B1 is the robust bar to beat: net-of-cost Sharpe ≈ 1.047.** *(Update iter-055: superseded as settled — B3+vt-dynamic point-beats it net-of-cost, 1.245/1.278, n.s. p ≈ 0.27, worse maxDD; the bar choice is T0009's first item.)* Not the zero-fee 1.247. A2 earns deployment consideration only by beating gated-B1 **net-of-cost** and clearing the worst-slice — cheaply enough that its turnover doesn't erase the edge.

## Suggested A2 design (a starting point, not a spec)<a name="suggested-a2-design-a-starting-point-not-a-spec"></a>

- **Reuse the A1 substrate**: `cli/features/` (add a Donchian `channel_position` ensemble — `channel_position` already exists), `cli/benchmark/strategies.py` (`vol_target`, `dynamic_inverse_vol_basket`), `cli/alpha/a1.py`'s per-asset-direction + inverse-vol-weight machinery (an A2 book is the same shape with a Donchian-ensemble direction instead of the SMA-gate + trend-agreement one), `cli/alpha/killbar.py` (`a1_kill_bar` **and** `net_of_cost_verdict`), `cli/costs/` (spot + margin carry), `cli/registry/` (family accounting — decide whether A2 continues the `A1` family key or a shared `A` key, given "one family").
- **Toggles** (each a registered trial, within the shared A = 40 budget — 16 remain): Donchian lookback ensemble set; **short on/off** (given lesson 3); rebalance cadence / breakout-hold; vol target. Keep it small — the budget is shared with A1's 24.
- **Verdict**: `a1_kill_bar` (pre-registered) **and** `net_of_cost_verdict` (the honest head-to-head), both against gated-B1, net-of-cost, with the short leg's margin carry charged. An honest "A2 also loses net-of-cost / gated-B1 stays" is success.
