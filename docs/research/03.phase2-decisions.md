[iter-022] Phase 2 — the injected-leak acceptance test (§9 "look-ahead bug must be caught"). (Decision: 1)
The last acceptance check: demonstrate that CPCV's embargo removes a genuine data leak. A fixed rule can't leak
(no fitting), so the leak needs a FITTED predictor + overlapping labels. Construction options:
  1. **Overlapping-label series + nearest-neighbor-by-index leak metric** — labels `y_t = Σ_{k<H} e_{t+k}`
     (H-sum of iid noise, so `y_t, y_s` share `H-|t-s|` noise terms iff `|t-s| < H`); feature `x_t = t`, so
     the 1-NN-by-feature predictor picks the nearest train index. Metric = mean over test of `ŷ_i·y_i`. WITHOUT
     embargo, a test point at a CPCV block edge finds an ADJACENT train index (overlapping label) → `E[ŷ·y] =
     H-1 > 0` (fake skill = the leak); WITH `embargo=H` the H boundary indices are purged → nearest train is
     ≥H away → `E[ŷ·y] = 0`. Assert leak(embargo=0) clearly positive, leak(embargo=H) ≈ 0. Recommended — a
     genuine, analytically-grounded leak whose removal by embargo is measurable and unambiguous.
  2. OLS-coefficient leak — cleaner model but x⊥y gives β̂≈0 with or without purge, so it does NOT demonstrate
     a leak. Rejected (no measurable effect).
  (Decision: 1) — the overlapping-label NN construction: the leak is real (shared-noise covariance H-1),
  embargo's removal is analytically predicted and empirically checkable, and the reviewer (fresh context)
  verifies the effect is signal not noise — the right way to land the subtle "leak is caught" check.

[iter-023] Phase 2 — SPA / reality-check machinery (§9.4 "beats the benchmark?"), and its variant. (Decision: 1)
The last substantial §9.4 harness component: a data-snooping-corrected test of whether the best of a family of
strategies beats a benchmark. (Multi-seed runner is YAGNI-deferred — the plan de-emphasizes stochastic learners.)
Variant:
  1. **White's Reality Check (unstudentized), reusing the stationary bootstrap** — statistic `V = max_k d̄_k`
     (best strategy's mean outperformance vs the benchmark); bootstrap the time rows, recenter `V* =
     max_k(d̄_k* - d̄_k)`, p-value `= (1 + #{V* ≥ V})/(B+1)`. Correct + calibrated (White 2000), never-NaN
     (no `/std`, p ∈ (0,1]), and reuses `stationary_bootstrap_indices` (iter-016). Recommended.
  2. Hansen's SPA (studentized + data-dependent recentering, SPA_c/l/u) — more powerful/robust but materially
     more complex (variance estimation, threshold recentering) and the `/ω_k` reintroduces a NaN trap. Defer
     the studentized refinement; the Reality Check is the clean, correct v1.
  (Decision: 1) — White's Reality Check: the calibrated, bootstrap-reusing, never-NaN core of §9.4's
  benchmark-superiority test; acceptance = a planted-superior family → low p, an all-null family → FP ≈ alpha.
  The benchmark family it runs against is Phase 3; the machinery is proven on synthetic data now.

[iter-114] Which spread statistic should the cost model charge? (Decision: 2)
  1. **Median top-of-book `spread_bps` per pair** — the obvious reading of "per-pair spread", one number per pair, directly comparable to the catalog's first-look figures. Rejected on measurement: BTC/EUR is tick-quantised at €0.10 and sits at exactly one tick 42–58 % of the seconds on complete UTC days (41.4 % including the two partial edge days; 49.5 % pooled). Because that fraction straddles 50 %, the *median* flips ~15× (0.29 → 0.018 bps around 2026-07-13) on a modest change in the one-tick share while the distribution barely moves — mean ÷ median 11.2× for BTC against 0.9–1.3× for every other pair. A statistic that unstable cannot anchor a cost model, and it is what produced the apparent 5× disagreement with `data-catalog-full.md`'s 2026-07-15 BTC figure (both medians are correct on their own windows; the statistic is the problem).
  2. **Mean effective spread at the traded notional (`fill_bps`)** — what we actually pay, and empirically stable everywhere (BTC p50 0.346 vs mean 0.392 bps at €1k) because walking the book averages over the tick quantisation. Recommended and taken: it removes the instability *and* is the more faithful quantity, so there is no trade to make. **Stated assumption:** an unconditional time-average is the right estimator only if trade times are uncorrelated with spread; the book rebalances at bar boundaries, and top-of-hour seconds are measurably ~1.2× wider (decaying by ~second 50), so a taker firing inside the first minute of a boundary would pay above this table. Over the ratified 15–30 min execution window the effect washes out (×1.010).
  3. **Mean top-of-book spread** — stable (a mean is not mode-sensitive) but prices an infinitesimal order: for BTC it is 0.199 bps against a measured 0.252 bps fill at €100, and the gap widens steeply at €1k/€10k. Rejected as systematically understating.

[iter-114] Should the spread term carry a session-time dimension? (Decision: 1)
  1. **No session term — one constant per pair per size.** Measured across all ten pairs at €1k: Asia 00–07 / EU 07–15 / US 15–24 ratios span **1.01×–1.10×** (widest LTC 1.098×). Recommended and taken — but on **materiality, not absence**: a ≤10 % modulation of a 2–4 bps term against a 40–80 bps fee does not earn a per-session dimension. A paired day-level test *does* detect a consistently-signed effect (t = −1.9…−2.3 on BTC/ETH/LTC, 7/10 pairs Asia-wider), so "inside the noise" would have been the wrong justification.
  2. **Per-session constants** — matches the topic's stated ask and would capture the small liquidity cycle. Rejected: triples the surface to maintain for an effect an order of magnitude below the fee it sits beside.
  3. **Continuous time-of-day curve** — strictly more expressive, strictly less defensible at this effect size; rejected on the same ground.

[iter-114] How should the helper price a notional between (or beyond) the pinned €100/1k/10k grid? (Decision: 2)
  1. **Linear in notional** — simplest. Rejected: cost is strongly convex in size on the thin pairs (DOT 3.68 → 5.55 → 12.41 bps), so linear interpolation flattens exactly the curvature that matters.
  2. **Linear in log-notional, clamp below €100, REFUSE above €10k.** Recommended and taken. Log-interpolation tracks the observed convexity; refusing to extrapolate is the honest failure, because on a convex curve an extrapolation understates cost precisely where the error is most expensive. A caller needing €50k must recalibrate with a larger pinned size rather than receive a confident wrong number.
  3. **Fit a parametric impact curve (e.g. square-root law)** — smooth and extrapolable. Rejected as unearned: three pinned points per pair cannot identify a functional form, and a fitted curve would license exactly the extrapolation option 2 refuses.
