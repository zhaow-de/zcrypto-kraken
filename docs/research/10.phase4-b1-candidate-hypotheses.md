# B1 candidate hypotheses — a survey for the owner's pick

This note surveys new, fence-legal ideas for the B1 family after its two rejected trials and writes three of them up as pre-registration-ready options for the owner to pick from, or to decline. It answers the owner's standing disposition for the family of 2026-07-23, "draft candidate hypotheses", and it spends, pre-empts and pre-registers nothing: no number in it was computed from data, and no parameter below was chosen by looking at a return.

## What trials 45 and 46 settled

- **The fence.** B1 is seasonality used as conditioning features on the adopted signal, never a standalone high-turnover system, and it lives or dies on maker-fill realism and turnover control (`docs/research/00.master-plan.md:154`; family topic `docs/open-topics/T0022-b1-intraday-seasonality-family.md:10`). The 4h layer owns the signal; the 1h and 15m layer owns intraday realized vol, position scalers, stop distances and the execution scheduler (`docs/research/00.master-plan.md:181-182`).
- **Trial 1 (registry record 45), REJECT.** Arm B was the adopted A2-4h ensemble through favorable-window gating — 42 hour × weekday cells of the decision boundary, favorable when arm A's net return summed over the cell's walk-forward training stamps is positive, held through when not — plus a book-level vol-state scaler, 0.5 when the mean across assets of 24h 15m realized vol over its own 30-day median exceeds 1.5 (`docs/specs/00045-b1-seasonality-conditioning-design.md:11-17`). B failed the kill bar's SPA leg (p 0.3063–0.3243 across the grid) while arm A passed the identical bar (decisive p 0.0085), and the head-to-head was B ≤ A (−2.648e−5 per bar from 2016, p 1.0) (`docs/reference/trial-registry.jsonl:45`; `docs/research/10.phase4-decisions.md:209`).
- **Trial 2 (record 46), REJECT.** The same gates with the scaler off passed the bar by inheriting arm A's edge (SPA 0.0085–0.0155) but lost the head-to-head (−1.358e−6 per bar from 2016, p 0.8061) (`docs/reference/trial-registry.jsonl:46`; `docs/research/10.phase4-decisions.md:216`). Both records now re-derive from committed code, subjects `b1-conditioning` and `b1-window-only` (`docs/specs/00130-b1-records-rebuild-design.md:9`, `:28`; `cli/research/subjects.py:686`, `:696`).
- **Mechanism fact 1 — hold-through defers, it does not eliminate.** 5,506 held stamps saved 1.6 % of turnover (146.19 → 143.84), because the trade a held stamp skips comes back as a catch-up trade when the gate reopens, and the held exposure is exposure arm A had already shed: maximum drawdown 24.25 % against 21.54 % (`docs/reference/trial-registry.jsonl:46`; `docs/specs/00130-b1-records-rebuild-design.md:11`).
- **Mechanism fact 2 — a discrete book-level vol state re-trades the whole book at every flip.** 4,167 scaled boundaries took turnover from 146.19 to 195.53 (+33.8 %) and maximum drawdown to 30.07 %, and the decisions log records that the scaler harmed even before flip costs, which is why the hysteresis variant's prior was rated weak (`docs/reference/trial-registry.jsonl:45`; `docs/research/10.phase4-decisions.md:216`).
- **Mechanism fact 3 — calendar cells fitted on arm A's own returns carried nothing the head-to-head could see**, on either window, in either trial (`docs/research/10.phase4-decisions.md:209`, `:216`). The family topic's conclusion: the conditioning-overlay class is attributed out on the A2 book, and a further trial needs a genuinely new hypothesis (`docs/open-topics/T0022-b1-intraday-seasonality-family.md:27`).
- **What the two trials did not test:** a sizing input other than a state multiplier; any condition on the A2 signal's own events (its breakouts) rather than on the calendar; the intraday-trend half of the family's title (`docs/research/00.master-plan.md:154`); the execution layer.
- **Four facts that bind any new candidate.** (a) Arm A's cost drag is 7.03 %/yr at 0.006 per side (`arm_a_spot_drag_pct_yr` 0.0703, `docs/reference/trial-registry.jsonl:45`) — the line a B1 knob exists to cut. (b) The head-to-head and the SPA leg read the mean of B − A in raw net returns (`cli/alpha/killbar.py:151-189`), so a knob that lowers exposure wins only if the exposure it removes had negative net expected return, and a knob that raises exposure wins by leverage alone. (c) The trial-1 design's decision matrix leaves one cell open — B above A on the point estimate, SPA not significant, turnover down by less than 20 % — which the records' reproduction test reads as reject (`docs/specs/00045-b1-seasonality-conditioning-design.md:48-53`; `docs/specs/00130-b1-records-rebuild-design.md:50`); the B2 opening design's matrix closes every cell (`docs/specs/00128-b2-family-opening-design.md:59-66`), and each matrix below does too. (d) Over 2020-09-22 → 2026-06-30 the unconditioned ensemble fails the bar (DSR 0.8785, SPA p 0.3843), while over the full history it passes (`docs/research/10.phase4-decisions.md:245-251`; `docs/reference/trial-registry.jsonl:45` notes), so a B1 trial is interpretable only on a window from the series start.

## The longlist

01. **Session-open entry filter** (§5's session-boundary momentum): accept the ensemble's breakout entries only on 4h bars that span the Asia or US open. **Eliminated — attributed out.** It is favorable-window gating restricted to entries: a rejected off-session breakout returns as the same trade at the next session bar, the deferral that sank trial 46, at a worse price; and the US open is set in Eastern Time ([NYSE hours](https://www.nyse.com/markets/hours-calendars)), which moves against the fixed UTC grid with daylight saving, so any fixed window is a seasonal fit. The documented effect is half-hour scale ([Shen, Urquhart & Wang 2022](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/bitcoin-intraday-time-series-momentum/)), out of reach of a 4h signal without a standalone sub-hour system, which the plan rules out (`docs/research/00.master-plan.md:172`).
02. **Hour-of-day return windows from the literature** (BTC strongest 21:00–23:00 UTC, [Quantpedia, Padyšák & Vojtko](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-seasonal-intraday-or-overnight-anomalies-in-bitcoin/)). **Eliminated — high-turnover by construction as a standalone, attributed out as a conditioning.** A daily two-hour long is 365 round trips a year, about 180 % a year at the 0.50 % maker round trip (`docs/research/00.master-plan.md:154`) against the 33 % a year the source reports gross; as a conditioning it is the return-fitted calendar cell of trials 45 and 46, and the wider evidence finds such return patterns time-varying and not persistent ([Baur, Cahill, Godfrey & Liu 2019](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-any-simple-calendar-effects-in-bitcoin-market/)).
03. **Weekend↔weekday transition** (Monday effect, weekend volume trough) as a weekend hold or a Friday de-risk and Monday re-risk. **Eliminated.** A weekend hold is a calendar gate with hold-through (trial 46); a scheduled de-risk and re-risk re-trades the book twice a week, the churn the trial-1 design itself warned of (`docs/specs/00045-b1-seasonality-conditioning-design.md:15`); and the return effect is weak — a Monday premium in BTC alone, its trading simulations mostly indistinguishable from random ([Caporale & Plastun 2019](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/17208)).
04. **Faster intraday vol timing** — the banded or hysteresis scaler, or a continuous short-horizon (HAR-type, [Corsi 2009](https://academic.oup.com/jfec/article/7/2/174/856522)) vol target. **Eliminated — attributed out.** It is trial 45's mechanism without the flip; the scaler harmed before costs (`docs/research/10.phase4-decisions.md:216`), and a short-horizon continuous response re-sizes the whole book at every boundary. External evidence agrees: net of costs, vol management of factors other than the market earns no abnormal return ([Barroso & Detzel 2021](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/355328)).
05. **Intraday realized vol as the ensemble's cross-asset weight estimator** — **shortlisted as candidate A**: a continuous, level-neutral estimator swap at the arms' own horizon, aimed at the cost line.
06. **Seasonally normalized volume confirmation of the ensemble's breakout entries** — **shortlisted as candidate B**: a condition on the signal's own event that uses the one intraday seasonality the literature finds robust, volume.
07. **Hourly channel exits** (§6's risk layer on the ensemble's own channel) — **shortlisted as candidate C**: the untested intraday-trend half of the family.
08. **Phase-tranched decision clock** — the ensemble averaged over the four hourly phases of the 4h grid ([Hoffstein, Faber & Braun, rebalance timing luck](https://cxoadvisory.com/?p=34592)). **Eliminated as a B1 trial.** B is the mean of A and its three phase siblings, so its head-to-head against A measures only how A's phase compares with theirs — with no time-of-day structure the expected difference is zero — and the matrix can only reject or report A's phase luck. That is a robustness read of the adopted sleeve, which a zero-spend decision-support read has precedent for (`docs/research/13.phase5-decisions.md:54`), not a conditioning knob; adopting it would also need hourly engine cycles.
09. **Execution-layer seasonality** — work each boundary's orders in the liquid hours, or as limit orders on intraday dips. **Eliminated — cannot be valued by a flat-cost backtest.** Its target is spread and fill quality, which a flat 0.006 per side cannot see; a 15m backtest sees only price drift on the traded quantity, which is not the hypothesis; a dip-fill model needs queue realism the bars cannot give, and the L2 panel that could starts 2026-07-08 (`docs/reference/data-catalog-full.md:148-154`). The family topic already places execution-scheduler seasonality with the go-live engine, not with a backtest overlay (`docs/open-topics/T0022-b1-intraday-seasonality-family.md:27`), and the engine already executes inside a 15-minute maker time box after each boundary (`cli/engine/executor.py:102`, `:115`).
10. **No-trade band or partial adjustment toward the target.** **Eliminated — not B1's subject.** It has no seasonal or intraday input, its width is fitted unless pinned to the venue minimum, and the system runbook assigns a rebalance dead-band to engine calibration with research charging every diff (`docs/research/12.phase5-system-spec-runbook.md:64`).
11. **US macro-print calendar** (FOMC and CPI stamps, `docs/research/00.master-plan.md:184`). **Eliminated — needs data that does not exist** (no such set in `docs/reference/data-catalog-full.md`), and its knob would be a gate or a scale, the two attributed-out classes.

- **Budget**: one trial of Bucket B's 23 (`docs/open-topics/T0016-bucket-b-c-alpha-families.md:20`); B1's `n_trials_in_family` becomes 3, and the DSR is taken at 3. Registration needs a committed subject in `cli/research/subjects.py` (`docs/specs/00128-b2-family-opening-design.md:73`).

### A — Intraday realized volatility as the ensemble's cross-asset weight estimator

- **Hypothesis.** Arm B — each adopted arm with its per-asset inverse-volatility weight estimated from the realized volatility of hourly log returns over the same 180 four-hour intervals instead of from the standard deviation of 180 four-hour returns — has a higher mean net-of-cost return than arm A at 0.006 per side (SPA p < 0.05, full window) and lower turnover.
- **Mechanism.** A held asset's position is weight × direction × book vol scalar (`cli/alpha/a2.py:114-158`), the weight being 1/σ renormalized over the eligible assets and re-estimated at every boundary from the last 180 four-hour returns (`cli/alpha/a1.py:164-189`). Each boundary one 4h return enters the window and one leaves, so a single large 4h move shifts σ — and every held asset's position — twice, on entry and 30 days later on exit. Hourly realized variance over the same 30 days estimates the same quantity from four times the observations, so the weights move less from boundary to boundary and the book trades less to follow them. Intraday returns giving materially better volatility estimates, worth 50–200 bp a year to a vol-timing investor after costs, is the finding of [Fleming, Kirby & Ostdiek 2003](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/357738).
- **Why it is not the attributed-out class.** There is no state, no threshold and no flip, so nothing re-trades the book at once (trial 45's +33.8 %). The horizon is the arms' own 30 days, so this is not vol timing. The weights renormalize to one and the book's level stays with A2's own 4h vol target applied to B's book, so the knob cannot move the book's risk level the way the scaler, a level multiplier, did. No stamp is held, so there is nothing to defer or catch up (trial 46).
- **Arms.** A as above. B: the three arms with `_inverse_vol_weights` alone replaced; directions, the `vol_target` call (its 4h estimator on B's own book base returns, cap 1.0), the ensembling and the costing are arm A's. One knob: the weight estimator.
- **Parameters — all structural, none fitted.** Horizon 180 four-hour intervals, A2's own basket lookback, so only the sampling frequency changes. Sampling hourly, the cadence §6 gives intraday vol (`docs/research/00.master-plan.md:182`), fixed before data for two mechanism reasons: the thin EUR alt pairs' 15m series omit no-trade slots (88–97 % density for AVAX, LINK and DOT, `docs/open-topics/T0022-b1-intraday-seasonality-family.md:22`), where bid–ask bounce inflates short-interval realized variance pair by pair; and the venue's REST 1h window, about 30 days (`docs/reference/data-catalog-full.md:91`), is exactly the horizon, so the estimator is servable live without a new history store. Estimator: the sum of squared log returns between consecutive present hourly closes whose close lies in `(ts[t−180] + 4h, ts[t] + 4h]` — a gap return spans its missing hours — not demeaned, divided by 180 (realized variance, [Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold & Labys 2003](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/2003/03/01/modeling-and-forecasting-realized-volatility)); the window ends at the decision boundary, so it is leak-free. Eligibility: an asset is weighted at stamp `t` exactly when arm A weights it (180 prior returns, no missing 4h return in the window, σ > 0), so the asset set per stamp is identical across arms.
- **Data.** `ohlc-full`'s 240 and 60 files for the ten EUR assets, 2013-09-10 → 2026-03-31 for BTC (`docs/reference/data-catalog-full.md:29-71`) — the 240 files are the ones records 45 and 46 read; equivalently `ohlc-15m` derived to 1h, bit-identical on prices at the seam (`:85`), or `ohlc-rolling` with 60 derived on read (`:11`). All exist in the catalog today; no L2.
- **Engagement proof, defined now.** (1) Stamps where B's position vector differs from A's (records 45 and 46's `stamps_positions_differ`) — 0 is a dead knob. (2) The weight path's total variation, Σ_t Σ_a |w(t) − w(t−1)|, B over A — the mechanism predicts below 1; at or above 1 the mechanism inverted, recorded as the trial's mechanism finding whatever the verdict. (3) Per-asset mean weight, B over A, per year — descriptive. (4) `turnover_a`, `turnover_b`, `turnover_reduction_pct` as the records hold them.
- **Turnover and drawdown.** Turnover down; its size is unknown — no number was computed. Maximum drawdown: no direction predicted, since both books are vol-targeted by the same estimator and only the composition moves. Trials 45 and 46's failure cannot recur: nothing is held, nothing caught up, nothing flips. The known confound is the estimator's bias against 4h variance, which differs by asset — an asset whose hourly returns mean-revert shows hourly realized vol above its 4h vol and is under-weighted, a trending one over-weighted — so B tilts as well as smooths; engagement items (2) and (3) together say which one a result came from.
- **Kill criterion and matrix** (every outcome in exactly one row; head-to-head read on the full window):

| Kill bar (B vs frozen benchmark) | Head-to-head (B vs A) | Verdict |
| -- | -- | -- |
| pass | mean outperformance > 0 and SPA p < 0.05, and `turnover_b` < `turnover_a` | **adopt** |
| pass | mean outperformance > 0, SPA p ≥ 0.05, and `turnover_b` < `turnover_a` | **park** (variants: 15m sampling; the estimator also in the book vol target) |
| pass | mean outperformance ≤ 0, or `turnover_b` ≥ `turnover_a` | **reject** |
| fail | any — arm A having passed the same bar at the pre-flight | **reject** |

- **Honest prior: about 0.12 that it is adopted; about 0.55 that the point estimate favours B.** The direction is well supported; the size is the problem. Only the cross-asset weights move, the 180-bar window is already long, and turnover is driven as much by direction flips and the book vol scalar as by weights, so the saving is a fraction of a 7 %/yr drag, and it is valued at 0.006 per side, whose 20 bp spread headroom is five to ten times the 2–4 bp a side measured from captured L2 (`docs/research/12.phase5-system-spec-runbook.md:78`), so a backtest win overstates the live one. The tilt confound can add or subtract more than the smoothing gains.
- **The owner still decides:** the dataset and window and `var_trials` (the common items below); hourly against 15m sampling, if the owner reads §6's "15m bars" as binding over the two reasons above; whether the park row's turnover condition should be the trial-1 design's ≥ 20 % rather than any reduction; and accepting the adoption path — the engine store holds only the 1440 and 240 grids (`cli/engine/store.py:42`), so deploying needs a 60 grid as well as the combination trial.

### B — Seasonally normalized volume confirmation of the ensemble's breakout entries

- **Hypothesis.** Arm B — each adopted arm with a Donchian entry accepted only when the breakout bar's volume is at or above its hour-of-week norm, exits always accepted — has a higher mean net-of-cost return than arm A at 0.006 per side (SPA p < 0.05, full window) and lower turnover.
- **Mechanism.** Each lookback's state goes long when the 4h close is the highest of the last `w` closes and flat when it is the lowest (`cli/features/channel.py:6-19`, `cli/alpha/a2.py:68-78`); every false breakout costs arm A an entry and an exit, 1.2 % of the traded size at 0.006 per side, and the move back. Volume is the observable that separates a breakout many participants act on from a print in a thin hour. Crypto volume carries the most robust intraday and weekly seasonality the literature reports — higher while US exchanges are open, lower overnight and at weekends ([Baur et al. 2019](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-any-simple-calendar-effects-in-bitcoin-market/); [Eross, McGroarty, Urquhart & Wolfe 2019](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/the-intraday-dynamics-of-bitcoin/)) — so a raw comparison would reject a breakout for the hour it happens in; the knob compares the bar's volume with what is normal for that hour of the week. External support for the premise is indirect: abnormal volume predicts subsequent returns in equities ([Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin 2001](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v56y2001i3p877-919.html)), and in crypto, reversal returns concentrate in low-activity pairs and a lagged-return × volume interaction predicts returns ([Bianchi, Babiak & Dickerson 2022](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v142y2022ics0378426622001418.html)).
- **Why it is not the attributed-out class.** The condition is the signal's own event and that bar's own volume, not a calendar cell fitted on returns. It acts on entries only: exits are never held, so B's state is at or below A's on every lookback at every bar, and B can never carry exposure A has shed — the drift that worsened trial 46's drawdown is impossible. A rejected entry either never happens (the breakout fails, and A's round trip is saved outright) or happens once at a later confirming bar — the same single trade later, not trial 46's accumulated catch-up. No book-level state or scale.
- **Arms.** A as above. B: the three arms with only the state machine's entry transition gated; B's position is arm A's weight × B's direction × arm A's vol scalar, so the knob moves directions only and B's position never exceeds A's. One knob: entry confirmation.
- **Parameters.** Structural: an entry event is a bar where the channel position reaches the band (1.0) while the lookback's state is not already long — A2's own transition; the norm is `E[k] = L[k] × S[c(k)]`; accept when `volume[k] ≥ E[k]`, "at or above typical"; `L[k]` is the median volume of the asset's prior 180 bars, A2's own 30-day horizon; `c(k)` is the hour and weekday of the decision boundary `ts[k] + 4h`, the trial-1 design's 42-cell key (`docs/specs/00045-b1-seasonality-conditioning-design.md:11`); `S` is held at 1 while a cell has fewer than 100 earlier observations, the trial-1 design's thin-cell rule (`:13`), so the weekly shape binds about two years into each asset's history (disclosed as the trial-1 design disclosed its burn-in); an asset with fewer than 180 prior bars has its entries accepted (it carries no weight then anyway); a forward-filled BTC stamp carries no volume and never confirms; medians throughout, so one spike does not set the norm. Estimated, walk-forward and leak-free by construction: `L` and `S`, where `S[c]` is the median over the asset's earlier bars in cell `c` of `volume[j] / L[j]` — both read volume only, never a return, and bar `k` reads only bars before it.
- **Data.** `ohlc-full`'s 240 files with their volume column (`cli/ohlc/dataset.py:10-11`; `docs/reference/data-catalog-full.md:29-71`) — the files records 45 and 46 read; no 15m, no L2. In the catalog today.
- **Engagement proof, defined now.** Entry events and rejected entry events per year, per arm × asset × lookback, and the share rejected; stamps where positions differ; `turnover_a`, `turnover_b`, `turnover_reduction_pct`. Zero rejected events in every year is a dead knob; a year with events and none accepted is reported as such.
- **Turnover and drawdown.** Turnover down: a rejected breakout that fails saves A's round trip, and one confirmed later still trades once. Maximum drawdown expected at or below A's, since B's position never exceeds A's on any asset — not guaranteed, since B can hold the subset that falls. Trials 45 and 46's failure: the catch-up cannot recur and the drift cannot recur; a later, worse entry price on breakouts that do continue is this knob's own cost, and the head-to-head is exactly what weighs it.
- **Kill criterion and matrix** — candidate A's table, with the park row's variants "hour-of-day cells only (6); the mean in place of the median".
- **Honest prior: about 0.08 that it is adopted; about 0.35 that the point estimate favours B.** B's exposure is a subset of A's, and A's exposure carries the edge that clears the bar, so B wins only if the opening bars of low-volume breakouts, up to confirmation, lose money net of the round trips they cost. Trend-following's return sits in a few large trends; if those often break out in quiet hours and confirm later, B pays the delay on exactly the trades that matter. The evidence for the premise comes from other markets and horizons.
- **The owner still decides:** the common items; whether volume confirmation is B1's — it uses the family's seasonality as the norm — or opens a new Bucket B family key, which changes the DSR count, not the shared budget; the park row's turnover condition. Adoption is the cheapest of the three: the engine store already carries volume on the 240 grid (`cli/engine/store.py:42`; `cli/ohlc/dataset.py:10-11`).

### C — Hourly channel exits on the ensemble's own channel

- **Hypothesis.** Arm B — each adopted arm with a lookback's long state also exited at the first hourly close inside the interval that breaches the level at which the 4h close would be a new `w`-bar low — has a higher mean net-of-cost return than arm A at 0.006 per side (SPA p < 0.05, full window) and a lower maximum drawdown.
- **Mechanism.** Arm A exits at a new `w`-bar low of 4h closes and is blind for the four hours between boundaries; the plan gives stop distances to the hourly risk layer (`docs/research/00.master-plan.md:182`). Checking the arm's own channel at each hourly close exits a trend earlier when the breach holds to the 4h close, and adds a false exit — and a re-entry at the next new 4h high — when it does not. Stop-loss rules always lower expected return under a random walk and can add value under momentum ([Kaminski & Lo, working-paper summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=1809); published version, [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)), so the knob's sign is a test of hourly momentum in these pairs, which intraday BTC evidence suggests at half-hour scale ([Shen, Urquhart & Wang 2022](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/bitcoin-intraday-time-series-momentum/)).
- **Why it is not the attributed-out class.** B trades earlier, never later: there is no hold, no deferral and no catch-up; no calendar cell; no vol state. B exits wherever A does and possibly sooner, and re-enters only where A2's own rule fires, so B's state is at or below A's at every bar.
- **Arms.** A as above. B: the three arms with arm A's weights and vol scalar and B's directions from the state machine plus the hourly exit check; the interval's return is split at the exit hour — the pre-exit position earns `close[k]` → the exit close, the post-exit position the exit close → `close[k+1]` — and the hourly trade is charged 0.006 per side; the next boundary continues from B's state. One knob: the exit's sampling.
- **Parameters — all structural, none fitted.** Hourly sampling, §6's risk-layer cadence (`docs/research/00.master-plan.md:182`); the breach level for lookback `w` in the interval after boundary `k` is the minimum of the last `w − 1` four-hour closes, the level at which `close[k+1]` would be a new `w`-bar low under the channel's definition at band 1.0 (`cli/features/channel.py:6-19`); the check runs on the interval's first three hourly closes, the fourth being the 4h close arm A already reads; a missing hour is skipped; exits only — entries stay on the 4h signal layer (`docs/research/00.master-plan.md:181`).
- **Data.** `ohlc-full`'s 240 and 60 files (`docs/reference/data-catalog-full.md:29-71`), or `ohlc-15m` derived to 1h (`:85`), or `ohlc-rolling` (`:11`). In the catalog today; no L2.
- **Engagement proof, defined now.** Hourly exit events per year per arm × asset × lookback, split into early (arm A also exits at that interval's 4h close) and false (it does not); stamps where positions differ; turnover including the intra-interval trades. Zero hourly exit events is a dead knob.
- **Turnover and drawdown.** Turnover up: every false exit adds a round trip A never pays; an early exit adds none. Maximum drawdown down is the expectation. Trials 45 and 46's deferral cannot recur; this knob's own failure mode is whipsaw, the one the plan warned the A1 short extension against (`docs/research/00.master-plan.md:148`), and the false-exit count measures it.
- **Kill criterion and matrix** (the B2 opening design's drawdown-conditioned form, `docs/specs/00128-b2-family-opening-design.md:59-64`):

| Kill bar (B vs frozen benchmark) | Head-to-head (B vs A) | Verdict |
| -- | -- | -- |
| pass | mean outperformance > 0 and SPA p < 0.05, and B's maximum drawdown below A's | **adopt** |
| pass | mean outperformance > 0, SPA p ≥ 0.05, and B's maximum drawdown below A's | **park** (variant: the check at 15m) |
| pass | mean outperformance ≤ 0, or B's maximum drawdown not below A's | **reject** |
| fail | any — arm A having passed the same bar at the pre-flight | **reject** |

- **Honest prior: about 0.05 that it is adopted; about 0.25 that the point estimate favours B; about 0.7 that the drawdown falls.** Hourly closes breach a 4h-close channel at least as often as 4h closes do, and in practice more, each false breach costs a round trip plus the gap between the exit and the next new high, and the mean-difference head-to-head penalizes the exposure B gives up even where its risk-adjusted return improves. A likely kill, informative in one way the others are not: it closes the intraday-trend half of the family and tells the go-live engine whether an intra-cycle risk layer is worth building.
- **The owner still decides:** the common items; whether the mean-difference head-to-head is the right read for an exposure-reducing knob (the B2 opening design kept it; a risk-matched read would be a protocol change); and the adoption path — the engine runs four-hourly cycles whose orders end about 3h47 after the boundary (`cli/engine/executor.py:112-115`), so deploying needs an intra-cycle exit path and a 60 grid.

Common decisions the owner makes for whichever candidate is picked:

- **Dataset and window.** (a) The frozen `ohlc-full` (and its 60 files where needed) over 2013-09-10 → 2026-03-31, the window records 45 and 46 used, on which arm A reproduces and is known to pass; or (b) `ohlc-rolling` as of a named day from 2013-09-10, as the rolling-canonical design moves new searches (`docs/specs/00125-rolling-canonical-design.md:68-70`) — which reads bars inside the spent holdout's window, 2026-04-01 → 2026-07-09 (`docs/research/13.phase5-holdout-ledger.md:7`), as the B2 opening design's window already did (`docs/specs/00128-b2-family-opening-design.md:38-41`), and which needs the arm-A pre-flight to decide. A window starting later is ruled out by fact (d) above. This note recommends (a): it changes nothing records 45 and 46 fixed and leaves the holdout question unasked.
- **`var_trials` at n = 3.** 0.0, the convention records 45 and 46 used, which makes the DSR leg PSR against 0 (over 27,337 returns, an annualized net Sharpe above 0.466, `docs/specs/00128-b2-family-opening-design.md:53`); or the variance of B1's recorded per-period Sharpes, the family-dispersion convention the A family used (`docs/research/13.phase5-decisions.md:84`).
- **Registration details** the spec fixes before its hash: family key, variant name, the subject's name and its metric keys, as the B2 opening design did (`docs/specs/00128-b2-family-opening-design.md:70-73`).

## Ranking and the no-trial option

- **1. Candidate A.** It aims straight at the cost line B1 exists to cut, without giving up exposure and without anything that can flip, hold or catch up; it has the only direct external evidence of net value (an intraday volatility estimate); every parameter is fixed by the arms themselves; and its worst case is bounded — a level-neutral estimator swap cannot de-risk or churn the book the way the scaler did.
- **2. Candidate B.** Also a turnover knob, and the one that uses the robust intraday seasonality (volume) on the signal's own events, with the cheapest live path; ranked below A because it removes exposure from an arm whose exposure carries its edge, and its evidence is indirect.
- **3. Candidate C.** The most informative kill, the least likely adoption: it raises turnover against the family's own premise, the mean-difference read handicaps it, and its live path needs engine work that does not exist.
- **The no-trial option.** I think recommending no B1 trial — closing the family topic as attributed out — is the better use than candidates B or C, and slightly better than A. The shared budget is not the binding cost: 23 of 25 remain for four families, and a B1 trial raises only B1's own DSR count. The binding costs are the spec, plan, subject, test and review cycle a trial takes, during the go-live run-up the umbrella topic already cites as the reason to defer a ripe family (`docs/open-topics/T0016-bucket-b-c-alpha-families.md:28`), and the fact that even an adopt needs a second, combination trial before it reaches the deployed book. Against that, A's most likely outcome is a small, insignificant difference that changes no decision. The robust part of intraday seasonality — volume, volatility and liquidity by hour — is an execution-cost question that the flat-cost backtest cannot value and that the engine's realized-fill measurement already owns (`docs/research/12.phase5-system-spec-runbook.md:78`). If the owner wants the family's last word to come from a trial rather than from attribution, candidate A is the one to run.
