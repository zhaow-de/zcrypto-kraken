[iter-119] Where do §10's three whole-book limits compose into the target pipeline? (Decision: 2)
  1. **Outside the governor** — clamp `multiplier × capped` so the limits see the book that is actually submitted. Superficially the safest reading of "whole-book", and wrong: the governor is a pure returns overlay by construction, so clamping after it silently re-scales the multiplier's own effect, and a de-levering governor would then be partially undone by a limit that re-normalizes gross.
  2. **Between the per-asset caps and the governor** — `combined → apply_position_caps → gross_leverage_cap → net_exposure_band → margin_floor → × governor`. Taken (owner, 2026-08-02). Per-asset shaping first, then whole-book ceilings on the shaped book, then the overlay outermost and untouched. Costing moves to the limited book in the same change, so the governor is fed the returns of the book that would actually trade rather than of a book the limits would have rejected.
  3. **Wire only `apply_margin_floor`** — the one limit with a venue-liquidation consequence, deferring the other two. Rejected: all three are already implemented and tested, the composition question has to be answered for any one of them, and leaving two unwired preserves exactly the gap the topic exists to close ("we have a margin floor" in the design docs, enforcing nothing).
  **Order among the three is deliberately unpinned as unobservable**: all three are proportional whole-book scalings, so on a breaching fixture the ruled order and its reverse give the same book. What the tests pin is the observable part — the stack sits after the (non-proportional) per-asset caps and inside the governor. No order-sensitivity property was asserted, because none holds.
  **Verdict-neutrality was measured, not assumed, and the neutrality claim was separately proven non-vacuous**: the committed frozen figures are unmoved (Sharpe 1.5609, maxDD 0.1357/0.1866, `cap_breach_bars` 1318, `governor_engaged_bars` 7302), and all **137** journaled cycles replay with no stage-identity break — cap-bound cycles **0 of 137**. Nothing binds because the **capped** book, the operand the whole-book limits actually see (they run before the governor multiply), has a median gross of **10.74 %** against a 150 % soft cap; the familiar ~5.4 % is the *post-governor* final gross, a different book (10.740 × 0.500 = 5.370 on the median cycle). Choking `soft_cap` to 0.01 then fails every replayed cycle, at the predicted per-cycle scale factor where checked in full (0.13724 vs 0.13725 on a cycle whose capped gross is 7.286 %) — which is what distinguishes "correctly wired and inert" from "inert code".
  **The wiring's real defect was downstream, not in the builder**: three out-of-builder recomputations (`cli/engine/feeders.py`'s stage-identity check and two in `cli/engine/soak.py`) rebuilt the book with `apply_position_caps` alone, so the first binding limit would have raised `stage identity broken` and dropped `reconcile_ok` — a tooling false-positive firing **precisely when the risk layer works**. All book-rebuilding sites now share one public helper. **Three cap-breach mirrors were deliberately left pre-limits** (`cap_bound` in `feeders.py`, `cap_breach` in `_net_live_from_result`, the `breach` term in `realized_internals`): each mirrors `cap_breach_bars`, which the builder computes *before* the limits, so applying the stack there would have introduced the divergence instead of removing it. Only one is a whole call site left untouched — the other two sit inside sites whose other quantities did move — so the split had to be made per-quantity rather than per-file.
