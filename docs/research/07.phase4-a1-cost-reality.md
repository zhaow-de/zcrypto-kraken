## Summary<a name="summary"></a>

iter-046 recorded that A1's `equal_risk_basket / ensemble / confirmed_bear` variants clear the pre-registered Phase-4 kill bar and beat gated-B1 (family SPA p=0.011). This robustness pass adds the short-borrow **margin carry** the iter-046 cost model omitted, sweeps the finding-2 **short-band**, and — most importantly — runs the **net-of-cost head-to-head** the pre-registered bar never makes. The headline result:

**Net of realistic cost, gated-B1 decisively beats every A1 survivor.** gated-B1 keeps a net-of-1.5×-cost Sharpe of **1.047** (it is cheap: BTC-only, ~1.7 %/yr turnover drag, no short). The A1 survivors' Sharpe **collapses from ~1.35 zero-fee to 0.40–0.55** once their own turnover and short carry are charged — below gated-B1 at every band and vol. **A1's edge over gated-B1 is a zero-fee artifact that does not survive the turnover the book requires.**

So the honest, corrected verdict: **A1 does not raise the deployable bar. gated-B1 remains the deployable system** (per master-plan §9's net-of-cost "best of {benchmarks ∪ survivors}" rule). The iter-046 "win" was true *only zero-fee and per the literal bar*; this is an honest kill on economic grounds — which the research loop counts as success, not failure.

## Robustness (a) — the survivors survive margin carry, but it is a heavy drag<a name="robustness-a-%E2%80%94-the-survivors-survive-margin-carry-but-it-is-a-heavy-drag"></a>

Adding a conservative short-borrow carry — for each period, each shorted asset pays `margin_carry(|notional|, 24h, high-band rate)` = `|notional|·rate·7` (1 open + 6 four-hourly rollovers), `cli/costs/` — on top of the 1.5×-spot-turnover cost:

| survivor (band=0)                      | cost-stressed Sharpe, spot only | + margin carry | margin drag | still passes literal bar? |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------: | -------------: | ----------: | :-----------------------: |
| eq_basket/ensemble/confirmed_bear/0.10 |                            0.81 |       **0.40** |   4.94 %/yr |            yes            |
| eq_basket/ensemble/confirmed_bear/0.12 |                            0.83 |       **0.42** |   5.90 %/yr |            yes            |

The kill bar's cost leg only asks "is the cost-stressed Sharpe > 0", so both still pass — but the ~5 %/yr short carry halves the cost-stressed Sharpe, an early signal the short is expensive.

## Robustness (b) — the short-band helps zero-fee (but not net-of-cost)<a name="robustness-b-%E2%80%94-the-short-band-helps-zero-fee-but-not-net-of-cost"></a>

Sweeping `short_band ∈ {0, 0.03, 0.05, 0.10}` (the finding-2 whipsaw buffer, defaulted 0 in iter-046) on the survivor config (the band monotonically shrinks the short-active fraction 38 %→28 %):

- **band=0.05 improves all three margins** at both vols — annSharpe 1.42–1.43 (up from 1.34–1.36), lower SPA p (0.005–0.018), and the worst slice moves off 2014 to a healthier +0.016–0.018.
- **band=0.10 over-suppresses the short** and fails the bar (2014 worst-slice Sharpe goes negative).
- 6 of the 8 band variants pass the literal bar; the zero-fee family SPA (K=8) stays p=0.007.

So a protective band is a genuine zero-fee improvement — but it does not close the net-of-cost gap below.

## The decisive test — net-of-cost head-to-head<a name="the-decisive-test-%E2%80%94-net-of-cost-head-to-head"></a>

The pre-registered kill bar tests "SPA beats gated-B1" on **zero-fee** returns and "survives 1.5× cost" **in isolation** (Sharpe > 0) — it never compares the two strategies **net of cost**. For a family (A1) far more expensive than the benchmark (gated-B1), those two legs can both pass while the benchmark still wins net-of-cost. Charging both sides realistically (1.5× spot turnover; A1 also pays the ×7 short carry):

| strategy                                           | zero-fee Sharpe | net-of-cost Sharpe | vs gated-B1 net-of-cost |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------: | -----------------: | :---------------------: |
| **gated-B1**                                       |           1.244 |          **1.047** |            —            |
| A1 eq_basket/ensemble/confirmed_bear/0.10 (band 0) |           1.345 |              0.400 |        **loses**        |
| A1 …/0.12 (band 0)                                 |           1.361 |              0.417 |        **loses**        |
| A1 …/0.10 (band 0.05, best)                        |           1.417 |              0.535 |        **loses**        |
| A1 …/0.12 (band 0.05, best)                        |           1.427 |              0.549 |        **loses**        |

Even the best band variant nets 0.55 vs gated-B1's 1.047 — a wide gap. And it is not the margin carry alone: spot-turnover-only, the A1 survivors net ≈ 0.81, still below gated-B1's 1.047. **The A1 book's daily 10-asset inverse-vol rebalancing is the dominant cost**; the diversification/ensemble/short edge is real zero-fee but is more than eaten by the turnover it demands.

## Finding — a cost-asymmetry gap in the kill bar<a name="finding-%E2%80%94-a-cost-asymmetry-gap-in-the-kill-bar"></a>

The pre-registered bar's `SPA-on-zero-fee + cost-survive-in-isolation` design **over-credits a high-turnover family** against a cheap benchmark: A1 cleared it while being net-of-cost inferior. The fix (flagged for the human / a future iteration): the **"SPA beats benchmark" leg should be run net-of-cost** (both sides charged), or an explicit **net-of-cost head-to-head leg** added, so a strategy cannot clear the bar on a zero-fee edge its turnover erases. This does not invalidate the iter-046 records — they are an accurate audit of the *literal* bar outcome (append-only) — but it corrects the *deployment* reading of them.

## Cost-optimization: the short's carry is the binding drag (iter-048)<a name="cost-optimization-the-shorts-carry-is-the-binding-drag-iter-048"></a>

The obvious rescue is to rebalance less often — the daily 10-asset reweighting is the dominant *spot* cost. Holding the survivor's positions across rebalance blocks (faithful + look-ahead-free) and recomputing net-of-cost — **averaged over all block-start phases** (a single phase is noisy: ±0.2–0.45 Sharpe swing from the phase alone, so the offset-mean is the defensible read, min–max in brackets):

| vol  | cadence       | full net-of-cost (offset mean [min–max]) | spot-only, margin-waived (mean [min–max]) | margin drag |
| ---- | ------------- | ---------------------------------------: | ----------------------------------------: | ----------: |
| 0.12 | daily         |                                    0.549 |                                     0.906 |    5.1 %/yr |
| 0.12 | weekly (7)    |                        0.720 [0.50–0.88] |                     **1.065** [0.85–1.24] |    5.1 %/yr |
| 0.12 | biweekly (14) |                    **0.722** [0.49–0.87] |                     **1.065** [0.85–1.20] |    5.1 %/yr |
| 0.12 | monthly (30)  |                        0.654 [0.43–0.95] |                         0.979 [0.78–1.26] |    5.1 %/yr |

(vol 0.10 is similar: full-net offset-mean peaks 0.709 at biweekly; spot-only 1.049.)

**With the confirmed-bear short, no cadence rescues A1 — robustly.** The offset-mean full net-of-cost peaks at **0.722** (biweekly), and even the single luckiest phase tops out at **0.946** — never near gated-B1's **1.047**. Lowering the rebalance frequency cuts the *spot* drag (7.5 %→1.7 %/yr), but the short's **margin carry is a daily borrow *holding* cost, flat at ~5 %/yr regardless of cadence** — turnover control cannot touch it. A1-with-the-short is a robust net-of-cost kill.

**But the kill is specifically the SHORT, not the turnover.** *Margin-waived* (spot-only — i.e. a long/flat book paying no borrow cost), A1's offset-mean net-of-cost **edges past 1.047** at weekly/biweekly (1.05–1.07, up to 1.26 at a lucky phase). So it is the short leg's borrow carry — not the diversification or the rebalancing itself — that makes A1-with-short net-of-cost uncompetitive. This **elevates a long/flat (short=off) A1 to a genuinely promising open direction** (it avoids the carry entirely), tempered by the disqualifying 2014 drawdown the short was rescuing — a distinct test, not settled here. (Method note: the single-phase numbers are offset-noisy; only the offset-mean is load-bearing.)

## Corrected verdict + next steps<a name="corrected-verdict--next-steps"></a>

- **A1 does not raise the deployable bar; gated-B1 (net-of-cost Sharpe 1.047) remains the deployable system.** An honest economic kill.
- **Registry**: the 8 band-sweep variants are recorded with verdict **`park`** (they pass the literal bar and have a real zero-fee edge, but are net-of-cost inferior — parked pending a cost-optimized redesign, not adopted). Each carries a `net_of_cost_sharpe` metric. The iter-046 `adopt` records stand as the literal-bar audit; report `06` now carries a forward-note to this correction.
- **Deployment stays human-gated** and is now moot for A1 as-built; the holdout was not touched.
- **Cost-optimization tested (iter-048, above)**: no rebalance cadence rescues A1 **with the confirmed-bear short** — the short's borrow carry is a cadence-insensitive ~5 %/yr structural drag, so the offset-mean net-of-cost never clears gated-B1's 1.047. But margin-waived it does clear, isolating the **short's carry** (not the turnover) as the killer.
- **The promising next test (autonomous, reversible)**: a **long/flat (short=off) A1** — it avoids the borrow carry entirely and its margin-waived net-of-cost already edges past 1.047 at weekly/biweekly. The catch is the disqualifying 2014 drawdown the short was rescuing; whether a lower-cadence long/flat A1 nets above gated-B1 *despite* that 2014 exposure is the distinct, unsettled question this finding surfaces. Then **A2** (per-asset TSMOM).
- **Human-gated (from this finding)**: the kill-bar **net-of-cost SPA refinement** (run the "beats benchmark" leg net-of-cost), a change to the pre-registered validation protocol.
