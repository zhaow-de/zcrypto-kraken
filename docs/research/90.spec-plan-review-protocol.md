# Reviewing an implementation plan before it is executed: four controlled runs

**Status:** complete. Four controlled runs, 2026-08-30 to 2026-09-01, on four distinct subject artefacts, plus one **field application** of the derived protocol on 2026-09-01 to 02 (§9), which corrected three of the findings below.
**Scope:** the review step that sits between writing an implementation plan and executing it.
**Self-containment:** this document is the durable record of the experiment. The working directories and git refs that held the raw arms are removed; every figure required to check a claim is reproduced below; two trajectory figures measured by the single-lineage loop that preceded run 2 are labelled as such where they appear.

## 1. Framing

The pattern under study is the one Claude Code's `superpowers` plugin implements. It separates *what to build* from *how to build it*, and separates both from *building it*:

1. a **specification** (the `brainstorming` skill's output) records the decisions — what the system must do, and which alternatives were rejected;
2. an **implementation plan** (`writing-plans`) decomposes the specification into ordered tasks, each carrying the exact code, commands and tests an implementer will produce;
3. **execution** (`subagent-driven-development`) hands each task to a fresh subagent that sees only that task's text and a short block of global constraints — never the whole plan, never the other tasks, never the specification.

The third property is what makes the second document load-bearing. An implementer cannot resolve an ambiguity by reading ahead, cannot notice that a symbol is introduced two tasks later, and cannot tell that a test it is told to write can never fail. Every such defect is discovered at execution time, when the cost of repair is highest, or is not discovered at all and ships.

The review that precedes execution is therefore the only place where the pair can be corrected cheaply. Its design is nonetheless usually left implicit: dispatch a reviewer, read what comes back, fix it, repeat until the reports look clean. Four questions follow, and none of them has an obvious answer:

- **When does the loop stop?** "Until no findings remain" is unreachable on a large artefact; "until the reviewer reports zero serious findings" delegates the decision to the party whose leniency is unmeasured.
- **Is another round the best use of the next unit of budget**, or is a second independent reviewer?
- **Does the reviewing model matter**, and at which step — review, or repair?
- **What can this method not do at all**, however many rounds it runs?

The runs below were designed to answer these by measurement rather than by argument. All four share one dependent variable — the defects surviving in the artefact — and differ in what they hold constant.

## 2. Conclusions

Stated first; the hypotheses, setup, results and findings that support each one follow in §3–§6.

01. **The loop's own verdict is not a stopping signal, and optimising for rounds-to-converge selects the most lenient reviewer.** Use a fixed budget plus an outside judgement.
02. **Stop on consequence.** The terminating condition is that the remaining findings no longer threaten the property the artefact exists to protect — which requires every finding to state its consequence, not merely its severity.
03. **Buy breadth before depth.** A second independent reviewer is the cheapest quality available; rounds are a poor substitute, because each inherits the previous round's framing.
04. **Make the reviewers disjoint by design.** Explicitly different lenses, each told what the others cover, achieve the same low overlap as sampling diversity at both artefact sizes measured.
05. **Consolidate mechanically and never re-grade downward.** Maximum severity across reviewers, no judging consolidator.
06. **Run a contract pin first, always.** Verify by execution every premise the pair asserts about an external system, and hand its output to the reviewers as fact. It is the only step that can catch a false premise, and no reviewer can perform it from the text.
07. **Add an executability pass last.** Assemble and run what the plan describes. It finds a class — constructor arity, fixture contracts, substitution scope — that semantic review is structurally blind to, and it is wasted if run first.
08. **Budget the blind gate as a complement to the second lineage**, never as a substitute, and never let its clean count stand as evidence that no serious defect exists.
09. **Fix families, not instances**, and record the enumeration in the artefact.
10. **Repair is not free**: roughly half the budget and a steady tax of new defects independent of model. Fewer, better-informed repair rounds beat more of them, and the repairer must retain an explicit licence to refuse.
11. **Harvest before archiving.** Any arm about to be discarded is diffed against the survivor first.

## 3. Hypotheses

Stated before the runs that test them, and each is falsifiable by a count.

- **H1 (stopping rule).** The loop's own reports are a valid stopping signal: a round returning no serious findings indicates an artefact with few serious defects.
- **H2 (depth versus breadth).** Additional rounds of one reviewer and one additional independent reviewer are substitutes; the cheaper of the two dominates.
- **H3 (model).** A more capable model (Claude Fable over Claude Opus) finds a superset of what the lesser one finds, so model selection is a cost-quality trade with a frontier to choose a point on.
- **H4 (repair).** Repair is a low-risk mechanical step; the cost of a review-and-repair loop is dominated by review.
- **H5 (completeness).** A sufficiently thorough review of a specification and plan can establish that the described work is correct.

## 4. Experimental setup

### 4.1 Common instruments

In runs 2 and 3 every reviewer, repairer and adjudicator was a Claude Code subagent dispatched through the Agent tool with a cold context and an explicitly named model; run 1's arm-A repairs were the orchestrator's own, with full session context (§7), and run 1's adjudicating panel and run 4's pin and lenses ran as subagents of a Claude Code Workflow script. Three prompt templates were held byte-identical across every arm of runs 2 and 3, so that model selection was the only variable:

- a **reviewer** template — cold context, a severity rule, a required output shape, and a rule that a finding which cannot be quoted is not a finding;
- a **repairer** template — fix every serious finding, amend the specification where it is wrong or silent, and an explicit licence to *refuse* a finding verified to be wrong;
- an **adjudicator** template — used on the final artefact only, once per arm in run 2 and once on run 3's surviving arm, denied the review reports and the loop's commit messages, and told to assume nothing had been caught.

The severity rule is the experiment's most important instrument and is reproduced here with its second-person phrasing made impersonal (the template's enumeration of Minor examples and its closing admonition are omitted), because every count in this document depends on it:

> A finding is **Critical or Important only if a concrete failure scenario can be stated in which it changes what ships, what an operator does, or what a number says.** Write that scenario out. If one cannot be written, the finding is Minor — by construction, regardless of how wrong it feels.
> **Critical** — the scenario reaches production, is irreversible, loses data, or defeats a safety guard. **Important** — meets that bar but is recoverable. **Minor** — everything else.

### 4.2 The four runs

| run | subject artefact | design | isolated variable |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 1 | a small operations-tooling pair | two arms, Opus versus Fable as reviewer, six and two rounds | model — **confounded**, see §7 |
| 2 | a 74-line specification, 90-line plan, 12 tasks | four arms crossing {Opus, Fable} × {review, repair}, five rounds each, blind adjudication per arm | model, per role |
| 3 | a 92-line specification, **3,383-line** plan, 13 tasks | two arms, Opus versus Sonnet as repairer, five rounds, blind adjudication, cross-arm harvest | repairer model at 37× the size |
| 4 | an 8-decision specification, a 654-line 5-task plan | contract pin → two lens-differentiated reviewers → mechanical union | review *structure* rather than model |

Runs 2 and 3 both forked every arm from a single frozen commit, so round 1 of every arm reviewed a byte-identical artefact; in run 3 both arms also received the same round-1 review, so they diverge from round 2. That property is what makes cross-arm coverage comparable at round 1 and only at round 1; from round 2 each arm reviews text its own repairer has changed.

### 4.3 Measurements

One row per agent invocation: run, arm, round, role, model, tokens, duration, findings by severity, findings attributable to the immediately preceding repair, and the resulting commit. Three Claude models appear. **Claude Opus** performed every role unless a table says otherwise; **Claude Fable** (priced at twice Opus per token) and **Claude Sonnet** (priced at 0.4× Opus) were each compared against it. Every cost below is in Opus-equivalent tokens: Fable raw tokens × 2 (run 2 and §5.4), Sonnet raw tokens ÷ 2.5 (run 3).

Two measurement decisions matter for reproducibility:

- **Per-agent attribution came from Claude Code's task notifications — the per-agent usage line the Agent tool returns — not from transcripts.** An aggregate figure for a multi-agent run cannot separate a cheap agent from an expensive one, and the per-agent transcripts carried partial usage records — one agent reporting 923 output tokens across 32 turns for a run that ended in a 700-word report. The orchestration was chosen to expose a per-agent figure.
- **Finding counts were derived from the report body, never from the summary the reviewer returned.** §6.6 explains why.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 Run 2 — four arms, one artefact, blind adjudication

Rounds fixed at five. "Own final verdict" is the last review's counts; "adjudicated" is the blind pass on the final artefact.

| arm | reviewer | repairer | cost (Opus-eq) | own final verdict | **blind-adjudicated** |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| A | Opus | Opus | **1,951k** | 1C / 2I / 11M | **0C · 0I · 5M** |
| D | Opus | Fable | 2,346k | 1C / 2I / 11M | 0C · 2I · 5M |
| B | Fable | Fable | 3,227k | 0C / 3I / 7M | 0C · 2I · 8M |
| C | Fable | Opus | 2,301k | **0C / 0I / 8M** *(self-declared converged)* | **0C · 4I · 6M** |
| C2 | C plus one Minor-only sweep |  | +508k | 0C / 0I / 3M | 0C · 1I · 9M |

**The arm that declared itself converged adjudicated worst; the arm still reporting a Critical adjudicated best, at the lowest cost of any arm.** Self-reported convergence was anti-correlated with artefact quality. **H1 is falsified.**

Round-1 coverage, measured against a 40-defect union assembled by clustering all four arms' round-1 findings and adjudicating each cluster:

| arm | defects found, of 40 | round-1 cost |
| -- | -- | -- |
| A | **27 (68%)** | 355k |
| D | 20 (50%) | 524k |
| B | 13 (32%) | 675k |
| C | 12 (30%) | 470k |

**20 of the 40 defects were found by exactly one arm.** The two Opus reviewers overlapped on only 12 findings and unioned to 35/40 (88%). **H2 is falsified in the direction of breadth**: a second independent reviewer buys 20 points of coverage for roughly the cost of one review, while a second round of the same reviewer inherits the first round's framing of what the document is about.

The Minor-only sweep at the serious-finding floor (arm C2) cost 508k, took the arm from 4 surviving Importants to 1, and **introduced no new defects** — the best marginal return measured anywhere in the four runs. At the floor the remaining edits are local, which is why they do not propagate.

### 5.2 Run 2 — the repair tax

In round 2 — the round after the largest structural repairs — every repairer introduced 4–6 new defects irrespective of model; across the whole run — every review row — 43 of 256 findings (17%) were attributable to the preceding repair:

| arm | repairer | findings returned by the round-2 review | of them introduced by the preceding repair | ratio |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| A | Opus | 19 | 6 | 32% |
| D | Fable | 16 | 4 | 25% |
| C | Opus | 10 | 6 | 60% |
| B | Fable | 8 | 5 | 62% |

The ratio differs only because a weaker review surfaces fewer *other* defects, inflating the denominator's complement. **The absolute count is flat across models. A better repairer does not reduce the tax; a smaller repair does** — §9.1 measures a dependence on repair SIZE that this run could not see, its four repairs being of similar size. **H4 is falsified**: repair is not low-risk, and it is roughly half the budget.

Cause taxonomy of the 21 round-2 defects, recoverable per-defect because each carried a quoted line and a failure scenario (rows approximate; one defect fits two classes):

| class | count |
| -- | -- |
| invented a justification without verifying it | ~6 |
| edited locally, broke a consequence elsewhere in the pair | ~5 |
| wrote a mechanism that cannot work | ~4 |
| added a requirement with no deliverer | ~2 |
| re-introduced the thing warned against in the same passage | ~2 |
| ignored the execution model | ~1 |

The largest class was already covered by an explicit item in the repairer's own self-check, and shipped anyway. The asymmetry worth exploiting: the reviewer template demands **evidence** (the line judged must be quoted) and reviewers comply near-perfectly; the repairer template demanded only a **promise** that it had checked.

### 5.3 Run 3 — the same result at 37× the artefact size, and a sharper failure

Two arms, five rounds, the reviewer held at Opus, the repairer varied.

|  | arm A (Opus repairer) | arm B (Sonnet repairer) |
| -- | -- | -- |
| findings by round (C/I/M) | 0/2/12 → 0/2/8 → 0/1/8 → **1**/1/9 → 0/1/7 | 0/2/12 (arm A's report, paired) → 0/2/10 → 0/2/11 → 0/1/11 → **0/0/13** |
| defects introduced by repair | 0, 1, 2, 0 = **3** | 1, 0, 2, 4 = **7** |
| repair cost per round (first four repair rounds) | 208,759 | **86,178 — 41%** |

Three results, each stronger than run 2's version of the same claim.

**A Critical hid in the Minor column for three rounds.** One line — opening a controlling terminal in a mode the pinned language runtime rejects — made the artefact's central command impossible to invoke. Four Opus reviewers graded that one line **not raised (round 1) → Minor and Important (round 2, one per arm) → not raised (round 3) → Critical (round 4)**. The reviewer that graded it correctly was the one that **executed** it. Filed as a Minor it was legitimately skippable, the repairer skipped it with a stated reason, and every later round inherited an artefact in which the line had already been "considered". Nothing was done wrong procedurally, and the arm's counters read zero Critical through those three rounds.

**Five review rounds left two Criticals that a parallel arm had already fixed.** A cross-arm harvest, performed before discarding the arm judged not worth pursuing, reproduced both against the artefact's own files: a directory the command writes into is never created, so the command dies at its first action; and a single unreadable input aborts an account-wide sweep before its first write. **The first was invisible to any test the surviving arm could have written, because its own fixture created the directory unconditionally** — a harness that cannot expose, one level above the familiar problem of a fixture that cannot discriminate.

**The blind adjudication did not substitute for the second arm.** It missed both harvested Criticals — while quoting a line adjacent to one of them — and found a third defect no other reader saw. It remains the highest-yield *single* review in the process, and it is not sufficient.

**Repairer model interacts with one specific edit.** The Sonnet repairer matched the Opus one on ordinary plan edits, reproduced findings before acting, and declined findings it judged wrong. Its excess over the other arm was concentrated in one round — four defects against none — and **two of those four landed in the specification it had just amended.** The specification is the highest-stakes edit a repairer makes and the one no reviewer is guaranteed to re-read.

### 5.4 Run 3 — Opus versus Fable as reviewer, head-to-head

Opus and Fable as reviewer, identical prompts, same commit, on the large artefact:

|  | Fable | Opus |
| -- | -- | -- |
| findings | 3 (0C/0I/3M) | **9** (0C/**1I**/8M) |
| unique to it | **1** | **7** |
| normalised cost | 541,782 | **320,605** |
| **verification markers** | **8** | 2 |
| defects-introduced-by-repair flagged | **1** | 0 |

Opus covered 9 of the 10-finding union at 59% of the cost. **H3 is falsified as stated** — the relationship is not a superset, and there is no frontier to choose a point on for defect discovery.

**But the two agents were not performing the same task.** The lower-yield reviewer (Fable) *assembled the plan's code fences into real modules and ran them* — full test suites, every mutation probe applied and confirmed to kill its target, one probe checked to have killed for the *right reason* by reading the assertion text rather than the exit status, and an independent re-run of the previous repair's own discrimination check. Its single unique finding was a defect introduced by that repair, which the higher-yield review had scored as zero.

**A findings count measures defect discovery and scores establishing-that-it-works at zero.** The two are different jobs. Run 4 tests the consequence.

### 5.5 Run 4 — structure instead of model

Design: a **contract pin** that runs code to verify the premises the pair asserts about the existing system, then **two cold Opus reviewers with explicitly different lenses**, each told what the other covers, then a **mechanical max-severity union** with no consolidating agent.

| source | Critical | Important | Minor | total |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| contract pin | 0 | 4 | 3 | **7** |
| lens: safety and the write path | 0 | 5 | 1 | **6** |
| lens: coverage and consistency | 1 | 5 | 4 | **10** |
| **all three sources, unclustered** | **1** | **14** | **8** | **23** |

**Approximately 2 of the 16 lens findings were found by both lenses.** Run 2 measured overlap of 12/40 with two *identical* prompts on a 90-line plan; run 4 obtains comparable disjointness from two *lenses* on a plan of roughly 650 lines (654 at the reviewed commit). Directed diversity is at least as good as sampling diversity at both sizes measured.

**The pin returned four Importants before a single review ran**, including one that falsified a premise **both documents asserted**. The specification claimed a repricing routine was reachable only from two crossing surfaces, on the strength of that routine's own docstring; the pin enumerated the call sites, found a third caller that fires on any venue-originated cancellation, and ran it. The plan had instructed implementers *not* to add a guard there — so the defect would have shipped under an explicit prohibition against repairing it.

**The max-severity rule paid for itself on its first run.** The same defect — an alert threshold above the metric's structural ceiling, so the rule could never fire — returned **Critical** from one lens and **Important** from the other. Any consolidator that averages, votes, or picks a primary ships it as Important. In run 4 the union was a concatenation — the two lens findings on the threshold carried different subject names and both stand in the raw record; the property measured is that the Critical was not re-graded downward, not that a key was merged.

**Then the executability pass (Fable), run afterwards and cold, returned 3 Importants that both lenses and the code-running pin had all missed.** All three were defects that would have broken *during execution* rather than shipping wrong behaviour: two new fields added with no defaults, breaking four existing constructor sites, one of them in a file no task's test command covers; a test requesting a fixture whose precondition the code path can never produce, failing at teardown on a healthy run with a message inviting the implementer to change live behaviour to satisfy it; and a rendered artefact using a substitution that only one of two publishing paths performs, so it would land inert while every existing check passed.

**This is the run's most useful output.** The two-lens union is strong on *what the artefact says* — contradictions, coverage, guards that cannot discriminate, false premises. It was blind to *whether the artefact can be executed against the current tree*. Those are separable capabilities, and the ordering matters: lenses first, because they are cheaper per finding; the executability pass last, because otherwise it spends its budget on defects the lenses find for a third of the cost.

## 6. Findings

### 6.1 No counter is a stopping signal

Three independent demonstrations: an arm that self-declared zero serious findings adjudicated worst (§5.1); an arm whose counters read zero Critical for three rounds while carrying an unusable command (§5.3); an arm reaching zero serious findings first while carrying thirteen Minors, four of them created by the immediately preceding repair, two of those in the specification (§5.3).

A **repair round produces no measurement.** Its output is a *hypothesis* about the artefact's state. In run 3 a repair claimed the last Important closed; the next review measured one Critical and two new defects. The serious-finding count of a round is the *review's* number, and a repair contributes none until a subsequent review takes one.

### 6.2 Zero is often not reachable, so the stop is a judgement about consequence

Important findings behave asymptotically on large artefacts. Two independent trajectories:

- 10 → 6 → 5 → 5 → 2 → 2 → 2 → 1 → 1 (nine rounds — the single-lineage delivery loop on run 2's artefact that preceded run 2, recorded in that loop's ledger)
- 2 → 2 → 1 → 1 → 1 (five rounds)

Each round keeps buying roughly one real defect for roughly one round's cost, and the last ones found were genuinely new and genuinely worth having. **Both trajectories here fall monotonically, and that is an artefact of two samples**: the field application's Importants went 14 → 8 → **9** → 5 → 4, rising at round 3 (§9). A stop keyed on the count falling therefore fires early on a real artefact. Critical clears first, Important second, Minor oscillates and never settles — so a stopping rule on *total* findings never terminates, and one on Critical alone stops far too early.

The signal that does work is **consequence**. One repair sequence produced a finding at three successive depths: a revert that died with its session (permanent data loss); a scheduled safety timer that could not be re-armed while its predecessor was still pending (a pause with no automatic end); and a failed timer blocking the re-arm with the recovery command unnamed (**a stalled recovery with the safety property already held**). Each was real. What changed was the cost, and the third review said so itself. **That sentence is the stopping signal: the remaining findings no longer threaten the property the artefact exists to protect.** A review that states each finding's consequence hands that judgement over directly; one that ranks severity alone does not.

Severity is partly a property of the grader — of nine round-1 defects where arms disagreed serious-versus-Minor, independent adjudication called three genuinely Important, and majority vote matched the adjudicator only five times in nine. Consequence is checkable in a way severity is not.

### 6.3 Coverage dominates depth, and it is not a model effect

Measured four times at increasing severity: 20/40 defects found by exactly one arm; the same defect found by two arms one round apart; **five review rounds leaving two Criticals a parallel arm had fixed**; and two lenses overlapping on 2 of 16. In the second and third, every reviewer was Opus; in the first, the two Opus reviewers alone overlapped on 12 of their 35.

Two corollaries. **The blind gate is a complement, not a substitute** — it found two Importants that the ten single-lineage rounds preceding run 2 had missed on that same artefact, and missed two Criticals on another while finding a third defect nobody else saw. Budget both. And **harvest before archiving**: diffing a discarded arm against the surviving one cost a single read and recovered two Criticals that would otherwise have shipped.

### 6.4 A reviewer that only reads is guessing

The difference between Minor and Critical on the terminal-mode line was execution. **A plan built of complete code fences is a program**; "does this plan's code work" is answerable by assembling the fences and running them. Observed three times, each time performed by the party that found the most: a repairer that assembled the module and its tests, ran them, then *deleted its own fix and watched the new fixture go red*; a reviewer that ran every mutation probe and checked one killed for the right reason; a harvest that reproduced both Criticals before reporting them.

Related, and general: **an introspection predicate is a claim about a language-level attribute, not about behaviour.** A coroutine-function check returned False for seven methods that nonetheless required an event loop, because they were native bindings. Calling the method gave the truth; the introspection API gave a confident wrong answer.

### 6.5 Document review cannot falsify a wrong premise — H5 is false

The decisive limitation, discovered during execution of run 3's artefact after roughly 1,900 passing tests: **the command the pair described could not run at all.** Every client call required an event loop the entry point never established, and a book-side accessor was a method read as an attribute. Neither document contained the words `async`, `await`, `asyncio`, `event loop` or `coroutine` — zero occurrences in both, verified by search.

Five review rounds on one arm, a complete second arm, a blind adjudication and an Opus-versus-Fable head-to-head had all read this pair. **None of them could have found it, and that is not a failure of any reviewer.** The defect is not *in* the documents; the documents are internally consistent. The defect is in their shared premise about an external system, and no amount of reading falsifies a premise.

Every finding count in run 3 is therefore a count of defects *within a shared false premise*, and nothing in runs 1 and 2 could have detected one either. The comparisons remain valid — both arms read the same documents — but the ceiling this places on the method is invisible from inside it.

**The remedy is a step no reviewer can perform from the text.** For every external system the specification assumes: call it and record what it returns. Not read its documentation, not check a type stub, not reason from the name. Run 4 implemented this as the contract pin and it returned four Importants before any review ran (§5.5).

A narrower instance of the same class: **a docstring that names a function's callers is not an enumeration of its callers.** It states what the author intended; the dangerous caller is the one they did not mean. To claim what reaches a function, enumerate its call sites.

### 6.6 The instruments themselves carry defects

- **Returned summary counts under-report.** In three of six reviews in one run the returned four-line summary disagreed with the report body — always an under-count, always by exactly one. A second artefact reproduced it independently. Any rule that gates on the returned figure gates on a number nobody checked. Derive counts from the body.
- **The regression metric under-counts by construction.** A two-value label conflates *shipped with the artefact* with *created by the loop two rounds ago*, because both are "not the most recent repair's doing". Three labels are needed: in-original, introduced-by-an-earlier-repair, introduced-by-last-repair. The middle one is the interesting one and nothing recorded it.
- **"Review X" without "report on X" is unfalsifiable.** A reviewer asked to review an unreviewed specification amendment passed silently; a clean pass is indistinguishable from a skip. Name where the verdict goes — a required line in the report.
- **An instruction to classify must name the field and its position.** A reviewer asked to triage each Minor restated the vocabulary in its report header and applied it to nothing.
- **Templates are not portable.** Every new subject broke an assumption at render time — an isolation rule contradicting where reports live, a log range assuming a base that had moved. Neither was an agent failing. Rendering a template against a new subject is itself a review step.
- **Never patch instruments mid-run, including with the run's own lessons.** The pull to improve an instrument while it is measuring is strong and constant; one run had to stop and re-dispatch an arm's repair when that repairer had received coaching the others did not.

### 6.7 What makes a repair good

- **A repairer that cannot refuse degrades the artefact one round at a time**, invisibly, because the counts fall. Both run-3 repairers refused findings they verified wrong; the clause licensing this is load-bearing.
- **A skip with a stated reason is a first-class outcome.** One repairer skipped a finding as impossible; the next round's reviewer read the reason and accepted it, rather than re-finding it. But **a skipped finding is not closed and nothing re-opens it** — the terminal-mode Critical rode three rounds precisely because a legitimate skip made it invisible background. Skips need an expiry: re-adjudicated by a later round, never inherited as settled.
- **Specification amendments must be enumerated in the report.** An amendment made silently is indistinguishable from a repairer redefining the standard it is measured against. And where a specification's hash is pinned by an external record, it is immutable — amending it silently breaks the record that verifies it. One repairer checked this unprompted; no template mentioned it.
- **The best repairs extend rather than restate.** Two things that must agree should be made *unable* to disagree; restating one in the other is how the next round's regression is born. This was the single most repeated observation across all four runs.
- **A repair that rewrites a test must state what the new assertion can still fail on.** One rewrote a test into a tautology the templating engine made unreachable.
- **A repair that introduces a new mechanism inherits that mechanism's failure modes**, and the review that approved the repair does not examine them. Each new mechanism — a timer, a lock, a file, a unit — earns its own scoped re-review whose subject is the mechanism, not the finding it closed.

### 6.8 Fix the family, not the instance

The highest-leverage single instruction found in any run. One defect shape in run 3's artefact — *one bad input raises before the first write, so the operation completes having done nothing* — had **five** trigger points. Three expensive independent readers found exactly one each; none generalised. A seven-line instruction to enumerate the family found all five, including one that would have **reported success over an unfinished operation**.

The artefact had even stated the principle in one of its own test docstrings, and applied it once.

**When a defect is found, name the family, enumerate its members exhaustively, and record the enumeration in the artefact**, so the next reader inherits it instead of re-deriving it. Cheaper than a round and strictly more productive.

A companion, from execution: **a guard combining N conditions, tested only with a fixture satisfying every one, never exercises the operator between them.** Five instances appeared on a single subsequent branch. The detector is a per-term sweep, not a per-guard probe; the fix is a fixture satisfying exactly one half. Related: a parametrised guard deserves a per-parameter probe — measured on one such guard, four of six parameters individually failed to discriminate while the aggregate reported success.

## 7. Threats to validity

- **Run 1 is confounded and is reported for completeness only.** Two variables moved together: the reviewing model, and *who performed the repair* — one arm repaired via the orchestrator with full context, the other via a cold agent working from a written, file-verified report. That discipline alone could account for most of the difference. Runs 2 and 3 isolate the variables run 1 conflated.
- **Small samples.** One artefact per run; one adjudicator per arm. Run 2's subject was a 90-line plan, small enough that additional depth may have had no room to pay.
- **The pair's author ran the review in run 4.** The reviewers were cold, but prompt authorship was not blind, so a lens may have been aimed where the author already suspected trouble. Runs 2 and 3 share this property.
- **Independence versus sampling is not separated.** A registered control — one reviewer run twice, union — was never executed. "Two reviewers" may be a sampling effect rather than a diversity effect; two *lenses* is a third condition, not that control.
- **Severity grading is reviewer-dependent** (§6.2), so any count in this document inherits that noise. The blind adjudication exists to place a single consistent grader at the end of each arm, which is why adjudicated counts and not self-reported ones are used for ranking.
- **Cost normalisation is a price ratio, not a throughput ratio.** The Sonnet repairer in run 3 consumed *more* raw tokens and more tool calls than the Opus one; the saving is a billing effect, so wall-clock and rate limits do not improve.
- **Model conclusions are scoped to this task kind.** Two artefacts, one class of work. Opus dominated Fable for *defect discovery* here, while Fable was the only one that established the artefact runs.

## 8. Registered but unexecuted

Recorded so that the absence of a result is not read as a null result. **The three-label origin scheme registered in §6.6 is no longer among them: §9 executed it.**

- **One reviewer run twice, union** — the control separating independence from sampling (§7).
- **An evidentiary repairer self-check** — requiring a `file:line` per new claim written, mirroring the demand the reviewer template already makes. The largest regression class is *invented a justification without verifying it*, and the reviewer-side analogue of that demand produces near-perfect compliance.
- **A second grader on Minors only** — three of nine disputed Minors adjudicated as genuinely Important, so the population at risk from a best-effort Minor policy is measurable and the mitigation is far cheaper than a second full review.
- **A mechanical blast-radius pass after each repair** — for each changed line, search the pair for anything citing it. Two of the six regression classes are exactly this, and it needs no model judgement.

## 9. Field application, and the three findings it corrects

The protocol derived above was applied once, as written, to a real specification and plan on a live branch —
a capture-path detector and two alert rules, 98 and 253 lines at the outset — over 2026-09-01 to 02. It is
**not** a controlled run: one artefact, no arms, no blind adjudication of the protocol against an
alternative. Its value is that it is the first application in which the protocol's own predictions could
fail, and three of them did.

### 9.1 The regression tax scales with the size of the repair

Five repairs, each followed by review. "Opened" counts findings labelled `last-fix` in the next review of
that repair — its own scoped re-review plus the following round's two lenses — which is the only population
that can be attributed to it:

| repair | diff (lines) | opened |
| -- | -- | -- |
| 5 | 39 | 4 |
| 4 | 94 | 8 |
| 2 | 101 | 8 |
| 3 | 103 | 11 |
| 1 | 321 | **18** |

Ordered by size, the count is monotone, and sub-linear: an eight-fold larger diff opened four to five times
as many defects, so the rate per line falls as the repair grows (5.6 per 100 lines at 321, 8–11 per 100
below that). §5.2's "4–6 new defects irrespective of model" was measured on one round of one run — round 2,
the round after that run's largest structural repairs — and is a point on this curve rather than a constant:
it is what a repair of a few tens of lines costs. The two readings differ in what they license. A per-round
constant leaves only one lever, fewer rounds; a size-dependent quantity makes the size of each repair a lever
within the round, which is the instruction the field application's later repairers were given and the reason
its last repair opened four.

### 9.2 The Important trajectory can rise, so a plateau rule stops early

Union counts across five rounds: **3C/14I → 0C/8I → 0C/9I → 0C/5I → 0C/4I**. Criticals cleared after round 1
and never returned; Importants rose at round 3. §6.2's two trajectories both fall monotonically, which
supports reading them as a smooth asymptote, and a rule of the form *stop when the count stops falling* would
fire here at round 3.

What that costs is measurable, and it is not the loop's own regressions — those would not exist had the loop
stopped. It is the defects **present in the artefact from the start** that later rounds still found: rounds 4
and 5's lenses returned 2 and 1 `in-original` findings, and the three floor passes returned 11 more, all
`in-original`. Fourteen defects that were in the pair before any repair touched it were found after the point
a plateau rule would have stopped. Keys fall monotonically here (22 → 17 → 15 → 11 → 10) and are the better
convergence signal; the stop was ultimately taken on the consequence statement, per conclusion 02.

### 9.3 The three-label origin scheme, executed

§6.6 records that a two-value regression label conflates *shipped with the artefact* with *created by the
loop two rounds ago*, names the missing middle label as the interesting one, and §8 registered it as
unexecuted. The field application labelled every finding `in-original` / `earlier-fix` / `last-fix`.

At round 3 the ten lens findings were **1 `last-fix`, 9 `earlier-fix`, 0 `in-original`**: the immediately
preceding repair introduced one, while nine had been opened by the repair two rounds earlier and surfaced
only then.

§6.6's two-value scheme splits on *the most recent repair's doing* versus not, so all nine fall on the "not"
side and read as **shipped with the artefact** — charged to the original author, to no repair at all. The
loop's own damage is therefore under-counted, exactly as §6.6 predicts, and by nine at a single round. The
middle label is not a refinement: without it those nine are invisible as loop damage, §9.1's table cannot be
built, and a run would conclude its repairs were nearly free.

### 9.4 What the protocol found, and what it cost

Three Criticals at round 1, none after. One was refused by the repairer and the artefact strengthened
instead — the refusal was independently re-derived by five parties, three of them by execution, and the
specification now records the argument it had been relying on silently. The contract pin refuted eight
premises by execution before any reviewer read the pair, including a fixture path that did not exist and a
probe that could not run where the plan placed it. The executability pass assembled and ran all seven tasks
end to end. Thirteen Minors were left — 5 from the executability pass, 6 from the blind gate, and 2 the Minor sweep recorded under `## Left` because their hunks fall inside owner-authorised text — each with a consequence its reviewer states is not production-reaching.

**The protocol's own defects, found only by running it:** a report-shape notation that every agent read as
choose-one, so the consolidator counted eleven real findings — including a Critical — as zero; dispatching
the pin and the lenses in one automated sequence, which leaves no step in which to discharge the pin's
host-bound premises; a pin that wrote a guessed path into a command and delegated the checking to whoever
ran it. None was visible from reading the protocol.
