## Why the daemon's own numbers were not used

T0003 records the trap: `GapMonitor` is in-process and resets on restart, so it cannot see the damage a restart does. It scored the 2026-07-13 crash at **~5.5 s** when the hour that crash clobbered actually lost **270 s** — a ~50× undercount. Every figure here is therefore derived from **segment-timestamp continuity** in the archive itself, via `infra/scripts/continuity.py`, run against a *pulled copy* (never the live directory the daemon is writing).

The instrument counts three gap kinds per **book** stream (books update continuously, so silence *is* downtime; trades legitimately go quiet and cannot measure uptime): a missing hour (3600 s), head/tail truncation, and intra-hour silence beyond a per-pair threshold derived from the data (p99.99 of inter-row spacing × 10, floored at 5 s — so natural quiescence cannot register as a gap).

## Instrument check — why the PASS is trustworthy, not an artifact

A verdict is only as good as the instrument, and this one was cross-examined four ways:

1. **The gap budget reconciles with independently-recorded events.** The two incidents sum to **83 + 270 = 353 s**. Measured per-pair gap is 361.6–393.4 s. The 8.6–40.4 s remainder is intra-hour silence — and it is *larger on the thinner pairs* (BTC +8.6 s, ETH +8.9 s → LTC +40.4 s, DOT +37.4 s), exactly as market structure predicts. ~93 % of the measured gap is accounted for by two events documented before this measurement existed.
2. **The measurement reproduces across time.** iter-095 ran the same instrument on 2026-07-14 and recorded **0.0890 %** worst stream, attributing 86 % of the budget to the same two events. Today's 0.0624 % is the *same 353 s* over a longer denominator (125 h → 175 h): 353/450000 ≈ 0.078 % then, 353/630000 ≈ 0.056 % now, each plus per-pair silence. The bar did not pass because something changed; it passed because nothing did.
3. **A lagging pull cannot fabricate a pass or a failure.** `span_hours` runs first→*last observed* segment, so hours not yet pulled end the span rather than counting as missing.
4. **The hash verification is independent of the pull's own exit code.** `verify_tree` re-hashes each segment against the `.sha256` manifest **the capture daemon wrote on the VPS at finalization**, so a tree that verifies proves the bytes survived every hop unaltered.

## The clean-run clock — why 2026-07-13 does not reset it

The window contains two incidents, so the clock's start is a judgment worth stating explicitly (ratified by the owner, 2026-07-16; decision logged `[iter-099]`).

The governing precedent is T0003's own ruling on the 2026-07-11 reboot: *"a within-budget gap is not a corruption event, so the ≥7-day clean-run clock continues from 2026-07-08"*. The 2026-07-13 crash is the same class: it cost 270 s of L2, but that loss is **honestly counted as a gap** and every surviving segment verifies (3738/3738).

The distinction that matters is **missing data versus wrong data**. The 2026-07-08 desync *did* restart the clock, because it produced checksum-**invalid** book segments — data that looks fine but is wrong, which silently poisons downstream research. A gap is visible, bounded, and priced into the \<0.1 % budget by design; corruption is none of those things. Only corruption restarts a clean-run clock.

Under that reading the clock runs from the 2026-07-08 ~20:07 UTC recovery, elapsing 7 days at 2026-07-15 ~20:07 UTC. The measured window does not depend on that start, though: it lies **strictly after** the recovery and is itself 7.29 consecutive clean days, so it satisfies "≥7 consecutive days" on its own. Nor does it depend on the extra hours — over a strict 7-day denominator the same 353 s is 353/604800 = **0.0584 %**, still inside the bar.

## Honest residuals

- **~270 s of L2 across 10 pairs on 2026-07-13 07:00 is permanently lost** (unbackfillable). It is inside the budget, not hidden by it.
- The window includes ~71 h of post-fix operation. The fixes' *own* verification (a venue WS restart ridden out in-process) is tracked in T0035, not discharged here.
- Trades are excluded from the gap measurement **by design** — a quiet pair legitimately prints nothing, so for trades silence is not downtime and cannot measure uptime. The gap figures above are a **book** measurement, which is the mission-critical, unbackfillable stream.
- **The "all hashes match" leg is strong for books and weaker for trades**, and this report does not claim otherwise. T0026 documents that a reconnect trade-snapshot can silently overwrite an already-finalized trade hour **and regenerate its manifest** — so the segment hashes cleanly while being shrunk. Hash verification cannot see that class; books are structurally unaffected (book snapshots carry current timestamps, so the book writer never rewrites a past hour). Trade completeness therefore rests on T0026, T0043, and the deferred REST trade-backfill (T0050) rather than on this leg.
