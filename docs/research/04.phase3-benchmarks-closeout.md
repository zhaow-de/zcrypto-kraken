## The frozen bar

**gated-B1** — vol-targeted BTC (10%/yr realized-vol target, 30-day lookback, ≤1.0× leverage) × the 200-day long/flat regime gate.

| Property | Value |
| -- | -- |
| Full-history Sharpe (2013–2026) | **1.247** (95% CI ~[0.61, 1.88], robustly > 0 across block ∈ {8,16,24}, seed ∈ {1,42,99}) |
| Max drawdown | **12.3%** (vs BTC buy-and-hold's 82.5%) |
| Sharpe after Tier-1 maker fees | ~1.12 (clears §9.6: doesn't die at 1.5×; Sharpe 0.99 at 2×) |
| Worst calendar year (in-sample) | **−5.5%** (2014); B0 has four double-digit-loss years |
| Total return / annualized | 2.75× / 11.1%/yr (vs B0's 607× / 66.7% — the cost of the bull-year cap) |

**Chosen on** point estimate + highest/robustly-positive lower-bound Sharpe + by far the best drawdown control — **not** a statistically-significant Sharpe edge over B0/B1, which the bootstrap CIs show does not exist. Any Phase-4 alpha must beat this bar net of stressed costs to justify added complexity.

## Key findings (the dossier's substance)

1. **The bar's edge is bear-year *elimination*, not higher average return.** gated-B1 turns BTC's catastrophic bear years (2014 −50%, 2018 −73%, 2022 −62%) into near-flat (−5.5%, −5.2%, −0.5%) by going flat below the 200-day line; the cost is capping the explosive bull years (2017 +47% vs +1211%). That bear-year elimination is the source of the 12.3% drawdown.
2. **The benchmark comparisons are dominated by estimation uncertainty.** Full-history BTC Sharpes are all significantly positive (lower bounds ~0.43–0.61) but their CIs overlap — mutually indistinguishable. The common-window (4.3-yr) family is *entirely* within noise (every CI straddles zero) — a statistical-power problem.
3. **Multi-asset diversification did *not* beat single-asset BTC.** The naive inverse-vol majors basket (B2) *lost* money over its common window (0.68×, deeper drawdown than BTC) — 8 of 10 majors ended 47–95% down in the 2022 alt-crash and inverse-vol weighting still holds the losers. **Vol-targeting, not asset selection, is the edge** (BTC-vol-target and basket-vol-target both ≈ 0.45 Sharpe).
4. **Shorting backfires.** B4 (basket long/short) has negative Sharpe — the lagging 200-day gate shorts after the crash and is whipsawed by bear-market rallies. On the basket, the gate is also *redundant* with vol-targeting (unlike BTC, where gate × vol-target is the best).

## Carried forward — remainder (parked / deferred)

- **B4 risk-behavior confirmation (§12 handback — the one human decision).** The master plan flags "confirm B4's risk behavior is acceptable as the fallback deployable" as a decision point for the human. The autonomous characterization is done (B4 is poor — negative Sharpe); the human's call is whether B4 is an acceptable *fallback* if Phase 4 yields no survivor. It gates the fallback choice (resolved by Phase 5), **not** the start of Phase 4's alpha search (which compares against the primary frozen bar, gated-B1).
- **Captured-spread cost calibration (T0003).** As in Phases 1–2: the fee/margin cost structure is applied at confirmed rates; the per-pair spread term needs ≥2 weeks of captured L2 → T0003 (human-gated on VPS). Benchmark cost stress used the confirmed fee ladder, per §9.6.
- **Basket-turnover cost model + its §9.6 stress.** The basket rebalances daily; its net returns are the most fee-sensitive, but the basket is below the bar regardless, so a basket-specific cost model is low priority (deferred).
- **Full-history dynamic-composition basket (T0007).** The fixed 10-asset basket's common window is only 4.3 years (all CIs straddle zero); the full-history variant would have the statistical power the common window lacks.
- **SPA / White's Reality Check across the family.** Deferred: the bootstrap CIs already show the benchmark Sharpe differences are within noise; SPA's multiple-comparison correction is for Phase-4 alpha selection (many fitted trials), not a handful of pre-specified benchmarks.
