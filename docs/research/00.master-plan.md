## 1. Executive Summary<a name="1-executive-summary"></a>

**Market context (⏱ snapshot 2026-07-06).** This plan is written into a deep, mature bear market: BTC ≈ $61–63k, roughly −50% from the ~$126k all-time high, with a 21-month low printed in recent weeks; ETH ≈ $1,700. This context matters twice: (a) it is precisely the regime where the prior long-only system was forced to sit in cash, so the short-side extension is the highest-value new capability; and (b) any backtest that ends in mid-2026 embeds a bear-heavy sample — validation must slice regimes explicitly.

**The honest thesis.** The realistic best case for a $10k, single-venue, spot+margin retail account is a **disciplined, vol-targeted trend/regime rule with a low-leverage short-side extension for confirmed bear regimes** — i.e., beta-timing done well, now with a second (short) tail. Everything beyond that is an *upside experiment* with a strict kill bar, not a gate for deployment. The prior phases already demonstrated that ML selection subtracted value OOS and that the alpha families accessible from daily OHLCV + keyless data were empty after multiple-testing correction. What is genuinely *new* this cycle — and therefore where residual alpha probability is concentrated — is: (1) the short side (regime-gated), (2) intraday horizons (1h/4h, with 15m for execution), (3) intraday seasonality/microstructure features, and (4) Kraken-specific data (own full tick history, own captured L2 books).

**The binding economics.** Two numbers shape the entire design space (⏱ §3, §4):

1. **Trading fees.** Kraken Pro base tier is **0.25% maker / 0.40% taker** (\<$10k 30-day volume), stepping down (≈0.20/0.35 at $10k+, further at $50k+; from July 9, 2026 tiers can also qualify via assets-on-platform). A taker round trip at base tier costs **0.80%**. This *kills* short-horizon mean-reversion at our size and forces maker-first execution and holding periods of days, not minutes.
2. **Margin carry.** Kraken spot-margin charges an **opening fee of 0.01–0.05%** and a **rollover fee of the same rate every 4 hours** the position stays open. At 0.01%/4h that is ≈0.06%/day ≈ **22%/yr**; at 0.02%/4h ≈ **44%/yr**. This *kills* persistent market-neutral long/short books and persistent structural shorts. Shorts must be **tactical**: entered on regime/trend confirmation, sized small, held days-to-weeks, with the carry explicitly modeled as a hurdle.

**Deployable stack (target).** ~10–12 Kraken margin-enabled majors, EUR-quoted primary legs plus one or two BTC-quoted relative-value legs; 4h decision cadence with 1h regime/vol inputs and a 15m execution scheduler; vol-targeted long/flat/short trend ensemble; gross leverage ≤1.5×, net short ≤0.5×; maker-first execution.

**Framework decision (RATIFIED 2026-07-06 — Option 4, §7):** a **lean custom research + validation core** (polars/pandas + purpose-built CPCV/DSR/bootstrap/trial-registry layer + a small explicit-cost vectorized backtester) as the primary deliverable, with **NautilusTrader as the execution/paper-trading engine** — materially strengthened since the last review because NautilusTrader now ships an **official Kraken adapter** (Rust core, Python bindings; live market data + order execution for Kraken Spot; Kraken is a listed API partner). vectorbtpro is excluded per constraint (paid); no other paid framework is proposed. Qlib is not recommended (unmaintained; solves the wrong problem — plumbing, not validation).

**Data plan (§8):** $0/month at start. Kraken's own free full-history OHLCVT ZIPs (1m…1d) + full tick history via the Trades endpoint + a forward-running WebSocket L2/trade capture daemon, cross-checked against Binance Vision. Paid feeds (CryptoQuant ~$29–109, Glassnode ~$39/mo tiers, ⏱) are unlocked *only* after a feature family earns it in validation — total remains far under the $200/mo cap.

**Deepest risk, stated plainly (§13):** there may be **no capturable alpha beyond beta-timing** at this size and venue. The plan is built so that this outcome still produces a deployable product (the Phase 3 benchmark rule), an honest negative-results dossier, and a live risk-managed system — not a fabricated winner.

## 2. Venue Snapshot: Kraken as Given (capabilities inventory, not re-evaluation)<a name="2-venue-snapshot-kraken-as-given-capabilities-inventory-not-re-evaluation"></a>

Kraken is fixed. This section only inventories what the plan builds on. All items ⏱.

**Regulatory.** Kraken's EU entity (Payward Europe Solutions Ltd) holds a **MiCA CASP authorisation granted 25 June 2025 by the Central Bank of Ireland**, passported across all 30 EEA states including Germany; authorised services include exchange, execution, reception/transmission of orders, and custody. The **hard MiCA enforcement deadline of 1 July 2026 has just passed** — Kraken is fully authorised with no product wind-downs, while several competitors are not (Binance's application reportedly faced rejection in Greece). For a German retail trader, Kraken is on the safe side of the cliff that just eliminated much of the field. Spot-margin is offered to eligible EEA retail clients.

**Spot fee schedule (Kraken Pro).** Maker/taker by 30-day USD volume: base **0.25%/0.40%** (\<$10k), ≈**0.20%/0.35%** ($10k–$50k), stepping down to 0.00%/0.05%+ at very high tiers; a maker-rebate program (to −0.02%) exists on selected lower-liquidity pairs; stablecoin/FX pairs have a separate cheaper schedule and do **not** count toward the 30-day volume. From **July 9, 2026** fee tiers can also qualify via **Assets on Platform** (real-time USD value of eligible holdings) — at $10k AoP this may pin us at or near the second tier regardless of volume; verify at Phase 0. Untouched (cancelled) orders are free.

**Spot-margin facility.** 200+ margin-enabled pairs; leverage up to 10× on top pairs (BTC, ETH, and a few others), 2–5× on most majors — we will cap far below this. Fees: **opening 0.01–0.05% of the extended amount** + **rollover at the same rate per 4 hours**, dynamic but **locked at order execution** and displayed pre-trade; standard trade fees apply on opening and closing volume (none on *settling* in kind); **3% liquidation fee** on forced liquidations, executed at index price. Shorting draws from the *asset's* margin pool (shorting BTC borrows from the BTC pool), so short capacity depends on pool depth — a structural reason to short only deep-pool majors. Margin allowance limits per verification tier are far above our size. Collateral: 48+ eligible assets (we will hold collateral in EUR/quote currency to avoid collateral-value beta).

**Margin risk mechanics.** Kraken applies a margin-level framework (margin call ≈ 80% margin level, forced liquidation ≈ 40% — reconfirm exact thresholds at Phase 0); positions can be auto-liquidated at index price with the 3% fee. Kraken has venue-idiosyncratic flash-crash history (notably the Feb 2021 ETH cascade far below other venues' prices), which argues for index-aware protective logic rather than naive resting stop-market orders, and for self-imposed margin floors far above Kraken's triggers (§10).

**API.** REST + WebSocket v2. Market data: REST OHLC returns **only the most recent 720 candles per interval** (older data is *not* retrievable via OHLC); the **Trades endpoint pages the complete time-and-sales history from `since=0`**; WS v2 streams trades, L2 book, and OHLC (note: Kraken's WS OHLC has no `is_closed` flag — bars must be buffered until the next interval begins). Trading: limit/market, stop-loss, take-profit, trailing, iceberg, **post-only** (essential for maker-first execution), and margin orders via a leverage parameter; private REST endpoints use a decaying call-counter rate limit by verification tier, and order entry over WS is supported. Exact rate-limit numbers and margin/reduce-only order semantics are Phase 0 verification items.

**Historical data supply (free).** Downloadable ZIPs of **full-history OHLCVT for every pair at 1, 5, 15, 30, 60, 240, 720, 1440-minute intervals**, plus **quarterly incremental ZIPs**; separate downloadable full time-and-sales (tick) CSVs per pair. Two caveats: (a) empty intervals are omitted (no-trade candles must be reconstructed); (b) **the archive covers active/recently-active markets only — delisted pairs drop out**, so a point-in-time universe file must be maintained by us from day one (the survivorship lesson from Phase 1 applies to Kraken too). Mitigations: retain every quarterly ZIP forever (older ZIPs preserve pairs later delisted), and cross-check against Binance Vision.

## 3. Universe: How Many Pairs, and Which<a name="3-universe-how-many-pairs-and-which"></a>

**Deriving the number.** Four forces bound the universe size:

- *Capital granularity.* $10k at ≤1.5× gross across N positions gives per-position sizes of $15,000/N at full deployment. Below ~$700–800 per position, minimum order sizes are still fine on Kraken, but fixed frictions (spread + fee) per rebalance eat a disproportionate share, and the marginal diversification benefit of the 13th–20th crypto (pairwise correlations of majors ≈ 0.6–0.85, higher in crashes) is small.
- *Strategy type.* Time-series (per-asset) trend needs breadth mainly for risk smoothing — 8+ is enough. Any cross-sectional tilt needs enough names to rank — meaningful below ~8 it is not.
- *Overfitting & ops.* Every extra pair multiplies data QA, borrow checks, execution monitoring, and the trial space.
- *Liquidity/borrow floor.* Restrict to Kraken **margin-enabled** pairs with deep books and deep margin pools; thin alts fail both the spread model and the short-capacity requirement.

**Verdict: 10–12 pairs.** Recommended core (all margin/short-enabled on Kraken; leverage caps per pair to be pulled from the `AssetPairs` endpoint's `leverage_buy`/`leverage_sell` fields as ground truth in Phase 1, ⏱):

| Tier | Assets | Quote leg | Role |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Core (deepest books & margin pools) | BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP | EUR | Long/flat/short trend; only tier eligible for shorts by default |
| Satellite majors | ADA, LINK, DOGE, LTC, DOT, AVAX | EUR | Long/flat trend + basket diversification; shorts only if pool depth verified |
| Relative-value legs (optional, Phase 4) | ETH/BTC, SOL/BTC | BTC | Crypto/crypto momentum & regime features; a native way to be "market-neutral-ish" **without paying short carry** |

**Quote-leg policy.** EUR primary: native SEPA rails, no USD-stablecoin FX noise, and post-MiCA stablecoin churn (USDT delisted from EEA order books) makes stablecoin quotes an operational liability; Kraken is the deepest EUR venue globally. Crypto/crypto pairs (ETH/BTC, SOL/BTC) are explicitly *encouraged* where the strategy wants relative exposure — a long ETH/BTC spot position is long-ETH/short-BTC exposure **with zero rollover carry**, which is strictly cheaper than a margin-constructed pair trade. The prior phase's forced-stablecoin-quote constraint is dropped.

**Quote leg, liquidity depth, and the floor — measured (Phase 1, ⏱ 2026-07-07).** The EUR-quote preference above was, until Phase 1, an asserted default. A live measurement (Kraken public `AssetPairs` + `OHLC`, all ten bases across EUR/USD/USDT, 30-day median of `volume × vwap`) grounds and refines it:

- **USD books are genuinely deeper — 2–8.5×.** Combined USD+USDT median quote-volume exceeds EUR for every base (DOGE 8.5×, LINK 7.3×, AVAX 7.3×, ADA 5.8×, XRP 5.8×, BTC 5.0×, SOL 4.5×, ETH 3.9×; LTC 2.0× and DOT 2.4× are closest). USDT is consistently the *thinnest* of the three, so the depth lives in the real-USD (ZUSD) book, not USDT. LINK/EUR (€283k/day), DOGE/EUR (€501k), DOT/EUR (€182k), and AVAX/EUR (€273k) fall below the original €1M EUR floor, but their USD legs clear it easily — their EUR-side thinness is a **quote-book-depth artifact, not asset illiquidity**.
- **EUR gives up no margin capability.** `leverage_buy`/`leverage_sell` are byte-identical on every xxx/EUR vs xxx/USD pair (nine bases 2–10×, DOT 2–5×), same 80%/40% margin-call/stop. USD *could* serve the short/hedge side exactly as EUR — the "EUR is needed to short" intuition is **false**. USDT breaks it (AVAX/USDT not marginable at all; LTC/DOT-USDT capped at 3×) and is MiCA-delisted from EEA books — off the table regardless.
- **Why EUR anyway — friction, not capability.** Kraken has no EUR-settled version of a USD pair, so a EUR-funded account trading USD pays a EUR→USD conversion round-trip, carries continuous EUR/USD risk on idle USD cash (even while flat), owes a per-fill EUR/USD join on every trade for §23-EStG cost basis (USDT is worse — the BMF treats it as a crypto Wirtschaftsgut, so each EUR↔USDT hop is its own taxable lot), and double-counts FX in the cross-margin cushion (EUR collateral vs USD borrow). EUR avoids all four at **zero capability loss**.
- **The depth does not bind at ~$10k.** At ≤1.5× gross over ~10–12 names a full per-name position is ~€1,000–1,400; even as a single order into the *thinnest* EUR book (DOT €182k/day) that is ~0.8% of daily volume, and post-only, split working orders are a small fraction of that. Market impact only bites near ~1% of daily volume — the 2–8.5× USD depth is depth we cannot use at this size.

**Floor decision (Phase 1).** The €1M EUR floor was therefore too strict for our footprint. It is lowered to **€150k/day** — set so a full max-size position (~€1,400) is ≈1% of median daily EUR volume, our actual microstructure-impact threshold — which admits LINK/DOGE/DOT/AVAX and restores the full Core+Satellite **10 EUR names**. The volume floor is also made **quote-currency-aware**: a BTC-quoted RV leg's `volume × vwap` is FX-normalized to EUR via the BTC/EUR reference price rather than compared unit-mismatched against a EUR floor, so ETH/BTC and SOL/BTC are judged on real turnover — both clear it, giving the plan's **12-name** target (10 EUR + 2 BTC RV), `escalate=False`. **Routing thin alts via their deeper USD pairs is deliberately deferred as a pre-registered *scaling trigger*** — revisit when per-name footprint approaches ~1% of a thin EUR book (≈5–10× this account), the only regime where the USD depth advantage becomes usable and worth its FX/tax cost. (Whether *this* EEA account can margin USD/USDT pairs at all remains account-gated — see T0000; this measurement supersedes the earlier unquantified EUR-preference note.)

**Universe governance (autonomous, rule-driven).** The final 10–12 names are selected mechanically in Phase 1 — margin-enabled ∧ per-pair leverage ≥2× (AssetPairs `leverage_buy`/`leverage_sell` as ground truth) ∧ 30-day median daily pair volume above a liquidity floor ∧ captured median spread below a cap, with BTC and ETH mandatory — and escalate to a human decision only if the rule yields \<8 or >15 names or diverges materially from the table above. Alongside it, a point-in-time universe file plus a **symbol & corporate-action ledger** is a Phase 1 artifact and a hard dependency of every backtest. Scoped to this basket, the ledger's *live* content is: Kraken internal symbol aliases (XBT=BTC, XDG=DOGE — they bite in every export and API mapping), redenominations inside basket histories (DOT's 1:100 redenomination of Aug 2020 is *in* the proposed basket's price history), and quote-book migration events (the USDT EEA delisting shifted volume into EUR/USD books — a discontinuity in any liquidity feature). Pure renames on non-members (MATIC→POL) enter the ledger only if the selection rule ever admits the affected asset; the *mechanism* stays, the specific entry is deferred.

## 4. Shorting on Kraken: Mechanics, Costs, and What They Do to the Design Space<a name="4-shorting-on-kraken-mechanics-costs-and-what-they-do-to-the-design-space"></a>

**Mechanics.** A Kraken short is a spot sale of borrowed inventory from Kraken's per-asset margin pool, opened by placing a sell order with a leverage parameter. Exposure is real spot flow; there is no funding rate, but there is the opening fee + 4-hourly rollover described in §2. Positions can be *closed* (opposing order; P&L realized in the quote currency) or *settled* (delivering the asset). Margin is cross against the account's trade balance in eligible collateral.

**The carry table (⏱ rates locked at execution; typical published band 0.01–0.05% per 4h; majors historically at the low end).** Round-trip trade fees assume maker-first at base tier (0.25%×2) — the realistic Phase 6 assumption is one tier better.

| Holding period | Rollover @0.01%/4h | Rollover @0.02%/4h | + open fee | + fees (maker RT) | Total hurdle @0.01% | Total hurdle @0.02% |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 1 day | 0.06% | 0.12% | 0.01–0.02% | 0.50% | ≈0.57% | ≈0.64% |
| 5 days | 0.30% | 0.60% | 0.01–0.02% | 0.50% | ≈0.81% | ≈1.12% |
| 20 days | 1.20% | 2.40% | 0.01–0.02% | 0.50% | ≈1.71% | ≈2.92% |
| 90 days | 5.40% | 10.80% | 0.01–0.02% | 0.50% | ≈5.9% | ≈11.3% |

Annualized, the borrow leg costs **≈22%/yr (0.01%) to ≈44%/yr (0.02%)** before trade fees. Three structural conclusions follow:

1. **Persistent market-neutral long/short books are economically dead at this venue/size.** A dollar-neutral book with a permanently margined short leg pays 22–44%/yr on that leg; no cross-sectional spread found by the prior phases (or plausibly findable now) clears that. Market-neutral designs are therefore demoted to (a) crypto/crypto spot legs (ETH/BTC — zero carry), and (b) *episodic* hedges.
2. **Shorts are tactical, regime-gated instruments.** A bear-regime short held ~5–20 days must clear ≈0.8–3% expected edge; crypto bear legs (−20…−50% over weeks) can clear that, chop cannot. So the short side attaches to the *slow regime/trend layer*, never to fast signals.
3. **Hedging is cheapest via short BTC (or reduced gross), not short alts.** BTC has the deepest margin pool, the lowest expected rollover band, and the least squeeze risk; alt shorts add pool-depth risk for little hedging benefit.

**Short-specific risks and mitigations (full risk model in §10):**

- *Asymmetric payoff / squeezes.* Crypto bear-market rallies of +20–40% in days are routine; shorts get hard stops, small size (≤10% NAV per asset, core tier only), and a squeeze guard (no new shorts after extreme down-moves or when external funding rates are deeply negative — crowded-short signal, free from Coinalyze).
- *Borrow availability / recall.* Pool exhaustion can block opening or (rarely) force closure; mitigation is core-tier-only shorting and treating "borrow unavailable" as a signal to stand down, logged as an execution event.
- *Liquidation cascade + 3% fee.* Self-imposed margin floor (≥250% margin level) and position-level stops sized so the account never approaches Kraken's ~80% call / ~40% liquidation levels (⏱ reconfirm); protective exits are managed by our engine at index-aware prices rather than resting stop-markets (Feb-2021-style venue-local wicks).
- *Weekend/holiday illiquidity.* 24/7 market but thinner books on weekends — the execution scheduler widens maker patience and halves allowed short adds during low-liquidity windows (this is itself a seasonality feature, §6).
- *Tax asymmetry.* Margin-trade P&L is likely taxed under a different regime than spot (§11) — a bookkeeping and net-return consideration, not a blocker.

## 5. Strategy Design Space With Shorting Allowed — Ranked by Expected Value<a name="5-strategy-design-space-with-shorting-allowed-%E2%80%94-ranked-by-expected-value"></a>

EV here ≈ (expected uplift × probability it is real) ÷ (overfitting surface × engineering cost), assessed against the prior phases' negative results (which shrink the prior for anything already refuted at daily frequency) and the cost model above. Every family below gets a pre-registered trial budget and a kill bar in Phase 4; ranks are for *ordering the research queue*, not promises.

### Bucket A — build first (highest EV; extends the proven survivor)<a name="bucket-a-%E2%80%94-build-first-highest-ev-extends-the-proven-survivor"></a>

**A1. Long/flat/short regime overlay on a vol-targeted majors basket.** Direct extension of the only prior survivor (200d-SMA gate, OOS Sharpe 0.31–0.50 long/flat). New work: (i) add a *short state* — e.g., enter short at confirmed bear (price < slow SMA − band, negative multi-horizon momentum), at reduced size (≤0.5× the long-state exposure) with the carry hurdle test applied at entry; (ii) replace binary gating on BTC-only with a small ensemble (BTC regime + per-asset trend agreement); (iii) vol-target the whole book to 10–12% annualized. Literature support for symmetric crypto trend is solid (net Sharpe ≈1.0–1.6 reported for majors trend; long-only crypto TSMOM concentrated its Sharpe in bull regimes — exactly the gap shorting fills). Prior-phase evidence says the binary all-or-nothing gate was load-bearing — the short extension must not soften it into a whipsaw machine; the kill bar tests exactly that.

**A2. Per-asset symmetric TSMOM ensemble with vol sizing (4h–1d).** Donchian/multi-lookback ensemble per asset, long/flat/short, inverse-vol aggregated, on the 10–12 universe. This is the strongest published crypto result family (e.g., Zarattini–Pagani–Barbon's Donchian ensemble with vol-based sizing on liquid coins, reported Sharpe >1.5 net with ~19% maxDD over 2015–2025 — treat as an optimistic upper bound and haircut for our fees/universe). Overlaps A1 heavily; A1 vs A2 is an A/B inside one family, not two families.

### Bucket B — genuine new ground (medium EV; strict rigor)<a name="bucket-b-%E2%80%94-genuine-new-ground-medium-ev-strict-rigor"></a>

**B1. Intraday trend + time-of-day/day-of-week seasonality (1h/4h core).** The genuinely new frequency band. Documented effects: session-boundary momentum (Asia open / US open), weekend↔weekday transition effects, and intraday realized-vol clustering; published intraday BTC trend benchmarks report gross Sharpe ≈1.6 vs ≈0.8 for vol-targeted long-only — but *gross*: at our base fee tier the maker round trip (0.50%) plus spread roughly halves it, so the family lives or dies on maker-fill realism and turnover control. Design: seasonality as *conditioning features* on the A1/A2 signal (trade only at favorable windows; scale by intraday vol state) rather than as a standalone high-turnover system.

**B2. Derivatives-positioning features at intraday horizons (funding, OI, liquidations — free external data).** Prior phase found daily funding-carry redundant with regime timing; *untested* is the intraday version: funding extremes and OI/liquidation spikes as short-horizon de-risking / re-entry triggers for the spot book (e.g., stand down shorts when funding is deeply negative; fade post-liquidation-cascade dislocations at 1h). Data is free (Coinalyze API: OI/funding/liquidation history; Binance public data as backup). Modest prior, cheap to test.

**B3. Cross-sectional momentum tilt, long-only, with BTC-short overlay.** The prior phases refuted ML cross-sectional selection *and* the L/S quantile spread OOS. ## 7. Framework Decision — RATIFIED 2026-07-06: Option 4 (hybrid)<a name="7-framework-decision-%E2%80%94-ratified-2026-07-06-option-4-hybrid"></a>

**The decision criterion, restated from experience:** the prior pain was *validation rigor* (purged/CPCV, deflated Sharpe, bootstrap CIs, leak-free labeling, trial-register integrity) — not backtest plumbing. The framework choice must therefore be judged on: (1) whether the validation layer can be first-class, auditable, and unit-tested; (2) realistic modeling of Kraken's actual costs (maker/taker tiers, spread, margin open+rollover, borrow availability); (3) intraday support; (4) a credible research→paper→live bridge on Kraken specifically; (5) maintenance health; (6) Claude Code developer ergonomics. Paid frameworks (vectorbtpro etc.) are excluded by constraint.

**Option 1 — Forked/pinned Qlib.** Not recommended. Established as unmaintained; daily-equity-centric design fights all three new requirements (intraday cadence, margin/short cost modeling, Kraken data plumbing); and its backtest/validation internals are exactly the opaque surface the trial-register incident taught us to own ourselves. Salvage value: its factor-zoo ideas (Alpha158-style feature construction) inform the Phase 4 feature menu — as *ideas*, not code.

**Option 2 — NautilusTrader as the whole stack.** Stronger than at last review: it now ships an **official Kraken adapter** (Rust with Python bindings; live market data + order execution for Kraken Spot; WS bar streaming with correct bar-close buffering; Kraken is a listed API partner of the project), is very actively maintained, models margin accounts/fees/order-book fills, and offers backtest↔live parity. Weaknesses for *us*: the research loop (thousands of CPCV folds over feature sweeps) is not its shape — event-driven replay is orders of magnitude slower than vectorized scans and would put our validation math inside someone else's abstractions again; and the Kraken adapter is new in 2026 (execution paths refined as recently as v1.226), with **spot-margin order semantics (leverage parameter, shorts) to be verified hands-on** — flagged as a Phase 0 verification item and a Phase 6 entry criterion.

**Option 3 — Lean custom everything, including the live engine.** Maximum auditability; the live engine for a 4h-cadence, 12-instrument book is genuinely small (WS consumer + order state machine + REST/WS order entry via a maintained SDK such as python-kraken-sdk or ccxt). Weakness: we re-implement execution details (reconnects, partial fills, rate-limit budgeting, reconciliation) that NautilusTrader has already hardened, and paper-trading realism is on us.

**Recommendation (Option 4 = 3 + 2 hybrid; my recommendation, yours to ratify):**

1. **Research & validation core: custom, first-class deliverable.** polars/pandas + numpy; a purpose-built validation library (purge/embargo CPCV, deflated Sharpe with pre-registered trial budgets, stationary-bootstrap CIs, PBO, SPA/reality-check vs the benchmark family) with **integrity by construction** (asserts: DSR never NaN, monotonic trial counts, immutable append-only registry, loud failure on corruption — the momentum_tilt lesson encoded as unit tests); a small vectorized backtester with an **explicit Kraken cost model** (fee-tier ladder incl. the July 2026 AoP rule, per-pair spread model estimated from our own L2 capture, margin open+rollover accrued per 4h bucket, borrow-availability switch). Target ≈2–4k LOC, fully unit-tested — this codebase *is* the product of Phases 2–5.
2. **Execution & paper trading: NautilusTrader with the official Kraken adapter**, adopted at Phase 6 *if* the Phase 0 verification (margin orders, shorts, post-only, reconciliation) passes; fallback is the thin custom engine (Option 3's live layer), which the 4h cadence makes viable. Either way, the strategy logic is a pure function shared by backtest and live (signal in → target book out), so the engine is swappable.

This resolves the real pain point (validation owned, auditable, tested), keeps every dollar in open source, matches the hardware, and leaves the heaviest engineering (exchange adapter hardening) to a well-maintained project we can still bypass.

**Decision status: RATIFIED 2026-07-06 — Option 4 (hybrid).** Fallback remains pre-approved without re-ratification: if the Phase 0 adapter verification fails on spot-margin/short order semantics, the live layer drops to the thin custom engine while the research/validation core is unaffected. (Housekeeping: any residual "based on Microsoft Qlib" phrasing in working prompts/role templates is legacy boilerplate from the earlier cycle — Qlib is not part of the ratified stack and templates should be updated to avoid steering future autonomous sessions toward it.)

## 9. Validation Methodology (the harness is the product)<a name="9-validation-methodology-the-harness-is-the-product"></a>

Carried forward and hardened. The harness ships in Phase 2 *before any strategy code*, and must itself pass acceptance tests on synthetic data (a planted signal must be recovered; a null strategy must fail; a look-ahead bug deliberately injected must be caught by the leakage tests).

**Core protocol per trial:**

1. **Leak-free labeling.** Forward returns with embargo ≥ label horizon + feature lookback overlap; features constructed strictly from information available at decision time (external data lagged by its real publication latency, captured point-in-time where possible).
2. **CPCV** (combinatorial purged cross-validation, e.g., N=10 groups choose 2 test → 45 paths) with purge+embargo; report the *distribution* of path Sharpes, not the mean alone.
3. **Walk-forward stress**: expanding-window annual OOS folds (2019/20→22, →23, →24, →25, →H1-26), the design that caught the 2025 inversion last time. Regime slices reported per fold (bull/bear/chop by the regime layer's own definition — including the 2024 top, the 2025–26 bear, and any 2026 recovery).
4. **Multiple-testing control:** pre-registered trial budget per family; **deflated Sharpe ratio** against the family's full trial count; **PBO**; and a **SPA/White reality check against the benchmark family** (§Phase 3), which is the deployment-relevant test: "is anything here better than disciplined beta-timing?"
5. **Uncertainty:** stationary block bootstrap CIs on Sharpe, maxDD, and the *delta vs benchmark*; multi-seed distributions for any stochastic learner.
6. **Cost stress:** every headline result re-run at 1.5× and 2× the fitted cost model (fees, spread, rollover), and with taker-only fills; a strategy that dies at 1.5× costs is not deployable.
7. **Registry integrity by construction:** append-only trial registry (one JSON record per trial: spec hash, dataset hash, seed, full metrics, verdict); asserts that DSR is finite, trial counts monotone, and registry hash-chain intact; CI test that corrupts a registry copy and requires a loud failure. A budgeted-holdout ledger tracks every "look" at the final holdout.

**Benchmark family (the bar to beat, fixed in Phase 3 before any alpha work):** B0 buy-and-hold BTC; B1 vol-targeted BTC (10–12%); B2 vol-targeted inverse-vol majors basket; B3 = B2 + 200d long/flat gate (the prior survivor); B4 = B3 + the simplest short extension. Deployment rule: **the deployed system is the best member of {benchmarks ∪ validated survivors} under the full protocol — and B3/B4 winning is a success, not a failure.** **Frozen benchmark (the kill bar's SPA reference): B3+vt-dynamic — adopted by the human 2026-07-09 (T0009 item 1), superseding gated-B1 (frozen at the Phase-3 close).** Construction, exactly: `dynamic_inverse_vol_basket(lookback=30)` over the 10-major union calendar (no BTC forward-fill); 200-day `sma_gate` on the basket's **own equity index** (cumulative product of (1 + its returns), seeded 1.0; warm-up flat); `vol_target` 10 %/yr (30d, max 1.0×) **sized on the raw basket, gate applied after**. Reference figures: zero-fee Sharpe 1.419 / maxDD 21.9 %; net-of-cost (per-asset turnover × 1.5× Tier-1 maker) 1.245 full / 1.278 post-warm-up (k ≥ 230). Evidence: `docs/research/04.phase3-benchmark-b2-dynamic-report.md` §"B3 and B4"; the Sharpe edge over gated-B1 is a point estimate (n.s., p ≈ 0.27) accepted with the higher drawdown — the human's call.

## 10. Low-Risk Margin/Short Risk Model (constants RATIFIED 2026-07-06 — D1)<a name="10-low-risk-marginshort-risk-model-constants-ratified-2026-07-06-%E2%80%94-d1"></a>

**Portfolio limits (hard, enforced in code pre-trade and by a runtime governor):**

| Limit | Value | Rationale |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Annualized portfolio vol target | 10–12% (est. on 1h RV, EWMA) | "Matched low risk"; implies typical net exposure ≈0.2–0.5× in crypto-vol regimes |
| Gross leverage | ≤1.5× soft / 2.0× hard | Low-leverage constraint; leaves margin headroom |
| Net exposure | −0.5× … +1.0× | Shorts are a tactical tail, never a mirror-image book |
| Per-asset | ≤20% NAV long; ≤10% NAV short (core tier only) | Idiosyncratic + squeeze risk |
| Self-imposed margin level floor | ≥250% (alert 300%) | Never approach Kraken's ~80% call / ~40% liquidation (⏱); avoids the 3% liquidation fee and index-wick risk |
| Collateral policy | Quote-currency (EUR/USD) collateral for margin | Avoids collateral-value beta doubling down in crashes |

**Drawdown governance:** daily loss ≥3% → flatten new risk, halve targets, human review flag; trailing maxDD budget 15% from HWM with a deleverage ladder (−7.5%: risk ×0.5; −11%: risk ×0.25; −15%: flat + post-mortem before restart). These thresholds are themselves backtested (they are part of the system, not an afterthought).

**Short-side rules:** entry only in confirmed bear state (regime layer) *and* expected-edge > carry-hurdle (per §4 table, at the executed rollover rate); hard stop per short (structure-based, index-aware, engine-managed); time-stop (if a short hasn't worked within its design horizon, carry is bleeding — cover); squeeze guard (no adds after >X% down-day; stand down when external funding < deep-negative threshold); borrow-unavailable → stand down + log. Rollover accrual is *live-tracked per position* and reconciled against Kraken's ledger entries (the ledger export exposes rollover fee records — an accounting cross-check built in Phase 6).

**Operational risk:** heartbeat/watchdog (stale WS ⇒ cancel-all resting orders and freeze), reconciliation loop (engine state vs Kraken open orders/positions every cycle), idempotent order ops with client order IDs, kill-switch file + phone-reachable manual halt, secrets in OS keyring / systemd credentials, API keys least-privilege (trade-enabled key only at Phase 6, only on the hardened D2 VPS; **no withdrawal permission ever**), single-host reality (live engine on the 24/7 VPS at Phase 6; workstation is research-only) ⇒ crash-safe order-state journal, a "reduce-only on restart" policy, and provider-outage handling (Linode incident ⇒ watchdog-triggered flatten-or-freeze runbook).

## 11. German Tax & Compliance — the Blockpit Pipeline (snapshot ⏱ — not tax or legal advice)<a name="11-german-tax--compliance-%E2%80%94-the-blockpit-pipeline-snapshot-%E2%8F%B1-%E2%80%94-not-tax-or-legal-advice"></a>

**Operating principle (ratified 2026-07-06):** the established, working division of labor is kept: **Blockpit is the calculation engine of record** for transaction-level crypto tax computation, and the Steuerberater reviews and files the resulting report. Transaction-level classification across thousands of automated trades is precisely what a specialized tool is built and maintained for, so the project does not build its own tax logic and does not gate on obtaining a bespoke classification ruling. Instead we (a) produce clean, complete, archivable inputs for Blockpit, (b) verify *empirically* how Blockpit classifies Kraken **spot-margin** activity, and (c) keep the deployment economics robust to either possible tax bucket. The separate tax-consultation blocking item from the earlier draft is folded into this pipeline.

**Legal background (scenario bounds only — not decisions to be made):** spot → §23 EStG (1-year period, €1,000 Freigrenze, personal rate 14–45%, per-wallet FIFO, documentation duties per the BMF letter of 6 March 2025); margin/derivatives settled by cash difference → commonly §20(2) Termingeschäft (26.375% incl. Soli, Anlage KAP; the €20k loss-offset cap was repealed retroactively by JStG 2024); DAC8 CASP reporting live since 1 Jan 2026 (Kraken reports automatically — assume full transparency). Gewerblichkeit remains a distant tail risk for a self-directed retail bot; it is a *watch item*, not an action item.

**The one genuine verification gap (⏱, found 2026-07-06):** Blockpit's published import scope for the **Kraken (spot exchange)** integration lists fiat/crypto, deposits & withdrawals, fee payments, converts, spot market trades, derivatives (tokenized assets), and earn rewards — but does **not explicitly list spot-margin trades or margin PnL**, whereas Blockpit's changelogs for several other exchange integrations list "Margin Trades" and "PnL (Profit, Loss, Fee and Funding)" explicitly, and Kraken *Futures* has its own separate integration with explicit futures-PnL scope. Kraken spot-margin shorts are, ledger-wise, sales of borrowed inventory plus per-position rollover-fee ledger rows; a naive spot-only interpretation could corrupt FIFO lots (phantom disposals/negative balances) or drop rollover costs. Whether Blockpit reconstructs this correctly is an **empirical question**, and it decides which bucket (§20-style derivatives PnL vs §23 disposals) the report of record will show.

**Actionable plan (autonomous except the single marked step):**

- **T1 — Doc & depot check (Phase 1, autonomous).** Re-verify Blockpit's current Kraken import scope from the help center; connect the existing Kraken account (read-only API key) to a Blockpit depot (import/testing is free per Blockpit) and inspect how any *historical* margin entries were ingested and labeled. Output: T2 verdict in the decision log.
- **T3 — Fallback (autonomous; built only if T2 fails).** A deterministic, unit-tested pre-transform mapping Kraken's ledger + trades exports (margin flags and rollover ledger rows are linkable per position via ledger IDs) into Blockpit's manual-import CSV with explicit margin-PnL rows. ### Phase 6 — Execution Engine, Paper Trading & Go-Live (interactive-heavy; ~4–8 weeks)<a name="phase-6-%E2%80%94-execution-engine-paper-trading--go-live-interactive-heavy-4%E2%80%938-weeks"></a>

**Goal:** the running application: monitors the market, places orders, survives operations, matches its simulation.
**Interactive kickoff (blocking):** engine choice finalized (Nautilus adapter verified for margin/short/post-only, else custom fallback) **and deployment host confirmed — default: the D2 VPS, since it is the only 24/7 host, with the §8 hardening applied before the trade key lands on it**; trade-enabled API key activated with least privilege (D3(ii)); tiny-live sleeve trade plan includes the §11 **T2 tax-probe set** (margin long + margin short across ≥2 rollovers; one close, one settle — D3(iii)); go-live criteria pre-registered (below).
**Stage 6a — Shadow (2–3 weeks):** engine runs live data, computes targets, *logs intended orders only*; concordance check sim-vs-intended (signal parity backtest↔live is a hard gate). Kraken has no spot testnet — shadow mode plus tiny-live replaces it.
**Stage 6b — Paper-with-teeth / tiny live (three rungs; amended 2026-08-02, see the phase-6 decisions log):** the original single-step form — €500–1,000 at full logic, quarter size, against a 10 bps weekly band — was measured against the real 6a journal and **cannot execute**: 0 of 801 intended orders clear Kraken's `ordermin` quantity floors at €500 quarter-size, and the band it is judged against is 5–12× below the floor those same minimums impose. 6b therefore runs as three rungs, each proving something the next depends on:
**— Rung 1 (plumbing, ~€10–30):** the §11 **T2 tax-probe set** IS this rung — ≥1 margin long + ≥1 margin short across ≥2 rollovers, one closed one settled. Proves the order path, fills, fees, rollover ledger rows, reconciliation and the tax pipeline in one pass, and supplies the first measured fills for the cost-basis re-pricing — the execution style itself is ruled (maker-first with a taker fallback); what rung 1 measures is the realized blend. Exit: every artifact present and reconciled; T2 verdict recorded (or the T3 fallback built).
**— Rung 2 (operational confidence; fewer assets, lower budget, time-boxed — the box fixed at entry):** exercises the full daily loop. Requires a **skip-or-carry policy fixed at entry** — what happens to a delta that cannot be placed. **Its tracking error is NOT a go/no-go input**: concentration fixes *entry* placeability, not *delta* placeability, so the number measures something adjacent to the deployable. Exit: the time-box expires and its report is read.
**— Rung 3 (the deployable; full weights at €1,000) → the go/no-go.** Orders are computed as `delta = target − actually held` (accumulation): an unplaceable delta persists as a growing gap that places itself once it crosses `ordermin`. **Preconditions:** rungs 1–2 complete; §10's whole-book limits wired; sleeve-occupancy observable (below); the venue-minimums snapshot re-confirmed.
**Go/no-go gate (pre-registered, human decision; band and baseline re-derived 2026-08-02):** **the weekly tracking-error report** — realized-vs-simulated sleeve return with its fills, fees, rollover accrual and slippage against the cost model, the same report that feeds cost-model recalibration from real fills — lands **within the measured band for ≥3 consecutive complete ISO weeks** (a partial week is reported but never counted toward the three). **The edge is the p95 of the per-cycle drift floor at the funded NAV** (`zcrypto engine accum-replay`), against that week's **mean** drift — not the original 10 bps, which presupposes a book an order of magnitude larger than rung 3's, and not the floor's median, which flawless execution fails roughly half the time by construction. The p95 is the honest line because the floor is what the *venue* imposes: a week landing under it says our execution added nothing on top, which is precisely what this stage exists to prove. Also: zero unreconciled order/position states; ops drills passed (kill-switch, WS-loss, restart-reduce-only); and performance **consistent with the model** — judged against the expectation **conditional on the book's realized composition, quoted as a band, never as a point and never against the headline record figure**.

**That band carries TWO independent uncertainties, and they are stated as separate terms rather than merged into one interval** *(amended 2026-08-03; the collision is recorded in the phase-6 decisions log)*: (a) the **statistical** confidence interval of the conditional estimate, and (b) the **cost basis** — which execution style the book actually achieves, since the realized maker/taker blend moves the conditional expectation by an amount comparable to (a). Merging them yields a single interval that *looks* like it covers estimation uncertainty while being silently consumed by the cost assumption, and a gate reading it would then have no room left for the uncertainty it was written for. The two also behave differently and must not be treated alike: **(b) resolves to a point once rung 1 measures the realized blend; (a) never resolves.**

**Divergence beyond that band is diagnosed before it is acted on, because the two terms fail differently.** Divergence attributable to (a) — the conditional expectation or its conditioning being wrong — **means the backtest is wrong ⇒ return to Phase 5**, not a 6b re-run. Divergence attributable to (b) — execution landing at a worse blend than assumed — is an **execution finding, not a model finding**: it re-prices the basis and may re-open the execution-style decision, but it does not by itself invalidate the backtest. Attributing a cost-basis miss to the model would send a sound system back a phase; attributing a model miss to execution would ramp capital onto a broken one. Re-derive the band before the gate reads it whenever the target series moves — including with no execution change at all, if the drawdown governor lifts or a dormant sleeve re-arms — **whenever the realized maker/taker blend moves**, and **at every ramp step, since the floor is a function of the funded NAV and collapses as it grows**; a stale band is too *wide* and would pass execution it should fail.

**What this gate certifies, and what it does not** *(ruled 2026-08-03; the gate's scope is narrowed to match its evidence rather than left to be inferred)*. Passing it certifies **execution**: that the live book reproduces the intended one, that orders place and reconcile, that costs and drift sit inside the measured band, and that the ops paths hold. It does **not** certify **edge**. The deployable carries **no out-of-time validation of its returns** — the one budgeted holdout look was spent on a superseded construction in a window where nothing traded, and the budget is now 0. The shadow-soak conformance read is explicitly not a substitute: it measures whether the live book *looks like* the backtest book, and an overfit strategy sits in-band most of the time at these window lengths. **So go-live rests on in-sample metrics plus proven execution, and that is the whole of it.**

**Two limitations are inherited by this decision and must be accepted knowingly rather than by nobody noticing.** First, the absence of out-of-time evidence above. Second, the deployable's **selection counterfactual sits outside the gate**: its verdict was ADOPT against an incumbent whose construction was never committed, and when this was ruled (2026-08-03) that comparison was unrebuildable at any cost basis. The incumbent's runners were later recovered and verified (2026-08-21, \[[T0148]\]), so the counterfactual is examinable again — but the gate was deliberately re-grounded to not depend on it (\[[T0125]\]) and stays that way; any re-read is decision-support, never a gate input. The first limitation is not obtainable at all (no available window is long enough to discriminate; see the phase-6 decisions log).

**The gate does not rest on that counterfactual, and this is a deliberate re-grounding** *(2026-08-03)*. The deployable's validation basis is its **benchmark-relative** kill bar, which is reproducible from committed code and has been **re-derived end to end rather than read from the registry**: the Sharpe-primary comparison against the frozen benchmark, the SPA grid across both block lengths and all three seeds, the deflated Sharpe with its trial variance, and the benchmark-relative worst-slice test. The cost-stress rungs and the drawdown figures are **not** part of that re-derivation and remain covered by the frozen-figure regression at its own tolerance — a distinction the committed re-derivation states about itself, and one this gate must not blur. What the missing incumbent removes is the answer to *why this candidate rather than that one*; what it does not touch is *whether this candidate clears the bar* — and the second is what the gate reads. That is a narrowing, not an equivalence: benchmark-clearing is **necessary, not sufficient**, and the only selection-bias correction inside the retained basis is the deflated Sharpe at its registered trial count, which deflates only slightly. The gate should be read accordingly. Anyone re-examining the deployable runs the committed re-derivation; nobody needs the vanished comparison to do it. They are stated here because the gate is where they bind, and a criteria-met reading that did not mention them would overclaim.

**Composition disclosure (stated knowingly, not inherited):** the book that rung 3 takes live may realize fewer active sleeves than the registered record combines — the fixed ⅓ weights then divide a smaller book, and gross scales with the count. That is a normal state of the validated system, not a defect, but the go/no-go reads the conditional expectation for the state actually running, and **sleeve occupancy must be observable before rung 3 opens** so a change is announced rather than discovered.
**Ramp:** 25% → 50% → 100% of the $10k on a calendar+criteria schedule (each step requires: no gate breaches, DD ladder untouched or correctly handled; and before exceeding 25%, the §11 tax-pipeline verification must be closed — T2 pass or T3 fallback deployed).
**Artifacts:** runbook, ops drill log, concordance reports, go-live decision record.

### Ongoing — Operate, Monitor, Revalidate<a name="ongoing-%E2%80%94-operate-monitor-revalidate"></a>

**Sleeve promotion — how an adopted family enters the live book (ruled 2026-08-29; see the phase-6 decisions log).** Research continues after go-live under the same registry, so an ADOPT verdict eventually supersedes the deployable. The path is scoped by **what the change touches**, not by a fixed duration, because the four gates that made the deployable trustworthy each proved something different.

*Always, for any promotion:* a new registry record via Phase-5 assembly; a builder-contract version bump (an unchanged contract cannot rebuild a changed book); and **shadow concordance re-earned on the new contract** — a new contract is new code on the very path Stage 6a exists to prove, so parity is unproven by construction. No class is exempt.

*Parity is re-earned by REPLAY, not by waiting.* Stage identity is exact float equality, so a contract either reproduces or it does not — there is no sample size. The new contract replays against the existing journal, every cycle since go-live, requiring zero stage-identity breaks. That is stronger than a fresh live window, which covers only whatever the market happened to do.

*Scoped re-entry beyond parity:* **class A** (weights only, same legs, same code path) — none; **class B** (new or changed legs) — rung 1, because the order path, fees, rollover and tax rows are unproven for an instrument never traded; **class C** (changed execution semantics: sides, order types, cadence, skip-or-carry) — rungs 1 and 2. **The class is declared and ratified BEFORE the evidence is gathered**, as a trial is pre-registered; declared afterwards it becomes an argument for the least work.

*The band is statistical and keeps its bar:* **≥3 consecutive complete ISO weeks**, every class. Its expectation is conditional on the book's realized composition, and composition is exactly what a promotion changes, so prior band evidence does not carry.

*Capital:* the new record runs in **shadow alongside the live record**, which keeps trading; cut over when the band passes. Stepping the live book down instead would measure the band at a NAV the book will not run at — the edge is the p95 of the drift floor **at the funded NAV** — so the evidence would not transfer. Capital never steps up while a re-gate is open. **Precondition:** this requires the engine to run two builder contracts at once, which it cannot today; the contract is wired in, not selected.

Monthly: revalidation on rolling data (registered as trials), fee-tier and ⏱ snapshot recheck, tax ledger export. Quarterly: OHLCVT increment ingestion, universe review, decay tests (CUSUM on live-vs-expected P&L). Pre-registered *production kill criteria* (e.g., DD ladder floor, N-month underperformance beyond CI vs its own simulation) so the retire-decision is mechanical, not emotional. New research = new Phase 4 sprints under the same registry.

**Realistic total:** ~3.5–5 months from ratification to full capital, dominated by Phase 4 breadth and Phase 6 patience — both deliberately budget-boxed rather than open-ended.

## 13. Honest Caveats (read twice)<a name="13-honest-caveats-read-twice"></a>

1. **The base rate says B4 wins.** Two disciplined phases found beta-timing and nothing else; the loosened constraints (shorts, intraday) add real new surface, but fees and carry consume most of it at $10k scale. The plan treats "deploy B4" as success — hold yourself to that framing when a shiny Phase 4 candidate is 0.1 Sharpe above the bar.
2. **Bear-heavy sample.** Any system validated on data ending mid-2026 has learned a lot of bear; a violent regime change (2023-style recovery) is the likeliest failure mode for the short extension. The regime slices and the walk-forward worst-fold criterion are the defense — respect them.
3. **Kraken-specific tails:** venue-local wicks (2021 precedent), margin-pool exhaustion in stress exactly when shorts matter, dynamic rollover repricing in volatility, and fee-schedule changes (one just landed July 9-adjacent) — all ⏱, all in the snapshot register.
4. **Costs are the model.** Half of the plan's honesty lives in the cost model; if captured spreads or live fills disagree with it, every upstream verdict is suspect — that's why concordance gates are hard gates.
5. **Own-process survivorship.** The registry guards against classic trial-hacking, but design iteration *between* phases is itself a selection process; the single pre-registered holdout look and the SPA-vs-benchmark test are the last line.
6. **Tax drag is real.** Short-term §23 gains at a high marginal rate (or §20 at 26.375%) materially change net compounding; the go/no-go economics are therefore computed under *both* scenarios (§11) and must hold under the worse one, with Blockpit's empirically verified classification becoming the reporting treatment of record.
7. **Key-person ops.** One VPS, one workstation, one human. The runbook, watchdogs, hash-verified data handoff, and reduce-only-on-restart policy are what stand between a WS disconnect (or a Linode incident) at 03:00 and an unmanaged margin position.
