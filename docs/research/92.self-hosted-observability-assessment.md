# 92. Self-hosting the observability stack — Grafana Cloud's free tier assessed against one self-hosted node

**Question put (2026-10-01).** The fleet's metrics, logs and alert rules run on Grafana Cloud's free tier, which has no ticket support and whose faults reach the pager. Should the project self-host them on one additional 4 GB node, Docker-free, and with which stack?

**Status:** an assessment at one date, not a design. `docs/open-topics/T0217-self-hosted-observability-stack.md` carries the work it recommends, and the spec that follows re-derives its figures. **Re-evaluate it when the fleet outgrows its premises**: a second Grafana or monitoring node, retention moved to object storage, `database is locked` in Grafana's journal, or series and log volume an order of magnitude above the figures in §2 and §3.

**How it was produced.** Five inventories of the repo's Grafana Cloud footprint, one web fact sheet on the candidate stacks, three independent designs each scored by two judges, and this synthesis. Paths are relative to the repo root at `develop` @ 141691990, and line numbers are that commit's. "Measured" means one of the inventory or design runs reported it; only the repo lines marked "re-read today" were read again for the synthesis. The inventories and the fact sheet are not kept in the tree.

## 1. Should the project self-host, and with which stack

**Yes, self-host, on Grafana OSS (SQLite) + one Prometheus as remote_write receiver + single-binary Loki, on one Docker-free 4 GB Linode behind Caddy.** It is not a fix for the rung-2 box: Grafana Cloud stays the evaluator through Sun 2026-11-01, and the cutover runs from Mon 2026-11-02.

- **This stack rewrites no alert expression.** Grafana stays the evaluator, Prometheus answers the 93 PromQL rules, and real Loki answers the 13 LogQL rules. The rule file, Slack template, five dashboards and the rule tests keep their shape.
- **The unchanged push script already works against Grafana OSS.** The victoria design ran `infra/scripts/grafana-push.sh` against a local OSS 13.2.3: 5 dashboards, the template, both receivers and 106 rules loaded, at a 60 s group interval, with anonymous `/api/health` answering 200.
- **It removes September's failure class.** The 09-28 to 09-30 evaluation errors were the evaluator timing out against a remote Prometheus (179 transitions across 52 rules on 09-30, ops journal 2026-09, on the `ops-journal` branch). On one node that call is loopback. Hibernation and the 14-day retention also go.
- **VictoriaLogs is rejected.** It is not an apt package anywhere, and it forces a LogsQL rewrite of all 13 log rules (8 critical dead-men), the logs board, `grafana-query.py --loki`, `ops_daily.read_logs` and their tests.
- **VictoriaMetrics is rejected.** It changes results for `increase`/`delta`/`changes` at a series' first sample and after a gap (measured on Debian's 1.112.0). That adds a false-page path on critical rules such as `zcrypto-gate-mismatch` (`infra/grafana/alerts.yaml:79`), for a footprint saving this fleet does not need at 24 samples/s.
- **The hybrid watch node is rejected as an end state.** It leaves 96 rules, all dashboards and all logs on the unsupported tier, and its in-box value rests on two untested premises (Cloud read tokens; Cloud stores answering during hibernation).

The judges scored the three designs 7 and 7 (Grafana + Prometheus + Loki), 5.5 and 6 (Victoria), 6 and 7 (hybrid). The recommendation below is the first design with its judges' required changes folded in.

The alternative to weigh is Grafana Cloud Pro at $19/month with 8x5 email support (https://grafana.com/pricing/). Nothing read establishes that it changes the evaluator, hibernation or `/api/health` behaviour.

## 2. Corrections to the framing, and the three remarks

### Corrections

| Framing | What the facts say |
| -- | -- |
| "Grafana Enterprise + PostgreSQL" versus "VictoriaMetrics/VictoriaLogs" | These are not alternatives. Grafana stores no telemetry; 93 rules need a PromQL store and 13 need a LogQL store behind it. The real choice is the stores. |
| The Victoria stack as native apt packages | VictoriaLogs has no package in Debian or any vendor repo (tarball only). Debian's `victoria-metrics 1.112.0` is 41 minor versions behind upstream. |
| Self-hosting protects the box | Nothing reviewed can land before Mon 10-05 (prerequisites are due Sun 10-04 20:00Z, `14.phase6-decisions.md`). `zcrypto-engine-log-dead` reads the engine's own log push, which moves only with an engine restart (`cli/logging/ship.py:130`). |
| Free tier quota pressure | The 1k-series budget is self-imposed. Live is 1,413 against Cloud's 10k limit, and logs are about 0.1 GB/month against 50 GB. Quota is not a reason to move. |
| "No SLA" | A 99.5% SLA page exists, but its remedy is credits against fees, so a free stack gets nothing. Support is community-only, as the owner found. |
| A local stack ends error pages | Not by itself. 104 rules carry `execErrState: Alerting` and 22 have `for: 0s`, so a local store restart reproduces the 09-29 shape unless the role is designed for it (section 5). |

### Remark 1: OSS or unlicensed Enterprise — OSS

- **What Enterprise adds for this fleet: nothing.** Every Enterprise feature is licence-gated: custom RBAC roles, datasource permissions, reporting, query caching, auditing, Enterprise plugins (https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/introduction/grafana-enterprise/). The fleet uses dashboards, Grafana-managed alerting, the provisioning API and service-account tokens, all in OSS.
- **Its one gain** is activating a licence later without swapping packages. A one-operator fleet with two Slack receivers has no use for that.
- **What it risks.** It is a custom-licence build (the apt index lists `grafana` as AGPLv3 and `grafana-enterprise` as "custom") on the host holding the Slack webhook and ingest credentials. No document states it behaves identically to OSS when unlicensed.
- **No patch-cadence cost.** Both packages ship from the same repo at the same version, 13.2.3.
- **What was not read:** neither package's licence text.

### Remark 2: SQLite or PostgreSQL — SQLite

- **The database holds only Grafana's own state**: 5 dashboards, about 110 rules and their instance state, 2 contact points, silences, one service account. The victoria run measured `grafana.db` at 2.8 MB after a full push.
- **The documented concern is write-heavy alert state at scale** (https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/alerting/set-up/performance-limitations/). Here it is about 110 rules on a 60 s tick. Sending state history to Loki instead of the default annotations backend removes more SQL writes.
- **Facts that would force PostgreSQL:**
  - a second Grafana instance, since HA needs a shared database; or
  - `database is locked` lines in `journalctl -u grafana-server`, or missed evaluations traced to them.
- **The second trigger is made observable** by a LogQL rule on Grafana's own journal for that string.
- PostgreSQL would also be the first Postgres role in the repo.

### Remark 3: Prometheus or Mimir monolithic — Prometheus

|  | Single Prometheus | Mimir monolithic |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Packaging | `prometheus 2.53.3+ds1-2` in Debian main, covered by Debian security | `mimir 3.2.1` on apt.grafana.com only, patched by hand |
| Memory | no vendor figure at this scale | no vendor figure; runs distributor, ingester, querier, store-gateway and compactor in one process |
| Storage | one local TSDB directory | built around object storage; the only single-process example is marked "Do not use this configuration in production" (https://grafana.com/docs/mimir/latest/get-started/); 3.x prefers Kafka-based ingest |
| Operational surface | one flag, `--web.enable-remote-write-receiver`, stable since 2.33 | tenants, per-tenant limits, hash rings, compactor and store-gateway tuning |

- **What Mimir would buy:** horizontal scale, replication, multi-tenancy, object-store retention. None applies at 1,413 series on one no-HA node.
- **Facts that would flip it:** a second node, or retention moved to object storage.
- **Not confirmed:** whether Mimir 3.x monolithic runs without Kafka by default.

## 3. Recommended design

Host `zcrypto-mon` (name proposed): Linode `g6-standard-2`, 4 GB, 2 vCPU, 80 GB, $24/month, Debian 13, in a region apart from the engine host's. The play is the bridgehead's shape (`infra/ansible/site.yml:248-271`) with a new `mon` role.

| Component | Package and source | Bind | Key settings |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Grafana OSS 13.2.3 | `grafana`, apt.grafana.com (source already at `infra/ansible/roles/access/tasks/main.yml:5-12`) | 127.0.0.1:3000 | SQLite; `root_url` set; state history `backend = loki`; start gated on store readiness |
| Prometheus 2.53.3 | `prometheus`, Debian main, no recommends | 127.0.0.1:9090 | remote_write receiver; no scrape jobs; 400 d retention; 8 h out-of-order window |
| Loki 3.7.8 | `loki`, apt.grafana.com | 127.0.0.1:3100 | single binary, filesystem, tsdb schema v13; compactor retention on |
| Caddy | cloudsmith repo, as on the bridgehead | 443 public | basic auth on the two ingest paths with separate `fleet` and `logship` users; UI and API behind mTLS (decision D5) |
| Alloy | `alloy`, apt.grafana.com, zaccess pattern | loopback | node metrics, the three services' `/metrics`, their journals |
| Self-check timer | role-written | — | pings healthchecks.io only when rules are fresh, ingest is fresh and Loki is ready |

- **Datasource uids are reused**: `grafanacloud-prom` and `grafanacloud-logs`, so about 415 dashboard literals and six test asserts do not move.
- **Not installed**: PostgreSQL, Mimir, Alertmanager, vmalert, the image renderer.
- **Package trap**: Debian's own `loki` is unrelated software ("MCMC linkage analysis"). The role asserts the candidate's origin before installing.
- **Sizing**: about 24 samples/s and 3–5 MB of logs a day put a year under 10 GB. Grafana's idle RSS was measured at 379 MB with all rules evaluating; the Prometheus and Loki figures are estimates to be read in the shadow run.
- **The node's own rules**: Alloy-dark, disk-low, reboot-pending, store-down, SQLite-locked and an ingest or certificate signal. Disk rules are per group (`alerts.yaml:998, 2321, 2767, 4872, 5302`) and the reboot rules name hosts literally (`alerts.yaml:2833`), both re-read today, so nothing is picked up implicitly. That is 4–6 new rules, each owing a runbook section.

Two repo mechanics the design must respect, both re-read today:

- **The drift assert.** Merging a `config.alloy` change arms a refusal on every host of that role until each is converged (`infra/ansible/roles/capture/tasks/main.yml:433-449`). The capture config is one file for `zcrypto-red` and `zcrypto`, so it must not merge inside the box.
- **Reload does not re-read the env file** (`infra/ansible/roles/capture/handlers/main.yml:8-18`). Env names and the container recreate go first, the config second. That is two tree states per role, or a workstation proof that a reload with unset names is harmless.

## 5. Semantic traps and how each is proven

No test reaches a live Grafana, so the suite stays green through a wrong migration. Every proof below is a read by value.

| Trap | Why it matters | Proof |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Range-selector boundary | Debian's 2.53.3 is a 2.x engine. Prometheus 3 made selectors left-open (https://prometheus.io/docs/prometheus/latest/migration/), and Cloud's engine version was not read. | One boundary-aligned `count_over_time(up[5m])` on both stacks, recorded in the spec. |
| Rules that rarely fire | Matching states for 7 days proves only rules that changed state. `delta[26h]` with `for: 27h`, `changes`, `resets` and `offset 24h` can pass unexercised. | The compare script diffs all 96 PromQL query nodes and the Alloy-shipped LogQL nodes on both stacks, after at least 26 h of data per host. |
| `up` present at 0 versus absent | `dark-with-exposure` (`alerts.yaml:419`) and the alloy-dark rules rest on it (`alerts.yaml:438-443`, `4601-4609`). | An attended Alloy-stop drill on `zcrypto-red` or ops, read on mon. It is never induced on the primary. |
| Ingest dark looks like fleet dark | A failed certificate or auth on mon makes `up` absent while `last_over_time[24h]` still holds the position, so the exposure rule fires. | A drill with only ops and zaccess shipping; a separate ingest signal in the page. |
| Store restart or boot | 104 rules page on error, and systemd `After=` does not wait for readiness. | Grafana waits on Prometheus `/-/ready` and Loki `/ready`; `prometheus` is held off unattended upgrades for this host until a restart drill shows no pages. |
| Log lines lost in a mon outage | `loki.write` has no WAL and drops after about 10 retries. A lost nightly "pull complete" line can fire the 26 h archive-pull dead-men for a day. | A post-outage check in the mon-dark runbook; measured in the power-off drill. |
| Alert history read | The default annotations backend refuses a cross-rule read (measured: HTTP 500, "ruleUID is required"). | Run the daily report's alert read against mon with `backend = loki`. |
| Stack-only settings | The 60 s interval and policy timings are not in the repo. | Read back the 9 rule groups; diff `/api/v1/provisioning/policies` between the stacks. |
| Mixed-path log rule | `zcrypto-ops-error-logs` selects the direct-shipped `liquidations` stream as well as Alloy streams, so on mon it is blind to poller errors until cutover. | It goes on the exclude list with the six direct-ship rules. |
| Slack links | Without `root_url` they point at localhost. | One real notification end to end. |

## 7. What gets worse

- **One more host, with no HA.** A dead node is the owner's outage to repair, at night if need be. After retirement, "engine dark and position open" is unevaluated until it is back, exactly as in a Cloud outage today.
- **Hand patching.** Grafana, Loki, Alloy and Caddy sit outside the Debian-security origin (`infra/ansible/roles/base/templates/50unattended-upgrades.j2:3-5`). Grafana 13 marks the contact-point, policy and template endpoints the push script uses as deprecated, so a major upgrade is a reviewed change.
- **Standing hours.** The judges estimate 2–4 hours a month, plus an incident tail that is someone else's today.
- **Shared fate.** Evaluator and stores are on one box, and the monitor is a Linode like most of the fleet. healthchecks.io stays outside.
- **History dies with the host** unless backups are bought (D7).
- **Render verification is gone.** The renderer asks for 16 GiB (https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/setup-grafana/image-rendering/).
- **Two stacks for about seven weeks.** Double pushes, a temporary second endpoint in every Alloy config, and a relaxed guard to restore afterwards.
- **Misleading names.** The `grafanacloud-*` uids live on for things that are no longer Grafana Cloud.
- **Money.** $24/month where there was none.

## 9. Effort

- **Process**: spec and plan through `zcrypto-plan-review`, then four or five PRs, each with pre-review, review, at most two re-reviews and a change-index row.
- **Size**: the host and role are M, close to the bridgehead (PR #229: 58 files, 24 commits, 2 converges). Dual-ship is M, cutover is M, retirement is S to M.
- **Working days**: 12–18. The first design said 6–9; its judge re-estimated against the cache precedent (PR #607 plus follow-ups: 105 commits, 19 converges).
- **Attended converges**: about 30. That is 3–5 on the new host, about 14 for dual-ship, 4–5 for the handover and 8 for retirement.
- **Drills**: store restart, 30-minute power-off, rebuild from nothing, and Alloy-stop on the secondary, each with a drill-log entry.
- **The owner's hand steps**: the Linode, the Cloud Firewall, one A record (IPv4 only, since the 09-24 fault was IPv6), the deploy keypair, vault values, a shadow Slack webhook and one healthchecks.io check.
- **Calendar**: paper in the week of 10-05, the node up in the week of 10-12, the cutover from 11-02, retirement around 11-24.
